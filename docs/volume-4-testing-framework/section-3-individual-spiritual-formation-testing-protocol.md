# Section 3: Individual Spiritual Formation Testing Protocol

## Starting with the Person in the Room
Every research program about spiritual formation has to make a choice about its unit of analysis. You can study churches, denominations, small groups, or individuals. The LotS project starts with the individual, not because community does not matter (it matters enormously, and Section 4 addresses small group testing directly), but because the heart that is being formed is a single heart. The Holy Spirit convicts one person at a time. The word of Christ lands in one person's hearing at a time. The Affective Taxonomy traces a journey that one person makes, stage by stage, even when they make it in the company of others.

The individual testing protocol is also where the LotS project stays most honest about what it can and cannot claim. A person's spiritual formation is complex, non-linear, and influenced by factors that no research design can fully account for: their family of origin, their prior experience of church, the quality of their current relationships, the trials they are walking through, the particular voice and character of the Spirit's work in their specific life. The protocol proposed here does not pretend to control for all of those variables. It is designed to observe the journey closely enough, over a long enough period of time, that patterns can emerge, and to capture both the patterns and the texture of what the journey actually feels like from the inside.

## 3a. Defining the Testing Unit
The individual testing unit for the LotS spiritual formation protocol is a person who meets four criteria. First, they are a self-identified Christian who takes the spiritual life seriously, not necessarily a mature or advanced disciple, but someone who genuinely wants to grow. The testing protocol is not designed for people who are indifferent to formation; it is designed to observe and support formation in people who have at least a Stage 1 or Stage 2 relationship to the journey (Receiving or Responding on the Affective Taxonomy). Second, they are willing to participate in a PROAPT small group meeting weekly for a minimum of twelve months. Third, they are willing to complete regular self-assessments and to allow their progress to be observed and documented. And fourth, they are willing to identify an external witness, a family member, close friend, or coworker who knows them well, who can be interviewed at the beginning and end of the study period.

The twelve-month minimum is important and deliberate. Stage transitions on the Affective Taxonomy are not quick events, they are more like tectonic shifts than lightning strikes. The research at Willow Creek (Hawkins & Parkinson, 2011) found that movement between spiritual growth stages typically takes years, not months, in the absence of structured formation support. The hypothesis of the LotS testing program is that the PROAPT model, applied consistently in a supportive community, can accelerate that movement, but "accelerated" in this context still means observable change over twelve months, not overnight transformation. The protocol is designed to be long enough to capture real movement while remaining feasible for church-based research.

## 3b. Assessing the Starting State
Before the formation process begins, each participant completes a baseline assessment designed to establish their starting position on three dimensions, each measured by one of the three Pilot Ready core instruments described in §3d below. (The earlier full-suite protocol assessed four baseline dimensions; the Scripture Trust and Spiritual Disciplines Frequency dimensions are now optional research-grade extensions documented in the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md). Studies wanting deeper baseline instrumentation should consult the appendix for the STI-A and SDFL descriptions.) These three dimensions are not independent of each other, growth in one tends to support growth in the others, but each captures something distinct, and tracking them separately over time allows the research to detect which aspects of formation are progressing, which are lagging, and where the next growth edge lies.

**Dimension 1: Affective Taxonomy Integration Profile (baseline SIP)**

The baseline AT integration profile is established by the participant's first administration of the Stage Integration Profile (SIP-Individual, described as Instrument 1 in §3d below). Unlike the earlier PSFA-based baseline (which classified the participant at their highest consistent stage and is preserved as a research-grade extension in the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md)), the SIP produces a profile across all five AT stages with the integration-vs-closure check built in. The facilitator observation component adds an outside perspective: the group facilitator, using the Group Affective Taxonomy Assessment described in Section 4, documents their initial impression of each participant's stage based on their first few sessions of observation, and notes any discrepancy between the SIP profile and the facilitator's reading as a starting-point conversation.

Determining a person's starting profile is an interpretive act, not a precise measurement. Most participants will show points distributed across multiple adjacent stages; the asymmetry of the distribution is itself the information the SIP provides. The practice in the LotS protocol is to read the SIP profile for both the highest-weighted stage (the analog of the PSFA's stage classification) AND the integration pattern (whether earlier-stage scores are sustained or starved). Movement over the twelve months is then defined not as movement from one stage to the next but as the shift in the profile shape — with the integration check confirming that any rise in higher-stage scores is paired with sustained earlier-stage openness.

!!! info "Affective Taxonomy Stages — Quick Reference for Assessors"

    - **Stage 1 — Receiving.** Awareness, willingness to attend. Shows up to the group. Listens without consistent engagement.
    - **Stage 2 — Responding.** Beginning intellectual and emotional reaction. Answers factual questions, asks follow-ups, and engages with the discussion. Scripture is interesting.
    - **Stage 3 — Valuing.** Beginning to live according to biblical teachings. Observable behavior changes. Personal prayer requests, repentance in the group. Scripture costs something.
    - **Stage 4 — Organization.** Organizing life decisions around biblical principles. Consistent patterns visible to outsiders. Scripture shapes the whole structure of daily life.
    - **Stage 5 — Characterization.** Barna's (2003) biblical worldview is fully evident. Character, not just behavior, is shaped by Christ. Pain is accepted as part of submission to God. Moving from hearing about God to seeing Him (Job 42:5).

    **Read the stages as integration, not replacement.** These stages name kinds of engagement that become accessible in this order, not states a person passes through and leaves behind. A participant whose SIP profile shows high Organization and Characterization scores but a starved Receiving score is not at Stage 4; the higher-stage weighting is masking a collapse. Assess for the profile shape *as a whole*. See [Vol 4 §2: Reading the Affective Taxonomy as Integration, Not Replacement](section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement).

**Dimension 2: Image of God Reflection (baseline NRP-IoG prompt)**

The baseline Image of God profile is established as the first entry of a recurring NRP prompt set asking the participant to rate their current experience of each of Kilner's (2015) four dimensions of reflection: Reason (the mind brought into alignment with God's truth), Rulership (responsible stewardship of their sphere of influence), Relationship (depth of connection with God and others), and Righteousness (habitual alignment of character with God's character). In the earlier full-suite protocol this was a standalone Image of God 4Rs Self-Assessment instrument; under the Pilot Ready core the 4Rs are folded into the NRP (Instrument 2 in §3d below) as a recurring prompt set rather than carried as a separate instrument. The qualitative register of the NRP is well-suited to 4Rs reflection — Kilner's dimensions are easier to describe in narrative than to score numerically, and the monthly NRP rhythm catches movement that a once-or-twice-yearly assessment would miss.

The baseline profile serves two purposes. First, it gives the participant and facilitator a common language for discussing formation goals: rather than the vague aspiration to "grow spiritually," the 4Rs framework invites a more specific conversation about which dimension is most alive in this person right now and which is most dormant. Second, it provides an endpoint comparison at twelve months: has the profile shifted? Which R has shown the most growth? Does the pattern of growth map onto what the SIP profile would predict?

**Dimension 3: External Witness Baseline (start-of-study EWI)**

The baseline External Witness Interview establishes the external triangulation that protects the Pilot Ready core against self-report inflation in either the SIP or the NRP. The participant identifies a family member, close friend, or coworker who knows them well; this external witness is interviewed at the beginning of the study, using the same EWI protocol that will be repeated at Month 12. The baseline EWI documents what this person currently observes in the participant — patterns of behavior, character, the consistency between the participant's stated beliefs and how they actually live in unguarded moments. The endpoint EWI in Month 12 produces the comparison; movement on the EWI is the most honest formation indicator in the protocol, because it comes from someone who has no stake in the research program and no reason to be generous in their assessment.

The EWI is described in detail as Instrument 3 in §3d below.

## 3c. The Primary Outcome Variables
The testing protocol tracks three primary outcome variables over the twelve-month period, each measured by one of the three Pilot Ready core instruments described in §3d below. (The earlier full-suite protocol tracked five outcome variables, including separate Scripture Trust and PSFA-based Stage Progression variables; those are now optional research-grade extensions documented in the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md). Studies wanting the additional outcomes should consult the appendix.) Together the three Pilot Ready outcome variables provide a multi-dimensional picture of individual spiritual formation progress with the integration-vs-closure check built into the primary measure.

**Outcome Variable 1: Affective Taxonomy Integration Profile Trajectory**

The primary outcome variable is the change over twelve months in the participant's Stage Integration Profile (SIP-Individual, described as Instrument 1 in §3d below). OV1 tracks two things simultaneously: (1) the shift in the participant's whole-person SIP profile across the five AT stages from baseline to endpoint, and (2) the integration-vs-closure status of that profile at each assessment point. A meaningful positive trajectory is upward shift in the higher-stage scores (D and E) **paired with sustained or rising** earlier-stage scores (A and B); a profile in which D and E rise while A drops below the 10-point closure threshold is recorded as a closure trajectory, not a progression trajectory.

OV1 is the operational form of the [§2 integration reading](section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement) as a measurable outcome. The SIP profile is the integration check itself, scored quantitatively; the SIP's closure-flag detects the divergence between surface stage and underlying integration directly. Not every participant will show dramatic profile movement in twelve months, particularly participants who begin at higher-stage weightings, where deeper integration is slower and more costly. But every participant should show some movement — either a profile shift, a deepening within their current weighting, or the emergence of new contributions from a previously starved stage. The earlier full-suite protocol's stage-transition framing — movement from one stage to the next — is now read as one specific pattern within the broader profile-trajectory measure.

Triangulation against external observation remains important. A SIP profile trajectory is corroborated when (a) the participant's monthly NRP narratives reflect the same direction of formation, and (b) the External Witness Interview at Month 12 confirms the change is visible to people outside the formation group. Where these three converge, the profile trajectory is read as genuine formation; where they diverge, the divergence is itself the research flag that invites qualitative investigation.

The following table names the behavioral indicators most associated with the emergence of each stage's contribution to the integration profile. Reading the indicators as *additions* to the existing profile (not replacements of earlier behaviors) is the integration-framing application of the table.

| Stage Contribution Becoming Active | Primary Behavioral Indicators |
| --- | --- |
| Stage 1 — Receiving | Genuine, ongoing openness to new words; shows up to the formation work; listens without immediately resolving what is being said. |
| Stage 2 — Responding | Active engagement with scripture and discussion; asks questions; shows emotional response; begins applying observations to their own life. |
| Stage 3 — Valuing | Observable behavior changes outside the group; personal conviction and repentance; speaks of scripture as personally authoritative; willing to repent publicly. |
| Stage 4 — Organization | Life-structuring decisions made on biblical principles, visible to external witnesses; consistent discipline patterns; testimony of scripture shaping major choices (relationships, finances, vocation); others seek their wisdom. |
| Stage 5 — Characterization | Character matches behavior — not just doing the right things but being the right person; response to suffering reflects Job 2:9 / 42:5 movement; uninstructed acts of love and sacrifice; no gap between public and private life as reported by external witness. |

**Outcome Variable 2: Image of God 4Rs Profile Shift (via NRP-IoG prompt)**

The second outcome variable is the change in the participant's Image of God 4Rs profile from baseline to endpoint, measured via the recurring NRP-IoG prompt set described in Instrument 2 (NRP) in §3d below. The earlier full-suite protocol used a standalone Image of God 4Rs Self-Assessment; under the Pilot Ready core the 4Rs are folded into the NRP as a recurring monthly prompt set, with the baseline and endpoint entries providing the comparison points.

A meaningful shift is identified by qualitative coding of the NRP-IoG narrative entries from baseline to endpoint, looking for new specificity, new growth, or new awareness in any of Kilner's four dimensions (Reason, Rulership, Relationship, Righteousness). Because the 4Rs are an endpoint description (the shape of the reflection of Christ), they are expected to change more slowly than the AT integration profile. A participant whose SIP profile shows Stage 2-to-3 movement may not yet show strong growth in all four Rs; a participant whose profile is integrating at Stage 4 or 5 should show robust growth in at least two Rs, and the specific Rs showing growth should map predictably onto their PROAPT experience.

**Outcome Variable 3: Belief-Behavior Integration as Observed Externally (via EWI)**

The third outcome variable is the change between the baseline EWI (conducted at the start of the study) and the endpoint EWI (conducted in Month 12), measuring the consistency the external witness observes between what the participant says they believe and how they actually live. One of the most reliable indicators of Stage 3 formation is the narrowing of the gap between belief and behavior: a person at Stage 2 can articulate biblical principles with precision while their life is organized around entirely different values; a person at Stage 3 is beginning to feel the cost of that gap (which is why Stage 3 is the stage of personal conviction, repentance, and the first costly behavioral changes); a person at Stage 4 has substantially closed the gap, with their life and beliefs organized around the same center.

The EWI question is not asked abstractly; it is grounded in specific observable behaviors. How does the participant handle conflict? How do they talk about money? How do they treat people who cannot help them? What do they do when no one is watching? External witnesses are often the most honest assessors of the belief-behavior gap, because they see the participant in the unguarded moments of daily life that the research group never observes. The external witness is not told what the participant's SIP profile shows; the convergence or divergence between the EWI report and the SIP self-assessment is one of the most interesting data points in the entire protocol — and the most telling indicator of whether genuine Stage 4 integration has occurred.

## 3d. The Three Assessment Instruments
The Pilot Ready individual testing protocol uses three instruments, each targeting a different dimension of the formation journey: a quantitative profile (SIP), qualitative texture (NRP, with Image of God 4Rs folded in as a recurring prompt set), and external triangulation (EWI). What follows here is a description of their purpose, their relationship to the LotS framework, and the principles guiding their use. *The earlier full-suite protocol used five individual instruments; the PSFA, STI-A, and SDFL are now research-grade extensions documented in the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md), along with five group-level extension instruments (GSFP, GCS, CSFA, SOP, Facilitator PSFA) and one situational extension (OT-5 Supplementary Interview). Studies wanting deeper instrumentation should consult the appendix for the full canonical descriptions and the "when to add this instrument" guidance for each. The instruments summary table is in [§5c](section-5-research-program-design-for-spiritual.md#5c-instruments-summary).*

**Instrument 1: Stage Integration Profile (SIP-Individual)**

The SIP is the operational form of the [§2 integration reading](section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement). Where the PSFA produces a single highest-consistent-stage classification, the SIP produces a *profile* across the five AT stages, with a built-in closure check that surfaces the Pharisee-as-stage-N-collapse pattern directly rather than requiring qualitative inference from PSFA-STI-NRP convergence. The SIP adapts the **structural pattern** of Cameron & Quinn's Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI) in *Diagnosing and Changing Organizational Culture* — forced-choice 100-point distribution across multiple value options, organized along multiple observation dimensions, scored twice (current and preferred) — applied to the AT's five stages across six personal formation contexts. The specific OCAI items, dimension wording, and CVF quadrant content are copyrighted; only the structural pattern is in the public domain and adapted here.

**Six dimensions of personal spiritual engagement.** Each names a context in which the five kinds of AT engagement show up. For each dimension, five stage-anchored options describe what that engagement looks like at each stage. The respondent distributes 100 points across the five options based on how alive each is *right now*. Then repeats for *preferred* (what the respondent thinks should be the case in one year).

- **Dimension 1: My engagement with scripture.** How does scripture land when I encounter it — a new passage, or a familiar one I'm reading again today?
  - (Receiving) I show up to scripture willing to hear it but without much engagement of my own yet. I read or listen; the passage doesn't yet reach me, but I'm here.
  - (Responding) I engage scripture with curiosity. I ask questions of the text, notice what's interesting, follow up on what catches my attention.
  - (Valuing) I find myself applying what I read to my own life as I read. The passage costs me something — a recognition, a conviction, a behavioral shift I begin to plan.
  - (Organization) Scripture is part of how I structure my life. The passages I've engaged with feed into how I make decisions across multiple domains, not just the immediate one.
  - (Characterization) Scripture has shaped who I am to the point that I no longer notice the integration. Others would describe me primarily in terms of how I live what I have read.
- **Dimension 2: My response to challenge or correction.** When someone disagrees with me on a matter of faith, shows me something I missed, or points out a pattern in me I hadn't seen — what happens in me?
  - (Receiving) I notice myself willing to listen. The challenge reaches me, even if I don't know what to do with it yet.
  - (Responding) I engage actively — ask follow-up questions, try to understand the other person's reasoning, look at the evidence they're pointing to.
  - (Valuing) I find myself willing to be wrong. If the challenge holds up, I let it cost me — apologize, change a behavior, revise a position.
  - (Organization) I treat correction as part of the ongoing formation work. It feeds back into how I make future decisions in adjacent areas, not just the immediate one.
  - (Characterization) Receptivity to correction is part of who I am. Others would describe me as someone who can be told the truth.
- **Dimension 3: How scripture shapes my decisions.** When a real decision intersects with what I've been reading or hearing — at work, with money, in relationships, with how I spend my time — what role does scripture actually play?
  - (Receiving) I'm aware that scripture has something to say in this area, even if I don't yet know what.
  - (Responding) I look for what scripture says about decisions like this one. I read, ask, study.
  - (Valuing) I let what I find from scripture cost me. I make the decision the way the passage actually points to, even when it's hard.
  - (Organization) Decisions across multiple life domains consistently reflect what scripture has formed in me. I don't think about it; the formation is already in the way I weigh options.
  - (Characterization) The way I make decisions has become a defining mark. People who know me would say my decisions look like a person whose life is governed by what God has said.
- **Dimension 4: My presence in community.** In the small group room, in church, with other believers — what kind of presence do I bring?
  - (Receiving) I show up. I am there. I listen to what others say.
  - (Responding) I participate. I share what I'm thinking, ask genuine questions, build on what others bring.
  - (Valuing) I take real personal risks in community — share a conviction, name a hard thing about myself, let the group see what is actually happening in me.
  - (Organization) I am consistently the kind of person in community whom others can rely on for honest engagement. The character of my presence holds across sessions.
  - (Characterization) My presence has shaped what is normal in the community I am part of. Others have grown into deeper community partly because of how I have been with them.
- **Dimension 5: My response to suffering or unexpected difficulty.** When something hard happens — loss, conflict, illness, betrayal, public failure, the slow grind of an unresolved situation — what happens in my engagement with God?
  - (Receiving) I stay in the conversation with God through it. I do not close.
  - (Responding) I bring the suffering actively into prayer and scripture. I ask questions; I look at what is happening.
  - (Valuing) I let the suffering do its formation work. I make the costly choices the situation requires, including the spiritual ones.
  - (Organization) I draw on what previous suffering has formed in me. The integration of past trials feeds into how I navigate this one.
  - (Characterization) Suffering does not change who I am with God; it deepens it. Others see in me what Brueggemann calls reorientation, what Job describes as moving from hearing about God to seeing Him.
- **Dimension 6: The Tell — what I do with what I have received.** After I have heard from God or from scripture, what crosses the threshold from interior reception to spoken word and outward action?
  - (Receiving) I notice what I have received. I hold it.
  - (Responding) I speak about what I have received in safe contexts — to spouse, friend, small group.
  - (Valuing) I tell people who specifically need to hear it, including people outside my close circle, including when it costs me something to say.
  - (Organization) Telling is part of how I live. The Romans 10 chain — what I have received becomes word that becomes formation in others — runs naturally through my daily life.
  - (Characterization) My life carries the word outward as a defining mark. The Tell is not a discipline I practice but the shape of who I am.

**Scoring.** Each dimension produces a current profile (five numbers summing to 100) and a preferred profile (five numbers summing to 100). Aggregating across the six dimensions produces a whole-person current profile, a whole-person preferred profile, and a per-stage current-to-preferred gap vector. Visualization: a five-point radar chart with current and preferred overlaid; the kite shape tells the story at a glance.

**Closure detection — the integration check operationalized.** The Pharisee-as-stage-N-collapse pattern produces a specific signature on the SIP: a profile dominated by Organization (D) and Characterization (E) with Receiving (A) starved. The simplest assessor rule: **any stage scoring below 10 points on a dimension is flagged as collapse-suspect.** Multiple low-A dimensions in the same profile constitute the closure signature; the assessor reads the flagged dimensions back to the participant as the starting point for the integration conversation — *"where has the kind of openness Stage 1 names gone quiet in you?"* This is the single operational move the SIP makes that a single-stage-classification instrument like the PSFA (now an Appendix extension) cannot.

The SIP is administered at baseline, six months, and twelve months. Time-on-task is approximately fifteen to twenty minutes per administration. The forced-choice distribution is the format that does the integration work; a Likert fallback would lose the constraint that makes the closure pattern visible.

**Instrument 2: Narrative Reflection Protocol (NRP)**

The NRP is the qualitative heart of the individual testing protocol. Each month, the participant responds to structured reflection prompts in a private journal format. The prompts are designed to surface the specific experiences and encounters that the LotS framework predicts will drive formation: encounters with the word, experiences of testing, moments of heart change, and evidence of Image of God growth.

The five monthly prompts in the Pilot Ready core NRP are:

1. *Describe a specific moment this month when the word of Christ landed in a new way — when you heard it differently than you had before. What were the circumstances? What changed?*
2. *Describe a trial or difficulty you encountered this month. In retrospect, how did it affect your trust in God and your engagement with scripture?*
3. *Describe one specific way your heart changed this month — a shift in what you value, what you desire, what you fear, or what you love.*
4. *Image of God — 4Rs reflection. Identify one or more of Kilner's four dimensions of reflection (Reason, Rulership, Relationship, Righteousness) where you noticed growth or stagnation this month. What made it visible to you?* (This is the prompt set that replaces the standalone Image of God 4Rs Self-Assessment used in the earlier full-suite protocol; see §3b Dimension 2 and §3c Outcome Variable 2 for the operational use, and the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md) for the standalone instrument as a research-grade extension.)
5. *SIP closure-flag follow-up (only if the assessor's most recent SIP review flagged a closure-suspect dimension). Where has the kind of openness that earlier-stage Receiving names gone quiet in you in the specific area the SIP flagged? What would re-opening it look like?* This prompt is included only when triggered by a SIP closure-flag from the most recent assessment; it is the qualitative companion to the integration check and is what makes the SIP's closure signal generative rather than just diagnostic.

The NRP entries are not shared with the research team in real time — they are collected at the end of the twelve-month period, with the participant's permission, for qualitative coding. This protects the formation process from becoming a performance: the participant writes for themselves and for God, not for the researcher. At the twelve-month endpoint, the participant reviews their own NRP entries before their final interview, identifying the moments they consider most significant. This review process is itself a formation exercise — a version of the Ignatian Examen applied to a year of documented spiritual journey.

**Instrument 3: External Witness Interview (EWI)**

The EWI is the external triangulation that protects the Pilot Ready core against self-report inflation in either the SIP or the NRP. The participant identifies a family member, close friend, or coworker who knows them well and who can be interviewed at the beginning and end of the study period. The external witness is asked to describe what they observe in the participant: patterns of behavior, character, the consistency between the participant's stated beliefs and how they actually live in unguarded moments. The EWI questions are not asked abstractly; they are grounded in specific observable behaviors. How does the participant handle conflict? How do they talk about money? How do they treat people who cannot help them? What do they do when no one is watching?

The EWI is conducted twice: at the start of the study (the baseline EWI in Month 1 establishes the comparison point) and at the endpoint (the Month 12 EWI provides the measure of change). The external witness is never told what the participant's SIP profile or NRP entries show; the comparison of EWI report against participant self-report is one of the most interesting data points in the protocol — convergence is corroboration; divergence is the most telling indicator of whether genuine integration has occurred.

The EWI is semi-structured: a fifteen-to-twenty-minute conversation following a question protocol, with the research coordinator (not the formation facilitator) conducting the interview to protect against any role conflict. The EWI is the single highest-trust-required moment in the protocol — the participant must consent to having someone close to them speak honestly about what they observe — and is part of what makes the protocol Pilot Ready in a sense the earlier full-suite design was not: the EWI is the irreducible external check that no quantitative self-report can replace.

## 3e. The Testing Timeline
The twelve-month individual testing protocol is organized into three phases. The phases are not arbitrary divisions; they correspond to the characteristic formation dynamics of the early, middle, and later stages of the Affective Taxonomy journey.

**Phase 1 — Orientation and Baseline (Months 1–3)**

The first three months are foundational. The participant completes the Pilot Ready baseline assessment — the baseline SIP and the first NRP entry (which includes the baseline Image of God 4Rs reflection as Prompt 4) — in the first week, and the research coordinator conducts the baseline EWI with the participant's identified external witness within the first month. They attend their first PROAPT group sessions and begin to find their footing in the structured bible study rhythm. The facilitator pays close attention during this phase to where each participant is starting: which participants' SIP profiles weight heavily toward Stage 1 (attending, listening, orienting), which toward Stage 2 (engaging intellectually, asking questions), and which already show Stage 3 contributions (applying the word at personal cost).

The NRP begins in Month 1. Many participants will find the early prompts difficult — not because the questions are hard, but because they have never been asked to document their spiritual experience in this specific way. The facilitator should offer encouragement and normalize the awkwardness: the first few months of NRP entries are expected to be the thinnest and most generic, and that itself is data about where the participant is starting.

Phase 1 is also the phase most vulnerable to dropout. Participants who are at Stage 1 — attending out of curiosity or social obligation — may find the commitment level of weekly PROAPT plus monthly NRP higher than they expected. The research protocol should include a check-in conversation at the end of Month 3, giving every participant the opportunity to ask questions, voice concerns, and either recommit or gracefully withdraw before the active formation phase begins.

**Phase 2 — Active Formation (Months 4–9)**

The middle six months are where the work happens. The PROAPT group is now past the initial awkwardness and into a rhythm of genuine engagement with scripture. The NRP entries should begin to show more specificity — particular passages that struck hard, particular trials that shook something loose, particular moments of unexpected heart change, and growing depth on the recurring Image of God 4Rs prompt.

One formal assessment point falls within Phase 2. At Month 6, the SIP is administered again — not announced as a major assessment event but built into the group's normal rhythm as a natural midpoint check-in. The facilitator reviews each participant's Month 6 SIP profile against their baseline and uses the results to inform their facilitation approach for the second half of the study: participants whose profile shows clear integrated movement need challenge and deepening; participants whose profile is stuck or shows a closure-flag pattern need gentle investigation of what may be blocking movement or what earlier-stage openness has gone quiet.

The most important formation events of the entire protocol typically occur during Phase 2. The LotS framework predicts, drawing from James 1:2–3 and from the MOVE study's finding about trials as transition catalysts, that the Stage 2–to–3 contribution — the activation of personal commitment alongside earlier-stage receiving — is almost always associated with a specific challenge or cost. Something happens in Phase 2: a relationship strained, a financial decision made on biblical principle at personal cost, a conviction that requires a public acknowledgment or apology. The NRP is designed to catch these moments.

**Phase 3 — Integration and Assessment (Months 10–12)**

The final three months shift from primarily formative to also evaluative. The participant's NRP entries during this phase should have a different quality than the earlier ones — more reflective, more integrated, more aware of the arc of the year.

At Month 12, the endpoint SIP is administered and the participant reviews their own twelve months of NRP entries before their endpoint interview, identifying the three moments they consider most significant in their formation during the year. The endpoint interview with the research coordinator is semi-structured, following a protocol that allows the participant to tell the story of their year in their own terms before the researcher asks specific questions about the SIP profile trajectory, the Image of God 4Rs shift, and the integration-vs-closure pattern.

The endpoint External Witness Interview is conducted in Month 12, independent of the participant's own assessment. The external witness is not told what the participant's SIP profile shows; they are asked only to describe what they have observed in the participant over the past year. The convergence or divergence between the external witness's observations and the participant's self-report is one of the most interesting data points in the entire protocol — and one of the most telling indicators of whether genuine integration has occurred.

## 3f. The Claims This Protocol Tests
The individual testing protocol is designed primarily to test four of the LotS claims identified across Volumes 1–3:

**LotS-H3** — Heart formation is progressive and follows the five stages of the Affective Taxonomy. The protocol tests this by tracking stage-characteristic behaviors over twelve months in a structured formation context. If the claim holds, PROAPT participants should show directional stage progression that is not observed in comparable non-PROAPT participants. Per [§2: Reading the Affective Taxonomy as Integration, Not Replacement](section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement), "progressive" here names the order in which the kinds of engagement become accessible, not the replacement of earlier stages by later ones; the claim is operationally confirmed when later-stage behaviors emerge *while* earlier-stage openness remains active.

**LotS-H4** — Reflective bible study (PROAPT model) is the primary discipline that advances heart formation at every stage. The protocol tests this through the NRP narratives, looking for the association between PROAPT engagement and formation movement predicted by the MOVE study (Hawkins & Parkinson, 2011). Studies wanting the additional quantitative discipline-frequency data should add the optional SDFL from the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md).

**LotS-F2** — Faith is primarily affective, not cognitive. The protocol tests this by examining whether SIP profile movement is associated with changes in the affect/will/desire-related dimensions of the SIP (Dimensions 1, 5, 6) before changes in the more cognitive dimensions (Dimensions 2, 3), as the LotS framework predicts.

**LotS-I2** — Spiritual formation is growth in reflection of the image of God, measurable through the 4Rs. The protocol tests this through the NRP-IoG prompt set at baseline and twelve months, looking for profile shifts that correspond to SIP integration trajectory and to the specific scriptural content engaged through PROAPT.

Secondary claims also touched by this protocol include LotS-F3 (trials as formation mechanism — captured in Phase 2 NRP entries), LotS-H1 (heart as the affective center through which all formation passes — tested by the correlation between SIP profile movement in the affect-related dimensions and overall integration trajectory), and LotS-W1 (wisdom development activated by affective desire — tested by the emergence of discernment language in later NRP entries; studies wanting the optional dedicated trust measurement may add the STI-A from the [Vol 4 Appendix](appendix-research-extensions.md)).

> Discussion Questions for Small Groups

**Section 3 Discussion Questions**

**1. **The protocol requires a twelve-month commitment to weekly PROAPT and monthly narrative reflection. What would need to be true about a small group or church community for that kind of commitment to be sustainable? What has prevented that level of commitment in your own experience?

**2. **The Pilot Ready protocol identifies three baseline dimensions: the AT integration profile (via SIP), the Image of God 4Rs reflection (via NRP), and the external witness baseline (via EWI). If you were to assess your own baseline honestly today, where would you place yourself on each? Which dimension feels most alive, and which most stagnant?

**3. **The External Witness Interview asks someone who knows the participant well to describe what they have observed in them, without knowing the participant's self-report scores. Who in your life would you choose as your external witness? What do you think they would say about the consistency between your stated beliefs and your actual life?

**4. **The protocol predicts that the most significant formation events will occur during the active middle phase — and that they will typically involve a specific trial or cost. Looking back at your own spiritual formation, when was the last time a specific challenge accelerated your growth? What happened? What did it cost?

**5. **The Narrative Reflection Protocol asks four monthly questions: a moment of hearing the word freshly, the impact of a trial, a specific heart change, and evidence of Image of God growth. Try answering all four questions for the current month. What does that exercise reveal about where your formation is most alive right now?
