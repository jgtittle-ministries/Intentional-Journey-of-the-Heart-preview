# Founders’ Genesis: How This Work Came Together

## **The starting point**
I came to the Lord on June 14th, 1982. Upon reflection, I can see his footprints drawing me step by step closer to Him. Carolyn had always been a believer, but I needed something more to accept that he was real and cared for me. We quickly started attending Truro Church in Fairfax, VA, and a small-group Bible study immediately after that. The extraordinary teaching of biblical basics by Rector John Howe and the small-group dynamics of the Bible study developed our two hungry hearts and filled us. Small-group Bible study was central to our continued development as the US Air Force moved us around the country. We were involved with a wide range of churches and denominations, in each case looking for inspiring worship services, Bible-based deep teaching, and close fellowship in a small group.

These three criteria have served us well as the Lord drew us deeper into Him. I have participated in small-group work ever since, including at some high-impact secular venues. I remember returning from an early weekend workshop and telling my pastor that the church desperately needed these tools for doing heart work and that Satan doesn’t get to keep them. It wasn’t hard to sort through what I could ground scripturally and discard the ungrounded metaphysics behind some of these tools.

John Eldredge’s “Wild at Heart” was the first time I found Christian process work and scriptural discussion that helped deal with what I have come to call heart work. His work gave me the Band of Brothers language that carried over into all my men’s small-group work. You will see this conversion/translation discussion in what follows. The small group impact and focus continue to this day and are reflected in all that follows.

This work was never solo, even though I was writing lots of journal entries and trying to organize things so they made sense. I’ve written these documents in my personal voice as my personal explorations, but each and every one was hammered out and experienced as group work. I’ve tried to distill and organize, as you will see in the following volumes, but I have undoubtedly missed many items that I hope will be added later. More than that, I hope that the community will now pitch in.

The BoB became the seed of what is now called the Fellowship of the Heart (FotH), the broader community of practice that has carried the work forward over the years. The shift from men’s work to mixed-gender work has challenged and expanded my understanding and practice of heart work. It might surprise you to learn that women often process heart work differently from men, in my experience. This, at least, has been a surprise to me, who grew up with three sisters, been married twice, raised a daughter, and helped with two granddaughters.

I was finally nudged to get this written down in a readable structure from a mass of notes and journal entries extending back to 1982. I realized that my personal runway was getting shorter, and I needed to do something to make available to my kids and grandkids what I have explored. A draft was finally at an acceptable starting point, and I realized it might have broader interest than just my family, so the move to an open-source project with community participation.

This work is offered as one stream of testimony in what I hope to become a much broader conversation, not as private discovery to be received passively, but as a community’s working out of what the Lord has shown over years of practice, offered to others walking similar territory.
