# Foundational Law XXXIV: The Marriage Covenant Architecture Law

The thirty-fourth foundational law operates at the dyadic-pair-bond scale (Period 2 / Group VI) and addresses an operational territory whose scriptural and traditional articulation is among the most canonically-developed in the entire corpus. The marriage covenant — instituted at creation, addressed by the Mosaic and prophetic literature, taught by Jesus with reference back to the creation institution, articulated by Paul with reference forward to Christ and the Church, and held with substantial uniformity across mainstream Christian tradition — operates as the architectural framework conditioning the operations within the marital dyad. The covenant is not merely an emotional bond, a social arrangement, or a contractual relationship that can be modified without operational consequence at the dyadic-architectural level; it is the architectural envelope within which the husband-wife relational dynamics, the one-flesh operational unity, the procreation-and-companionship operational purposes, the inter-generational household formation operations, and the Christ-and-Church reflective dimension all occur — and the framework conditions the operations.

The law is admitted at **Band 2** of the band-of-closeness clarification — the principle (marriage covenant operates as architectural framework) is admitted on its multi-author scriptural footing, with the specified pathway (the leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh pattern instituted at Gen. 2:24 and ratified by Jesus at Matt. 19:5) as part of the principle's scriptural articulation rather than as tradition-by-tradition theological determination. The Band 2 articulation is structurally distinct from FL.XXIII Sabbath Rest Law's and FL.XXXIII Community Polity Structure Law's Band 3 admissions: the marriage covenant's operational form (covenant-monogamy between man and woman) is held with substantially more uniformity across mainstream Christian tradition than the polity-form question, with the operational pathway scripturally specified rather than tradition-arbitrated.

The broader dyadic-pair-bond architectural territory beyond marriage — the close-friendship covenant (Jonathan-David type), the discipleship dyad (Paul-Timothy, Elijah-Elisha, Naomi-Ruth), the cross-generational-mentoring pair — operates by structurally analogous principles within distinct operational scopes. The present chapter articulates these related architectural forms within its dyadic-pair-bond-territory sub-section while admitting marriage as the canonically-articulated primary case at the Foundational tier.

## Scriptural Ground

The scriptural witness for the marriage covenant operates across more biblical authors than any other Foundational Law in the catalog, with operational depth that extends from the creation narrative through the apocalyptic vision of the marriage supper of the Lamb. The Genesis writer holds the institution at Gen. 2:18–25 — the canonical scriptural articulation of marriage's establishment at creation. The pericope reports the operational ground: "Then the LORD God said, 'It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him.'" (v. 18). The institution proceeds through the divine deliberation, the formation of the woman from the rib while the man sleeps (vv. 21–22), the man's recognition response ("This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man," v. 23), and the institutional declaration: "Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh" (v. 24). The closing observation "And the man and his wife were both naked and were not ashamed" (v. 25) articulates the pre-fall operational state of the marriage relationship.

The Gen. 2:24 institutional declaration is the canonical articulation of the marriage covenant's specified pathway — the leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh pattern. The three operations the declaration names (leaving father and mother; holding fast to the wife; becoming one flesh) are operationally precise: the leaving establishes the new household's structural independence from the parental households; the holding-fast establishes the covenant's structural durability through the operational adherence to the covenant partner; the one-flesh becoming establishes the operational unification at the bodily-relational-and-procreative level. The three operations together constitute the marriage covenant's operational pathway; the covenant is not merely a verbal commitment but an operational architecture established through these three operations.

Gen. 1:27–28 holds the parallel articulation at the creation account's broader level: "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created him. And God blessed them. And God said to them, 'Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdivide it.'" The Gen. 1 articulation establishes three operational features the marriage covenant operates within: the male-female creation as the image-of-God's structural completeness (with the human image of God operative in the male-and-female pair rather than in either alone); the divine blessing as the operational ground of the marriage relationship; the be-fruitful-and-multiply commission as the procreation operational purpose the covenant serves.

Malachi holds the canonical OT covenant articulation at Mal. 2:13–16: "And this second thing you do. You cover the LORD's altar with tears, with weeping and groaning because he no longer regards the offering or accepts it with favor from your hand. But you say, 'Why does he not?' Because the LORD was witness between you and the wife of your youth, to whom you have been faithless, though she is your companion and your wife by covenant. Did he not make them one, with a portion of the Spirit in their union? And what was the one God seeking? Godly offspring. So guard yourselves in your spirit, and let none of you be faithless to the wife of your youth. 'For the man who does not love his wife but divorces her,' says the LORD, the God of Israel, 'covers his garment with violence,' says the LORD of hosts. So guard yourselves in your spirit, and do not be faithless." The Malachi articulation establishes the marriage as covenant ("your wife by covenant"); names the LORD as the covenant's witness; articulates the covenant's purposes (the one-flesh union with a portion of the Spirit; godly offspring); and names the failure modes (faithlessness; covering one's garment with violence through divorce).

Jesus holds the canonical NT articulation at Matt. 19:3–9 (parallel at Mark 10:2–12). The Pharisees test Jesus on divorce: "And Pharisees came up to him and tested him by asking, 'Is it lawful to divorce one's wife for any cause?' He answered, 'Have you not read that he who created them from the beginning made them male and female, and said, "Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh"? So they are no longer two but one flesh. What therefore God has joined together, let not man separate.'" The Jesus articulation is operationally significant for several reasons. First, Jesus's reference back to the Gen. 1:27 and Gen. 2:24 institutional articulations names the creation-establishment as the canonical operational ground. Second, Jesus's "so they are no longer two but one flesh" articulates the covenant's operational reality — the marriage produces an operational unity that exceeds the sum of the two individuals. Third, Jesus's "what therefore God has joined together, let not man separate" names the covenant's structural durability through the divine-establishment character — the covenant's holding is grounded in God's joining-together work rather than in the human partners' continuing operational commitment alone. The Markan parallel at Mark 10:6–9 holds the same articulation with the additional pre-history context (Moses's Deuteronomic divorce provision as accommodation to hardness-of-heart at v. 5).

Paul holds the canonical Pauline articulation at Eph. 5:22–33 — the Christ-and-Church image. The pericope establishes the husband-wife operational dynamics through reference to the Christ-and-Church relationship: "Wives, submit to your own husbands, as to the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife even as Christ is the head of the church, his body, and is himself its Savior. Now as the church submits to Christ, so also wives should submit to their husbands in everything. Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word, so that he might present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing, that she might be holy and without blemish. In the same way husbands should love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself. For no one ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, just as Christ does the church, because we are members of his body. 'Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.' This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church." The Pauline articulation establishes three operational features the present law operates within. First, the husband-wife operational dynamics structurally parallel the Christ-and-Church relationship (the headship-and-submission pattern; the love-and-giving-of-self pattern; the sanctifying-and-cherishing pattern). Second, the Gen. 2:24 leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh declaration is cited by Paul as referring to "Christ and the church" at the deepest theological level — the marriage covenant has eschatological-and-ecclesiological reflective dimension beyond its dyadic-operational dimension. Third, the marriage covenant is named as "mystery" (μυστήριον) — a term that names the operational reality whose full significance is theologically-disclosed rather than merely-empirical.

The Hebrews writer holds the canonical articulation at Heb. 13:4: "Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterous." The Hebrews articulation names two operational features: the marriage's honor across the broader community ("among all") establishes the covenant's broader-community-recognition dimension; the marriage bed's undefiled character names the exclusivity of the sexual-relational operation within the covenant. The eschatological-judgment grounding establishes the covenant's eschatological-accountability character.

Peter holds the canonical articulation at 1 Pet. 3:1–7: "Likewise, wives, be subject to your own husbands, so that even if some do not obey the word, they may be won without a word by the conduct of their wives, when they see your respectful and pure conduct. Do not let your adorning be external — the braiding of hair and the putting on of gold jewelry, or the clothing you wear — but let your adorning be the hidden person of the heart with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which in God's sight is very precious. … Likewise, husbands, live with your wives in an understanding way, showing honor to the woman as the weaker vessel, since they are heirs with you of the grace of life, so that your prayers may not be hindered." The Petrine articulation establishes the operational character of both partners within the covenant and the prayer-life consequence of the operational dynamics.

Paul holds the supplementary Pauline articulation at 1 Cor. 7 — the extended Pauline marriage instruction. The pericope addresses multiple operational dimensions: the conjugal rights and responsibilities (vv. 1–5); the unmarried-and-widowed counsel (vv. 6–9); the marriage's permanence and the question of divorce (vv. 10–16); the broader question of remaining in one's calling (vv. 17–24); the unmarried virgins (vv. 25–38); the widow's situation (vv. 39–40). The 1 Cor. 7 articulation holds the marriage covenant's operational reality at the apostolic-pastoral instruction level with particular attention to the operational complexities the early-church marriage faced.

The wisdom literature holds the supplementary articulation across multiple texts. Prov. 5:15–19 names the marital sexual exclusivity in the canonical wisdom articulation. Prov. 18:22 names the finding-of-a-wife as obtaining favor from the LORD. Prov. 31:10–31 names the excellent-wife articulation. The Song of Songs holds the canonical articulation of marital love in poetic form, with the lover-and-beloved articulation operating as the canonical biblical love poetry.

The Old Testament marriage cases hold the supplementary narrative articulation across the canonical witness. Adam and Eve at Gen. 2–4 hold the canonical first marriage case. Abraham and Sarah at Gen. 12–25 hold the canonical patriarchal marriage case with the operational features of barrenness, faith, and inter-generational promise. Isaac and Rebekah at Gen. 24 hold the canonical case of marriage initiated through the household's deliberate establishment work. Jacob and Rachel/Leah at Gen. 29–30 hold the canonical case of marriage operating under complications. Boaz and Ruth at Ruth 4 hold the canonical case of marriage operating through covenant-redemption work. Hosea and Gomer at Hosea 1–3 hold the canonical case of marriage operating as prophetic-sign of God's covenant relationship with Israel. The narrative cases together establish the marriage covenant's operational reality across the breadth of canonical witness.

The apocalyptic articulation operates at Rev. 19:6–9 and 21:1–11 with the marriage-supper-of-the-Lamb and new-Jerusalem-as-bride material — the eschatological completion of the Christ-and-Church marriage that Paul's Ephesians 5 articulates. The apocalyptic dimension establishes the marriage covenant's eschatological-completion grounding within the canonical witness's broader scope.

The cumulative scriptural witness establishes the marriage covenant law's operational scope across both Testaments with at least seven distinct biblical authors holding the law substantively (the Genesis writer; Malachi; Matthew and Mark; Paul across multiple letters; the Hebrews writer; Peter; the wisdom writers; the Song of Songs poet; the Hosea writer; John in Revelation). The operational depth across creation, prophetic, gospel, apostolic, wisdom, narrative, and apocalyptic registers establishes the law's canonical centrality.

## Mechanism

The law's mechanism operates by the marriage covenant's architectural-framework function conditioning the operations within the marital dyad. The marriage covenant operates as the dyadic-scale architectural envelope at the same operational structural level the polity-structure operates at the community-scale.

The architectural-framework character of the marriage covenant is the law's pivot. The marriage covenant is not an operation within the dyad's life alongside the spousal-love and the conjugal union and the procreation and the inter-generational household formation; it is the architectural envelope within which those operations are conducted. The spousal-love operates within the covenant's exclusivity arrangement (the one-flesh union being structurally exclusive to the covenant partners per Mal. 2:14, Heb. 13:4, the Pauline 1 Cor. 7 conjugal-rights articulation). The conjugal union operates within the covenant's structural durability (the Matt. 19:6 "what God has joined together let not man separate" naming the covenant's not-to-be-separated character). The procreation operates within the covenant's openness to children (the Gen. 1:28 be-fruitful-and-multiply commission; the Mal. 2:15 "godly offspring" naming the covenant's procreative-purpose). The inter-generational household formation operates within the covenant's leave-and-cleave establishment of the new household.

The leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh pathway is the law's scripturally-specified operational pattern. The three operations the Gen. 2:24 declaration names — leaving father and mother; holding fast to the wife; becoming one flesh — constitute the marriage covenant's operational pathway. The Band 2 articulation reflects the scriptural specification of this pathway: the operational pathway is not tradition-by-tradition determined but scripturally articulated, with the same pathway named at Gen. 2:24 reiterated by Jesus at Matt. 19:5 and by Paul at Eph. 5:31.

**Leaving**: The structural independence of the new household from the parental households. The leaving is not merely geographical or financial; it is structural — the marriage covenant establishes a new household whose operational center is the marital dyad rather than either of the parental households the partners come from. The operational consequences include: the new household's independent decision-making authority (rather than parental decision-making for the married pair); the new household's primary loyalty arrangement (the spouse as primary relational priority); the new household's developmental trajectory (oriented toward the marriage's own formation rather than continuing the parental household's trajectory).

**Cleaving**: The structural durability of the covenant's operational holding. The cleaving (Hebrew dāḇaq, "to cling, adhere, stick to") is the operational adherence to the covenant partner across time. The mechanism by which the cleaving operates includes: the operational priority of the marriage relationship over other relational claims (with appropriate operational priority of God-relationship per FL.III Heart-Throne and FL.XIV Vanity-of-Substitutes); the operational durability through the difficulties that operationally arise (the cleaving's not-yielding-under-pressure character); the operational re-engagement after periods of operational distance (the cleaving's restoration character).

**One-flesh becoming**: The operational unification at the bodily-relational-and-procreative level. The one-flesh becoming is the operational unification of the two partners into the operational unity the covenant produces. The mechanism by which the one-flesh becoming operates includes: the sexual-relational unity (the conjugal union as the bodily expression of the covenant's operational unification); the relational-and-emotional unification (the operational becoming of the two partners as one operational unit at the relational level); the procreative-and-household unification (the children of the marriage as the operational expression of the one-flesh becoming at the inter-generational level); the eschatological reflective dimension (the marriage's one-flesh becoming as the reflection of the Christ-and-Church union per Eph. 5:32).

The Christ-and-Church reflective dimension is the third operational feature the Pauline material establishes. Eph. 5:32's "This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church" names the marriage covenant's deepest operational significance — the dyadic-architectural arrangement reflects the cosmic-eschatological arrangement of Christ and his church. The mechanism by which the reflection operates includes: the husband-wife dynamics structurally paralleling the Christ-Church relationship (the headship-and-submission pattern per Eph. 5:22–24; the love-and-giving-of-self pattern per Eph. 5:25–27; the nourishing-and-cherishing pattern per Eph. 5:28–29); the marriage's eschatological orientation toward the marriage supper of the Lamb (Rev. 19:6–9; 21:1–11); the marriage's operational embodiment of the gospel pattern at the dyadic-scale. The reflective dimension grounds the marriage covenant's significance beyond its merely-dyadic dimension; the marriage operates as the reflection of the cosmic-eschatological reality at the dyadic-architectural scale.

The procreation-and-companionship operational purposes deserve articulation. The marriage covenant operates for multiple purposes the scriptural witness articulates: the companionship purpose (Gen. 2:18's "it is not good that the man should be alone"); the procreation purpose (Gen. 1:28's be-fruitful-and-multiply and Mal. 2:15's godly offspring); the mutual-sanctification purpose (the Pauline Eph. 5:25–27 articulation of the sanctification work the covenant-love performs); the witness purpose (the Christ-and-Church reflective dimension of Eph. 5:32); the kingdom-extension purpose (the household formation as the foundational scale of the church's continued growth, per FL.XXI Household Formation Law and FL.XXVIII Generational Nested Structure Law). The various purposes operate within the single covenant architecture; the marriage covenant is not reducible to any single purpose but operates by the integration of the various purposes within the covenant's architectural framework.

The relationship to other Foundational Laws deserves articulation:

**FL.XII Honor-Authority Flourishing Law** (P2/GV): The honor-authority dynamics within the marriage covenant operate at the dyadic relational scale FL.XII addresses; the marriage covenant's architectural framework holds the honor-authority dynamics within its operational scope, with the Eph. 5:22–33 articulation establishing the specific operational form for the marriage relationship.

**FL.XVII Substitution-Cascade Law** and **FL.XXI Household Formation Law** (P4/GII, paired bidirectional): The marriage covenant operates as the foundational scale of the household whose formation FL.XXI and FL.XVII address at the inter-generational scale; the marriage covenant's operational state determines the household-formation dynamics that operate up-scale through FL.XXI and FL.XVII.

**FL.XXVIII Generational Nested Structure Law** (P4/GVI): The marriage covenant operates as the architectural framework at the dyadic scale within the nested-formation envelope FL.XXVIII articulates; the marriage-household-community-generation-tradition-eschatological-people-of-God nesting begins at the marriage-household scale FL.XXXIV holds.

**FL.XXXIII Community Polity Structure Law** (P3/GVI): The present law operates as the dyadic-scale parallel to FL.XXXIII's community-scale architectural-framework articulation; the two laws together hold the catalog's architectural-framework articulation at the dyadic and community scales (with FL.XXIII Sabbath Rest at P0/GVI and FL.XXVIII at P4/GVI completing the Group VI articulation across the major scales).

**FL.III Heart-Throne Law** and **FL.XIV Vanity-of-Substitutes Law** (P1/GII): The marriage covenant operates within the proper God-orientation FL.III names; the marriage relationship as substitute-for-God operates as the Mirror failure mode FL.XIV addresses, with the operational consequences the marriage-as-idol distortion produces.

## The Broader Dyadic-Pair-Bond Architectural Territory

The marriage covenant is the canonically-articulated primary case of the broader dyadic-pair-bond architectural territory at P2/GVI. The broader territory includes additional dyadic covenant arrangements whose operational pattern parallels the marriage covenant's architectural-framework function at distinct operational scopes.

**The close-friendship covenant** operates at the Jonathan-David case at 1 Sam. 18:1–4 ("As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. … Then Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul. And Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was on him and gave it to David, and his armor, and even his sword and his bow and his belt"); 1 Sam. 20:42 ("Jonathan said to David, 'Go in peace, because we have sworn both of us in the name of the LORD, saying, "The LORD shall be between me and you, and between my offspring and your offspring, forever"'"); 2 Sam. 1:25–26 (David's lament: "your love to me was extraordinary, surpassing the love of women"). The close-friendship covenant operates by structurally analogous architectural-framework function: the covenant's establishment → the architectural framework's operational conditioning of the friendship's operations → the operational consequences for the relationship's continued life. The operational pathway differs from marriage (no sexual-relational unity; no procreative purpose; no household-formation operation), but the architectural-framework function is structurally analogous.

**The discipleship dyad** operates across multiple canonical cases. Elijah-Elisha at 1 Kings 19:19–21 and 2 Kings 2:1–14 hold the canonical case at the prophetic level, with the operational features of the spiritual-anointing transmission (FL.XIX territory). Naomi-Ruth at Ruth 1–4 hold the canonical case at the cross-generational mentoring level, with the operational features of inter-cultural-and-cross-generational covenant. Paul-Timothy at 1 Tim. 1:2 ("To Timothy, my true child in the faith") and 2 Tim. 1:5–6 ("the sincere faith that is in you, a faith that dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and now, I am sure, dwells in you as well. For this reason I remind you to fan into flame the gift of God, which is in you through the laying on of my hands") hold the canonical case at the apostolic-mentoring level. The discipleship dyad's architectural-framework function operates by structurally analogous principles: the relationship's covenantal establishment → the architectural framework's operational conditioning of the discipleship operations → the operational consequences for the mentee's formation and the mentor's continued ministry.

**The spiritual-direction pair** operates in the patristic anam cara tradition, the Eastern Orthodox spiritual-father tradition, the Western contemplative spiritual-direction tradition, the Wesleyan class-meeting partnership tradition, and the modern spiritual-direction recovery. The spiritual-direction pair's architectural-framework function operates by structurally analogous principles to the discipleship dyad with the spiritual-direction-specific operational features (the structured listening; the discernment work; the prayer companionship; the soul-care continuity across time).

The catalog admits marriage as the Foundational Law at the dyadic-scale; the related architectural forms operate by structurally analogous principles within their distinct operational scopes and are articulated here as related cases within the broader pattern.

## Why This Is a Foundational Law

The law meets the four inclusion criteria at exceptional depth. First, multi-author scriptural support is among the strongest in the entire catalog. At least seven distinct biblical authors hold the law substantively (the Genesis writer; Malachi; Matthew and Mark; Paul across multiple letters; the Hebrews writer; Peter; the wisdom writers; the Song of Songs poet; the Hosea writer; John in Revelation), with the operational depth across creation, prophetic, gospel, apostolic, wisdom, narrative, and apocalyptic registers establishing the law's canonical centrality.

Second, broad acceptance across mainstream Christian traditions is uncontested on the operational form. The Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed, Lutheran, Anglican, Anabaptist, Wesleyan, Pentecostal, and broader evangelical traditions all hold marriage as covenant-monogamy between man and woman with substantially uniform operational articulation (the leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh pattern; the procreation-and-companionship purposes; the Christ-and-Church reflective dimension at Eph. 5:32; the eschatological orientation toward the marriage supper of the Lamb). Tradition-differences exist on specific operational questions (the divorce-and-remarriage permissibility; the specific liturgical articulation; the sacramental-versus-covenant status of marriage; the contemporary marriage-definition contestation in some traditions and cultural contexts), but the underlying operational form is held with substantially more uniformity than the polity-structure question that FL.XXXIII addresses at Band 3.

Third, the law is directional in the strict sense. The covenant's establishment → the architectural framework's operational conditioning of the marital dyad's operations → the operational consequences for the marriage's continued life and the inter-generational household formation.

Fourth, the law operates at the principle level with Band 2 articulation. The principle (marriage covenant operates as architectural framework) and the scripturally-specified pathway (leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh per Gen. 2:24 ratified by Matt. 19:5 and Eph. 5:31) are admitted together as the law's articulation. The Band 2 admission is structurally distinct from Band 3 (which leaves operational pathways to tradition-by-tradition determination) — the marriage covenant's operational pathway is scripturally specified rather than tradition-arbitrated, with the same pathway articulated across the canonical witness.

The placement at P2/GVI (Two-or-Three / Structural Frame) reflects the architectural-framework function at the dyadic-pair-bond scale. The vertical directionality tag (V) reflects the relational vector: the marriage covenant is divinely-instituted from creation (Gen. 1:27–28, Gen. 2:18–25); the covenant's binding character is grounded in God's joining-together work (Matt. 19:6 "what therefore God has joined together"); the eschatological reflective dimension grounds the covenant's significance in the Christ-and-Church relationship (Eph. 5:32). The vertical-from-God character is operationally primary; the horizontal-dyadic dimensions between the spouses are derivative from the vertical-divine-establishment character.

**Proposed Law: The Marriage Covenant Architecture Law (Band 2). The marriage covenant — instituted at creation, ratified by Jesus's teaching, articulated by Paul as reflecting the Christ-and-Church relationship — operates as the architectural framework conditioning the operations within the marital dyad; the framework's scripturally-specified operational pathway is the leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh pattern (Gen. 2:24 ratified by Matt. 19:5 and Eph. 5:31), with the operational features of covenant-monogamy between man and woman, exclusivity at the conjugal level, structural durability through divine joining-together, openness to procreation and the household formation operations, and eschatological reflective dimension grounding the dyadic-architectural arrangement in the Christ-and-Church cosmic-eschatological reality.**

The Mal. 2:13–16 failure mode names faithlessness to the covenant partner ("you have been faithless … the man who does not love his wife but divorces her covers his garment with violence"). The Matt. 19:8 failure mode names the hardness-of-heart that produces the divorce dynamic (the Mosaic Deuteronomic divorce provision as accommodation to hardness-of-heart rather than the covenant's normative operational form). The FL.III Heart-Throne and FL.XIV Vanity-of-Substitutes failure mode operates at the marriage-as-idol distortion — the marriage relationship enthroned as the structuring center of formation rather than the God-relationship producing the structural emptiness the substitute-for-God enthronement guarantees. The contemporary marriage-form contestation in some traditions and cultural contexts operates at the institutional-redefinition failure mode — the marriage covenant's scripturally-specified operational pathway (the leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh pattern between man and woman) being modified to other arrangements that operate as different relationship-forms rather than as the marriage covenant the law admits. The catalog's articulation holds the scripturally-articulated marriage covenant as the operational form the law admits; the catalog does not adjudicate on the contemporary contestation but articulates the scriptural-canonical operational form as the Foundational Law's content. Recovery of the law's positive operation requires the covenant's restoration in its scripturally-articulated form at each of the failure-mode levels the law addresses — the faithfulness restoration, the hardness-of-heart healing, the marriage bed's purity, the proper God-orientation displacing the marriage-as-idol pattern, and the covenant's scriptural-form preservation across the contemporary cultural pressures.*
