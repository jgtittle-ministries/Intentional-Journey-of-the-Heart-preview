# Part VI: Tool Protocols

**How to Read This Section**

Part VI: Tool Protocols houses the practitioner-run operational protocol details for the corpus's core tools, the kind of material a Companion II reads to lead a protocol in a session. The section sits structurally between Part V (LotS Project Participants) and the embedded Reference Material section because it is practitioner-oriented operational material rather than bibliographic source material. The relationship to Part III (Processes and Ministry Tools): Part III remains source-bibliography for tools that have institutional rather than single-author origins (GRH, TPM); Part VI is the runnable operational form of those same tools under IJH-core framing.

Each Part VI entry holds the practitioner-runnable detail for one protocol or one protocol family, in a standardized format: *what this protocol does* (the target blockage and what the protocol completes), *when to use it* (the conditions under which the protocol is the right next move), *when NOT to use it* (named failure modes, contraindications, and the discernment questions the Companion holds before starting), *the protocol* (numbered steps with Companion-led framing and participant-side instructions distinguished, step-level discernment notes inline), *closing and de-roling* where applicable, and *cross-references* to the V2 core claim entries the protocol operationally implements.

Every Part VI entry operates under V2.Exp0 (Tool Import Discipline): the source lineage of the protocol is named; what in the source frame requires adaptation or refusal is identified; the operational pattern as deployed in IJH-core form is specified. The Companion bringing the Christian framework to every tool is the operational ground of the section. Powlison's frame/pattern/response grid is the operational discipline that enacts this work.

**The A-list and B-list distinction**

The Part VI entries are organized into two lists, A and B, and the distinction matters operationally for the Companion deciding what to deploy.

**A-list entries (A1–A33)** are protocols for the work itself — what *gets done* in a session. They populate the menu the Companion picks from for the work-component (A26 Tell Your Story, A28 Heart Bible Study with PROAPT, A32 Hot Seat, A24 Blessing Receiving, A25 Breaking the Contracts, A33 Mission at Risk, and so on across the menu), plus the closing-component requirements (A13 Closing-Affirmation, A14 De-roling) and the clearing-methods that operate in the clearings sub-component (A1 MKP Clearing, A2 NVC OFNR).

**B-list entries (B11–B18)** are mostly sub-modalities, access modes, and supporting techniques — *how* the work gets reached or scaffolded, deployed inside other protocols rather than as the central work of a session. V2.Exp6 (Tool Map) names them in their access-mode role: B14 Kinesthetic Metaphor is the canonical operational form for somatic/kinesthetic entry; B11 Ideal Support Figure is the canonical operational form for imaginative-figure entry into grief and tender shame work. The remaining B-list entries follow the same shape: B12 Process Work Recipe (methodology), B13 Front/Back Facilitator Team Structure (team-structuring move), B15 Pull-out Work (Process Work technique), B17 PIES Communication and Clearing Model (clearings sub-component method). The B-list also carries a lineage shape: most of B11–B15 entered the corpus as Process Work imports (Arnold Mindell tradition) under V2.Exp0 Tool Import Discipline.

**The one explicit exception is B18 Heart Prayer Time** — the lone B-list entry that operates as a standalone core work-option in the work-component alongside the A-list options.

The Companion's discernment selects from both lists for a gathering: an A-list work-option for the work-component; B-list access-modes within or alongside the A-list deployment as the participant's processing-style and the surfacing material require.

**Container Forming: ****What this protocol does**

The session liturgy is the corpus's core operational form for community formation work — the sequenced whole of *front-notes → formally-marked container opening → clearings → work → formally-marked closing → integration teachings* that operates as one integrated practice across a single gathering. The session as a whole is the unit of formation; its components are sequenced moves within one practice rather than freely substitutable tools. The integrity of the sequence — the order, the marked transitions between components, the closing that integrates the work into life — is structurally constitutive of the formation the session does.

Within the sequenced whole, the Companion's discernment selects the specific work-option for the gathering from the core menu (the A-list and B-list Phase 2-complete entries plus B18 Heart Prayer Time and the foundational entries that supply scaffolding). The fixed elements are the six-component sequence and the formally-marked transitions; the variable element is which specific work happens in the work-component. The Companion-as-witness discipline (V2.Exp8 refined paragraph 3) operates throughout; the Spirit-mediation framing  operates throughout. *The session is the Lord's; we are gathered in His name; the Spirit is among us.*

This Part VI entry makes the session liturgy runnable for Companions. A Companion II reading this entry should be able to lead a full BoB session from the entry alone, without reverting to the source documents.

**When to use it**

• **As the ****core**** operational form for weekly BoB / FotH small-group practice.** The session liturgy is the default form; departures from it (didactic teaching evenings, social gatherings, retreat formats) are operationally different practices serving different purposes.

• **At weekly rhythm as ****core**** default.** Biweekly or monthly rhythm is acceptable at reduced formation depth; less frequent than monthly typically breaks the formation arc the session liturgy supports.

• **In groups with established container competency** — the V2.Exp8 refined four-condition discipline (Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional) operates as the structural foundation; new groups develop container competency through their first 4–8 sessions before the full session-liturgy's deeper work-options can be deployed reliably.

• **In retreat-format deployments** at extended length (3–6 hours; sometimes longer for multi-session retreats) — the same six-component sequence holds; the work-component expands and may include multiple sub-deployments; the integration-teachings component expands accordingly.

• **In Tool Time leader-formation gatherings** (per C10) — the session liturgy operates with the leader-formation work-component substituting for the regular work-component; the structural form is unchanged.

• **At Companion II competency for full session leadership;** Level I appropriate for assisting Companion II in established bodies. Companion III competency required for groups where significant trauma-history work, contracts work (A25), or breaking-the-contracts-adjacent work is reliably part of the work-component.

**When NOT to use it**

• **In groups that have not yet developed container competency.** The four conditions of V2.Exp8 refined are the structural prerequisite; a group whose container is not reliably formed cannot run the full session liturgy because the work-component depends on the container holding. New groups develop container competency over their first 4–8 sessions; the session liturgy can operate during this development period at reduced work-component depth (lighter work-options; more time on container and clearings; longer integration-teachings).

• **As a one-off event format.** The session liturgy is a *repeating discipline* (per V2.Exp10 Training Plan; per James K. A. Smith's liturgical-anthropology argument; per the 1 Tim 4:7–8 training-discipline scriptural anchor). A single deployment of the session liturgy without the repeating context does not produce what the session liturgy is for the formation over time of the participants and the body. Event-format deployments (workshops, weekend gatherings, retreats) can use the session-liturgy structure but operate as supplementary to repeating practice, not as a substitute for it.

• **In groups whose Companion is not at Companion II competency** for the work-options the gathering will deploy. The Companion II discernment competency is what makes the work-component operate cleanly; Level I assistance is appropriate but not Level I primary leadership.

• **In contexts where the gathering is not actually formation work** — institutional-business meetings, board meetings, planning meetings, social gatherings, didactic teaching evenings. These are legitimate gatherings serving different purposes; calling them "sessions" and running the session-liturgy structure produces neither what the meeting is for nor what the session liturgy is for.

• **When acute clinical or pastoral crisis is the immediate concern of a participant.** The session liturgy is a formation discipline operating over the long arc; acute crisis warrants direct pastoral care, clinical referral, or both, before or in parallel with continued session participation. The Companion's discernment about when crisis warrants stepping outside the session-liturgy frame is itself a Companion II / III competency.

**The protocol**

The session runs typically 90–180 minutes for in-person standard form; 60–75 minutes for compressed form (truncates the integration-teachings component); 3–6 hours for retreat-format deployments. The six components do not skip. The Companion's discernment determines pacing within components; the structural sequence is fixed.

**Component 1 — Front-notes / theology of the work (5–10 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Before we begin to gather formally, let me name briefly what we are doing here tonight. We are gathered in the name of Christ Jesus (Matt 18:20) — not as a meeting, not as a workshop, not as a self-improvement event. We are gathered as a body of disciples doing the Lord's work of formation in each other and through each other. The work is the Lord's; the Spirit is among us; we are the faculty through which the Lord's work operates. What I'll be holding tonight as Companion is the form so the Spirit's work can unfold; what each of you brings tonight is your deliberate attending and choosing — your spirit's faculty operating in active communion with the Holy Spirit (per V1.Exp2 refined). Tonight we'll be working with [name the work-option]. Before we begin, let's settle in briefly and then formally open the container."*

**Participant work:** Settle in, attend, receive the framing. The front-notes orient the participants to the work that follows; the Spirit-mediation framing names *the session is the Lord's* before the structural work begins.

**Companion discernment.** Front-notes vary by lifecycle stage and the work-option for the gathering. New groups (first 4–8 sessions) receive longer foundational front-notes naming what the session-liturgy is and what BoB / FotH practice carries; mature groups receive briefer front-notes focused on the specific work-option for the gathering. The Companion's discernment selects the appropriate front-notes depth.

**Component 2 — Formally-marked container opening (5–10 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Let's formally open the container. We're going to take a minute of silence, then I'll name the four conditions we hold the container under, then we'll do the opening question."*

**The opening moves, in sequence:**

1. **Silence (60–90 seconds).** The Companion holds the silence; participants attend. The marked silence is itself formative per V2.Exp8 refined paragraph 2 (*the formally marked opening is itself an act of formation*).

2. **Naming of the four conditions per V2.Exp8 refined.** *"We hold the container tonight under the four conditions: Safe — what happens in this room stays in this room; Present — we are here together, not somewhere else in our minds; Clear — we will deal with anything between us before we move to the work; Intentional — we are gathered to do this work, not for some other purpose."* The four-condition naming is core to V2.Exp8 refined and operates as the structural foundation.

3. **Opening prayer.** Brief, naming the gathering's purpose and the Lord's presence. Form varies; the Methodist-derived *"Lord, we gather in Your name; we are Your people; we ask Your Spirit's leading in our work tonight"* is one core form; other forms appropriate to the body's tradition are acceptable.

4. **Opening question.** Around the circle, each participant answers briefly. Core forms:

- *"How is it with your soul?"* (Wesleyan-Methodist class-meeting form; corpus default).

- *"What are you bringing to the gathering tonight?"* (BoB form).

- *"Where is the Lord with you this week?"* (Heart Prayer Time-adjacent form, Step 2 derivation).

The Companion selects the form; the form may vary across sessions; the around-the-circle move is structural.

**Participant work:** Land in identity-in-Christ (per V2.Exp9A; per A21 the identity declared in earlier sessions); attend to the four conditions; answer the opening question briefly with what is actually true in the soul, not the social-acceptable answer.

**Companion discernment.** If the opening question surfaces material that requires immediate attention (acute distress; significant news; major life event), the Companion may pause the structural sequence and hold space for the participant before proceeding to clearings. The discernment moves: is this material the gathering's work or is this material requiring its own time? The Companion's judgment governs; the structural sequence resumes when ready.

**Component 3 — Clearings (10–25 minutes; longer if frictions are substantive)**

**Companion framing:** *"Before we move to the work tonight, let's clear anything that's between us in this room. Is there anything anyone is carrying about anyone else — something said in a prior session, something between us outside the session, anything operational that needs to be named before we can be fully present to the work?"*

**The clearings sub-component options.** The Companion selects the clearing-method appropriate to the body and the friction:

• **B17 PIES Communication and Clearing Model.** The four-quadrant frame (Physical / Intellectual / Emotional / Spiritual) and the *when I saw → I felt → the story I told myself → how this affects our relationship → what I want/need* sequence per the pastoral-pattern framing of Founder Decision 5.1(c). Core clearing-method for the corpus; substantively addressed in Track 3 of Cycle 3.

• **A1 MKP Clearing.** The MKP-lineage clearing protocol; simpler than PIES; appropriate for established bodies with well-internalized clearing practice.

• **A2 NVC OFNR.** The Nonviolent Communication Observation → Feeling → Need → Request protocol; appropriate for clearings that touch deeper relational territory and benefit from the explicit needs-naming move.

Where there are no frictions to clear (which is common in mature bodies that clear in real-time outside sessions), the clearings component runs briefly as a quick around-the-circle check. The Companion's pre-session preparation and the body's between-session communication discipline govern how much clearing-work the component requires in a given gathering.

**Companion discernment.** The Companion is the witness per V2.Exp8 refined paragraph 3 — the clearings work is the participants' work; the Companion holds the room while the naming happens, offers the Lord's presence rather than the Companion's interpretation, and defers any pastoral redirection to a subsequent conversation. The Companion does not fix the friction; the participants do the clearing work themselves under the clearing-method's structure. If a clearing surfaces material that exceeds the clearings component's scope (significant trauma, deeper relational rupture, sin-channel material), the Companion routes per the routing logic (Companion II / III consultation; A6 Fifth Step / V2.Exp4 Confession; pastoral care outside the session).

**Component 4 — Work (30–90+ minutes depending on work-option and deployment scale)**

**Companion framing.** The Companion has discerned the work-option before the gathering (or in some cases during the opening question, when the body's bringing makes the work-option's selection emergent). The Companion names the work-option at the start of the work-component:

*"The work tonight is [Heart Bible Study with PROAPT on Romans 8 / Tell Your Story for [name] / Hot Seat for [name] / 7×7 Mortality Visualization / Council deliberation on [topic] / Heart Prayer Time / Blessing Receiving for [name] / Breaking the Contracts for [name] / Mission at Risk / Mission Arc work / Carpet-style work for [name] on [issue] / Confession / etc.]. The specific protocol I'll be running is [A-list entry name] from the **core** corpus. Let me name how we'll do this..."*

**The work-component deployment.** The Companion runs the selected work-option per its core Vol 5 Part VI protocol entry. The Companion II competency operates throughout; the V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices discipline operates wherever internal voices may surface; the V2.Exp8 refined witness-discipline operates wherever participant disclosure happens; the source-reversal Spirit-mediation framing operates throughout. Each work-option's Vol 5 Part VI entry supplies the specific operational depth.

**The work-option menu.** Selected by Companion discernment of what the Spirit is leading the body to engage:

• **A33 Mission at Risk** — surfacing the participant's mission-blockage; what's at risk if the mission is not lived out.

• **A28 Heart Bible Study with PROAPT** — scripture-engagement at six-step depth (Pray, Read, Observe, Apply, Pray, Tell).

• **A29 Any Doubts Process** — closing residual doubt about a specific truth-claim.

• **A26 Tell Your Story** — the participant tells the body their story; corporate witness operates per V2.Exp9A.

• **A32 Hot Seat** — focused mutual examination of the participant's current movement, blockage, or work.

• **A5 Council deliberation** — corporate discernment on a question facing the body or a participant.

• **B18 Heart Prayer Time** — structured five-step prayer protocol (Who am I? / Father, where are You? / What am I praying about? / Enacted prayer / Closure-into-obedience).

• **A17 + A23 Carpet-Style Inner Work family** — wound-healing or shadow-projection work; role-play required; A14 de-roling at closing required.

• **A18–A20 Tell-Show Release family** — wanting; wanting-to-forgive; wanting-forgiveness-from-others.

• **A24 Blessing Receiving** — God-split move plus blessing-round mechanic for shame-at-depth with image-of-God dimensions.

• **A25 Breaking the Contracts** — naming and breaking the contracts the wound carries; Companion II / III gating; spiritual-warfare framing.

• **A15 7×7 Mortality Visualization** — progressive shortening-of-horizons exercise; eschatological framing per Claim 7 in V2.Exp0.

• **A16 Group Mission of Service** — corporate discernment of the body's mission-of-service.

• **A21 Say Your Mission** — formal declaration of individual mission with corporate-witness embodied response.

• **A27 + A30 + A31 Mission-Arc family** — Who Are You / Shadow Mission / Mission Stretch Clarity arc.

• **A12 Splitting a Role** — wound-vs-wronger language move for re-integration work.

• **A8 Transforming a Shaming Voice** — internal-voice work with Spirit / accuser / lie discernment at the front.

• **A22 BoB Onboarding** — entry into the session liturgy for new participants.

• **A6 Fifth Step** — substantive confessional disclosure work.

• **V2.Exp4 refined Confession** — the corpus's broader confession protocol.

**Companion discernment within the work-component.** The Companion's primary work within the work-component is to run the selected work-option per its core protocol; the secondary work is to hold the Spirit-mediation framing throughout and to discern when material surfacing in the work routes beyond the work-option's scope. Routing logic: shame-at-depth with image-of-God dimensions → A24; contract material → A25; specific doubt-of-truth → A29; sin-channel material → V2.Exp4 / A6; wound material requiring carpet-style → A17 family; mission-articulation → A21 or Mission-Arc family; story-material → A26.

**Component 5 — Formally-marked closing (15–30 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Let's begin to close the container. We're going to do this in sequence: first de-roling if we did role-play tonight; then the closing affirmation; then the closing anchor; then a closing prayer; then the formal release of the container."*

**The closing moves, in sequence:**

1. **De-roling per A14 (where role-play occurred).** Non-negotiable structural requirement of the session liturgy's closing component whenever any work-option in the gathering involved role-play (carpet-style work, empty-chair speech, held-figure work, internalized-voice engagement). The Companion runs the A14 reciprocal de-roling protocol per its core Vol 5 Part VI entry. *"I am [role-player name], not [role I held]; I release the role of [role]; I am [my own name]; I am in this body, this room, this Christ-redeemed self."* Reciprocal acknowledgment from the participant who held the role-player. Without de-roling, the role-play work's residual material may carry forward in ways that interfere with the participants' integration of the work.

2. **Closing-affirmation per A13.** The Companion frames: *"What did the Lord speak in the work tonight? What is each of you taking from the work as the Lord's word to you?"* Around the circle, each participant names briefly what the Spirit named in the work. The closing-affirmation pattern per A13 — the affirmation is the Lord's, not the participant's self-affirmation and not the body's social-affirmation — is structural to the closing. The source-reversal core series at the closing-affirmation level (Cycle 1 / A13 Phase 2) operates here; the Companion's framing language keeps the Spirit-mediation explicit.

3. **Closing anchor.** Around the circle, each participant names briefly one or two sentences about what they take from the session into the week. The anchor integrates the work into life; without the anchor, the work may stay in the session and not translate to obedience. The closing anchor operates structurally as what A21 Say Your Mission and the broader V1.Exp5 Obedience Channel name — the work translates into the obedience-direction.

4. **Closing prayer.** Brief, committing the work to the Lord. Form varies; the core form names the Spirit's work in the gathering, the participants' carrying of the work into life, and the body's gathering again at the next session. *"Lord, we thank You for Your work in us tonight. We commit to You what we have heard and what we will do. Keep us in Your love until we gather again. Amen."* Other forms appropriate to the body's tradition are acceptable.

5. **Formal release of the container.** The Companion names the close: *"The container is closed. We are released. Go in peace to love and serve the Lord."* The release move may include a candle extinguished, a spoken benediction (Num 6:24–26 or similar), a final around-the-circle blessing-handshake, or another marker the body has established. The release is the formally-marked closing per V2.Exp8 refined; work that is opened but not closed dissipates rather than integrates.

**Companion discernment in the closing.** The closing's pacing matters — too fast loses the integration; too slow loses the marked-ness. Twenty minutes is core default; thirty minutes appropriate where the work was substantial; fifteen minutes appropriate for lighter sessions. The Companion's discernment selects pacing.

**Component 6 — Integration teachings (10–20 minutes; sometimes folded into closing or front-notes)**

**Companion framing.** Integration teachings carry the discipleship-training function across sessions. The Companion has discerned in pre-session preparation which teaching to deploy in the gathering — or whether the gathering's work substituted for explicit teaching (some sessions, the work itself is the teaching).

**The teaching options.** Core recurring teachings per TGL Feb 2004 and the corpus's broader anthropology:

• **Projection teaching.** The discipline of recognizing when participants are projecting onto each other or onto the work. *"What we see in another that troubles us often names what we have not yet faced in ourselves. The work is to attend to our own projection before naming the other."* Connects to V2.Exp2A False Self and A17 + A23 Carpet-Style family.

• **Theology-of-brokenheartedness teaching.** The Eldredge / Waking-the-Dead framing of why the work proceeds the way it does. *"The heart is the place of the work; the wounds are real; the healing is the Lord's work in time; the body is the place where the healing happens."* Connects to V1.Exp1 (Hearing Channel), V2.Exp1 (Heart Soil), V2.Exp2 (Emotional Knots), and the broader corpus's heart-formation theology.

• **Container teaching.** Particularly for new bodies — what the four conditions are and why each matters. Connects to V2.Exp8 refined.

• **Clearings teaching.** What clearings are and how they keep the container Clear. Connects to V2.Exp8 condition C and B17 PIES.

• **Hearing teaching.** What hearing-with-understanding is and how the affective taxonomy stages work. Connects to V2.Exp7 and Formation.HFT.

• **Tool Map teaching.** What the core work-options are and how the body's work selects from the Map. Connects to V2.Exp6.

• **Source-reversal teaching.** The discipline of locating agency at the Lord's level, not the participant's level. *"The affirmation is the Lord's; the mission is the Lord's; the work is the Lord's; we are the faculty through which the Lord's work operates."* Connects to V1.Exp2 refined and the source-reversal core series.

• **Lifecycle-stage teaching.** Where the body is in its lifecycle and what the current stage's work asks of the participants. Connects to V2.Exp10 Training Plan and (forthcoming) B21 Invitation + B22 Group-Lifecycle Closing.

**Companion discernment.** The body's lifecycle stage governs teaching selection — new groups (first 4–8 sessions) receive foundational teachings; mid-stage groups receive deeper teachings; mature groups receive contextual or lifecycle-stage teachings. The Companion's discernment selects the teaching appropriate to where the body is.

**Optional folding.** In some sessions, the integration-teachings component folds into front-notes (a substantive front-note that does teaching work) or into closing (a substantive closing-frame that does teaching work). The Companion's discernment governs whether to deploy explicit integration-teachings as a discrete component or fold into other components.

## Companion notes
**On the Spirit-mediation framing throughout.** The session liturgy operates under the source-reversal at seven core instances. The Companion's framing language at each component-transition keeps the Spirit-mediation explicit so the session is received already reframed, not as facilitated event with a Christian gloss. The framing matters operationally because the facilitated-event drift is the most common failure mode — the session can produce real human work without the Spirit-mediation framing, but the work it produces is different from what the core anthropology presupposes the session does.

**On the Companion-as-witness discipline (V2.Exp8 refined paragraph 3).** The Companion's stance throughout the session is witness rather than reframing. The naming itself is the formative act; the Companion's discipline is to hold the room while the naming happens, to offer the Lord's presence rather than the Companion's interpretation, and to defer any pastoral redirection to a subsequent conversation. This discipline applies in clearings, in the work-component, in the closing-affirmation, and in the closing anchor.

**On the body's lifecycle and the session liturgy's deployment over time.** The session liturgy's deepest work happens not in any single gathering but in the long-term accumulation of repeated session deployment across the body's lifecycle. New groups develop container competency over their first 4–8 sessions; the work-component depth grows as the body's container competency grows; mature groups can hold deeper work-options reliably. The Companion's pacing of work-option depth over the body's lifecycle is itself a Companion II / III competency.

**On retreat-format deployments.** The same six-component sequence holds in retreat format; the work-component expands and may include multiple sub-deployments; the integration-teachings component expands accordingly. Pacing changes (longer for each component; longer breaks between components); the structural sequence is unchanged. Retreats typically run multi-session over a weekend, with each session a full deployment of the session liturgy.

**On Tool Time leader-formation gatherings (C10).** The session liturgy operates with the leader-formation work-component substituting for the regular work-component; the structural form is unchanged. Tool Time gatherings deploy specific protocols for Companion II / III formation; the session-liturgy structure holds the work.

**On when crisis warrants stepping outside the session-liturgy frame.** Acute clinical or pastoral crisis warrants direct care outside the session-liturgy. The Companion's discernment determines whether the crisis can be addressed within the session (typical: a clearing escalating beyond clearings; a work-option's surfacing exceeding the work-option's scope) versus requires stepping outside the session-liturgy frame (typical: imminent self-harm; acute psychiatric symptoms; relational rupture beyond the body's capacity to hold). When stepping outside is needed, the Companion does so cleanly — names that the gathering's session-liturgy is suspended; addresses the crisis directly; brings the body's care to bear; reconvenes the session-liturgy at the body's next gathering or earlier per pastoral judgment.

**On attribution.** The BoB session liturgy was first drafted in written form in Feb 2004 (Session 1, Founder Decision 11.2 confirmed B16 as core home for the integrated session-liturgy attribution). Substantial subsequent operational development by Dave Smith (the four-connects of V2.Exp5; the BoB Carpet-Style Inner Work; the Heart Prayer Time April 2006 discussion notes per B18 Phase 2; the 2008 ASC Manual co-development) and by John Tittle (the broader corpus's session-liturgy formalization including this Cycle 3 Phase 2 work) followed; the Band of Brothers and Fellowship of the Heart bodies collectively practiced and refined the form across the following two decades. The honest framing: a lineage practice with the earliest written form, Smith and Tittle's substantial subsequent development, and the bodies' collective practice as the operational refinement.

## Materials
• **Space appropriate to the body's size.** Circle seating is core default for groups up to 12; larger groups may require modified seating. Quiet, private, with the door closed once the session begins.

• **A candle or other physical marker** (optional but common) — lit at the formally-marked opening, extinguished at the formal release of the container. The marker operationalizes the V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-opening-and-closing discipline at a sensory level.

• **A journal or notebook** for each participant (optional but encouraged) — for the work-component (some work-options use journaling explicitly: A15 7×7; A28 PROAPT; B18 Heart Prayer Time) and for the closing anchor (writing down what the participant takes from the session into the week).

• **Scripture** (Bible; phone-Bible acceptable; the core scripture for the work-option if specified).

• **Materials specific to the work-option deployed** — varied per work-option's Vol 5 Part VI entry. Common: chairs for empty-chair work (carpet-style family); space for stick-as-marker (A21 Say Your Mission); ax-handle for A25 if the ax-handle anger-release option is used per Companion discernment (Cycle 1 Decision 8.18 optional).

## Time
• **Standard session:** 90–180 minutes total. Allocation: Front-notes 5–10 min; Opening 5–10 min; Clearings 10–25 min; Work 30–90+ min; Closing 15–30 min; Teachings 10–20 min (or folded). Pacing varies by work-option and body lifecycle stage.

• **Compressed session:** 60–75 minutes total. Truncates integration-teachings (folded into other components) and may shorten work-component to a single-step or lighter deployment. Useful when the body has a hard time constraint; not core for regular weekly rhythm.

• **Retreat-format deployments:** 3–6 hours per session in retreat context; multiple sessions over weekend or extended-event. Work-component expands; integration-teachings component expands; structural sequence unchanged.

• **Tool Time leader-formation deployments:** Per C10; structural sequence holds; work-component is leader-formation work.
