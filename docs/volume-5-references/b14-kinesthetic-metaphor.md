# B14 Kinesthetic Metaphor

## What this protocol does
Kinesthetic Metaphor is the core somatic-first access-mode for carpet-work across blockage-types. For participants whose processing is body-led rather than verbal/visual-led — and for moments in any participant's work when verbal/visual entry has gone flat or stuck — B14 supplies the operational protocol: localize the felt sense in the body, refine a drawable metaphor, let the body conform to the metaphor's feeling, gather scene details, set out the roles where needed. The somatic entry replaces the verbal/visual split as the on-ramp; the rest of the carpet-work proceeds from somatic ground.

B14 is the core operational form of the *somatic-first access-mode* dimension of the Tool Map. Per Decision 12.2, V2.Exp6 core text gains a refinement naming the access-mode dimension (verbal/visual vs. somatic/kinesthetic) as orthogonal to the existing blockage-type axis (grief, shame, fear, anger, sin). The body is not external to formation work; the body is the Spirit-indwelt temple (1 Cor 6:19) where the Spirit speaks through what scripture calls the body's groaning (Rom 8:23). *The body's hearing is the Spirit's* — B14 is the core operational form of this reversal at the carpet-work modality altitude.

**When to use it**

• **For participants whose processing is body-led.** Some participants do not think in pictures, do not hear internal voices, do not narrate themselves in words — but their bodies carry felt-sense information richly. The verbal/visual access modes the existing Tool Map presupposes do not reach these participants; B14 is the core access mode.

• **For any blockage-type when verbal/visual entry has gone flat.** A participant who has been working verbally or visually and is now stuck — the words are circular; the picture is foggy; the work is repeating without moving — may benefit from somatic-first re-entry. The Companion routes mid-session from verbal/visual to B14.

• **For fear-knots specifically.** Fear is physically expressed by definition (Rom 8:15, the spirit of slavery leading to fear; physiological correlates of fear are body-resident). B14 is operationally well-suited to fear-knot work even when the participant has good verbal/visual access; the somatic entry tends to reach fear material more directly than verbal/visual entry.

• **For sexual-abuse-survivor adaptation work** where verbal narrative may not be available or may carry secondary trauma; the somatic-first access opens material that words cannot yet hold. Companion II minimum competency required; Companion III for substantive trauma deployment.

• **For participants who have been doing inner-work for years and have plateaued on verbal/visual entry.** Mature inner-work practitioners often benefit from somatic re-entry that bypasses the patterns their verbal/visual access has developed; the somatic entry opens material that the practiced verbal/visual access has learned to manage rather than feel.

• **At Companion II competency.** The somatic-first protocol presupposes carpet-work competency (the participant-Companion relationship is established; the container is held under V2.Exp8 refined; the Companion can deploy related carpet-work protocols when material surfaces).

**When NOT to use it**

• **As substitute for verbal/visual work when both access modes are operationally available.** B14 is not a "deeper" form of carpet-work than verbal/visual work; it is a different access mode. Where the participant's verbal/visual access is operating cleanly and the work is moving, switching to B14 produces operational friction without benefit.

• **In contexts where the body's felt-sense reading is unstable.** Some participants — particularly during acute dissociation, active flashback, severe trauma activation, or substance-impairment — cannot reliably read body felt-sense. B14 presupposes stable somatic perception; where the perception is not stable, the work routes elsewhere (B15 for active flashback; A8 or other appropriate work for the underlying material; clinical care if needed).

• **In contexts where somatic awareness is being weaponized.** Some participants have learned somatic-awareness practices in secular or non-Christian-grounded contexts (yoga; mindfulness; somatic-experiencing therapy; alternative spiritualities) and have developed somatic-attending habits that carry philosophical commitments the corpus does not endorse. B14's Christian-frame adaptation operates on body-as-Spirit-indwelt-temple grounding; participants whose somatic-attending operates on body-as-source-of-wisdom grounding may need the framing addressed before B14 deploys cleanly.

• **In contexts where the body's somatic emergence may be substance-related.** Some somatic phenomena have direct medical or substance-related causes that warrant medical attention rather than carpet-work; the Companion's discernment about whether somatic emergence is formation-relevant material or medical material governs.

• **In bodies that have not developed carpet-work competency.** B14 is a carpet-work modality; bodies whose container competency has not reached the point of holding any carpet-work cannot hold B14 specifically. The body's container-development governs whether B14 is available.

## The protocol
B14 runs across six operational moves, typically 45–90 minutes for a complete deployment. The protocol may operate as standalone work-component, as the access-mode within B12's recipe step 2 (representing the internal state somatically rather than verbally), or as mid-session routing from a verbal/visual carpet-work that has gone flat.

**Move 1 — Localize the felt sense in the body (5–10 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Let's let your body have a turn here. When you think about [whatever is being worked]... when it's there, where in your body is it? Not the answer your mind would give — what does your body show you? Where does it live?"*

**Participant work:** Attend to the body. The participant scans somatically — sometimes the answer is immediate (*"my chest"*; *"my throat"*; *"my gut"*); sometimes the answer requires sustained attending (the participant stays with the body for half a minute or longer before the location surfaces). The participant names the location specifically — not "in my torso" but *"high in my chest, just below my collarbone, on the left"* or *"in my gut, low and toward my back"*. Specificity matters because the metaphor refinement in subsequent moves operates on the located felt-sense.

**Companion discernment.** The localization move trains the participant's somatic-attending faculty. New B14 deployments may need extended time at this move while the participant develops the habit of reading the body; mature B14 deployments may move through localization in under a minute. The Companion's pacing accommodates where the participant is in this developmental arc. If the participant cannot locate after extended attending, the Companion may surface a possibility ("sometimes it's in the chest, throat, or gut for material like this — does any of those feel right?") but does not impose a location; the participant's own somatic reading governs.

**Move 2 — Refine to a specific drawable metaphor (10–20 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Now we'll let the felt sense show us what it is. What's the size of it? About how big? [Pause for somatic answer.] What's the shape? [Pause.] What's the color? [Pause.] What's the texture? [Pause.] So a big black pointy *what*? Let your body finish the sentence."*

**Participant work:** Stay with the felt sense and let it refine into a specific drawable metaphor. The participant's body supplies the data: *size* (a marble; a baseball; a basketball; a boulder); *shape* (round; oblong; angular; jagged; flat; pointed); *color* (black; red; gray; white; brown; rust); *texture* (smooth; rough; sharp; soft; spiky; metallic; rubbery; cold; hot). The Companion paces the question-sequence, letting the participant's body answer each before moving to the next.

The crescendo move is the noun-naming at the end: *"so a big black pointy *what*?"* The participant's body names the metaphor: *"a spike"*; *"a knife"*; *"a knotted rope"*; *"a brick wall"*; *"a steel band"*; *"a black ball of tar"*; *"a frozen lake"*. The naming is what makes the felt sense workable — the participant now has a specific drawable image that the body has supplied; the rest of the work operates on that image.

**Companion discernment.** The metaphor refinement is operationally the most consequential single move in B14. The Companion's pacing matters substantially — too fast loses the body's voice; too slow loses the participant's attention to the felt sense. Bilgere's prescribed pacing (slow, gentle, repeat-back-the-exact-words pacing the participant uses) is operationally important and theologically appropriate (the body-as-temple is engaged carefully; the somatic emergence is the Spirit speaking through the body's groaning per Rom 8:23 and is attended to as such).

The Companion's discipline at this move is *not to suggest the metaphor*. The participant's body supplies the metaphor; the Companion's role is to ask the question-sequence and to repeat back what the participant says, refining as the body refines. Companion suggestions ("could it be a spike?"; "does that feel like a wall?") substitute Companion content for participant body-voice; the discipline preserves the participant's somatic agency.

If the metaphor that surfaces is theologically loaded (e.g., the participant names the felt sense as "demon" or "darkness" or "evil presence"), the Companion's V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices discipline operates. Sometimes the participant's body is rightly naming spiritual material; sometimes the metaphor language is the participant's interpretation of a felt sense that is something different. The Companion holds the metaphor lightly and does not commit theologically to its specific framing until the work has further developed.

**Move 3 — Let the body conform to the feeling of that metaphor (5–10 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Let your body conform to the feeling of that [metaphor]. Let your posture, your breathing, your face — let your body show what the [metaphor] feels like from the inside. Take your time."*

**Participant work:** The participant lets their physical posture, breathing, facial expression, and overall embodiment conform to the metaphor. A spike: the body may become rigid, narrow, sharp at the edges. A knotted rope: the body may twist; the breath constricts. A frozen lake: the body becomes still, flat, cold. A steel band: the body holds tension, restriction, a particular gripped quality. The conformation is somatic embodiment of what the metaphor names.

**Companion discernment.** This move is what makes B14 distinct from purely cognitive metaphor work — the body becomes the metaphor it has named, and the work operates on the embodied state rather than on the abstracted concept. The Companion attends to the conformation (without staring intrusively); the body's conformation often surfaces material the metaphor alone did not show — what age the felt sense is from; what scene is associated; what figures are present in the memory.

If the participant cannot conform — if the metaphor is too abstract or the participant's body is not yet able to access the embodied state — the Companion may re-engage Move 2 to refine the metaphor further (sometimes a more specific metaphor surfaces; sometimes the metaphor was right but the conformation needs more time). The participant's body governs the pacing.

**Move 4 — Regress to the age when the metaphor first appeared (5–10 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"While your body holds the [metaphor], let me ask: how old are you in this place? How young is the [metaphor]? Not the answer your mind would give — what does the body show you?"*

**Participant work:** With the body still conforming to the metaphor, the participant identifies the age of the felt sense's first installation. *"Maybe five"; "I'm in elementary school"; "high school — I'm fifteen or sixteen"; "this is from before I was born — I can't put an age on it"; "this is from my mom carrying me"*. The age-identification follows the body's lead rather than the participant's narrative knowledge of their own history.

**Companion discernment.** The "regression" language and inner-child framing in the source document carry Jungian-Eriksonian commitments the corpus does not endorse; the core V2 adaptation substitutes "younger self" or "earlier season" language. The Companion's framing uses *"how young are you in this"* or *"what season is this from"* rather than *"regress to your inner child"*. The somatic mechanism (the body locating the felt sense's first installation) is theologically clean and operates under 1 Cor 6:19 body-as-temple — the body remembers what the cognitive memory does not, and the somatic reading is a faculty of the embodied person rather than a Jungian sub-self.

Some participants surface ages that pre-date their conscious memory — early childhood ages, infancy, pre-natal. Some surface no specific age but a season or pattern ("this is from every time my dad was disappointed in me"; "this is from how my mom held herself when she was anxious"). The Companion holds whatever the body surfaces without insisting on chronological specificity. The participant's somatic reading is the operational data.

**Move 5 — Gather scene details and set out the roles (5–20 minutes; depends on whether scene-externalization is needed)**

**Companion framing:** *"Now we'll let the scene come into focus. With the [metaphor] still in your body, with the age you've named — what are you wearing? Where are you? Who else is there? What's the room like?"*

**Participant work:** The participant gathers scene details — clothing (the specific outfit, often diagnostic of the moment); location (bedroom; kitchen; classroom; somewhere specific); other figures present (parents; siblings; a specific other adult; the participant alone); environmental details (the lighting; the sounds; the smell). The body's somatic reading supplies the details; the participant attends and names.

**Companion discernment.** This move is where B14 transitions from purely somatic work to scene-based carpet-work. When the scene comes into focus and other figures are present, the Companion sets out role-players to hold the figures (per B12 Process Work Recipe step 2; per the broader carpet-work tradition). When the scene is the participant alone or with environmental presence rather than human figures, role-players may not be needed; the work proceeds with the participant and the somatic-embodied scene.

Where role-players are set out, the work has now entered structural territory where A14 De-roling is non-negotiable at the work's close (per V2.Exp8B Component 5 closing). The Companion holds the de-roling requirement in mind as a structural feature of the rest of the work.

The scene-gathering move surfaces what the carpet-work needs to address next — which protocol from the carpet-work menu will deploy at Move 6. Common routings: a wound-figure present → A17 Healing and Forgiveness sequence; a shaming voice present → A8 Transforming a Shaming Voice; a projection-target present → A23 Shadow and Projections sequence; an absence (the absence is the wound — the parent who wasn't there, the love that wasn't given) → B11 Ideal Support Figure to bring in what was missing.

**Move 6 — Deploy the carpet-work protocol the scene calls for (20–60+ minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Now we'll engage the work the scene is asking for. What's surfacing is [name the carpet-work form — wound healing; shame work; projection recovery; reparenting]. Let me name how we'll do this..."*

**Participant work:** Engage the deployed carpet-work protocol from the somatic-first ground B14 has established. The participant works within whatever protocol Move 5's scene-gathering surfaced.

**Companion discernment.** B14's Move 6 is functionally the same as B12 Process Work Recipe's Step 4 — work the dynamic with whatever the discernment surfaces. The Companion runs the selected carpet-work protocol per its core Vol 5 Part VI entry, with the somatic-first ground from B14 supplying the access mode rather than the verbal/visual entry the protocol may default to. The closing of Move 6 happens when the work has reached natural completion; the work-component's closing per V2.Exp8B Component 5 (de-roling per A14 where role-play occurred; closing affirmation per A13; closing anchor) follows.

## Companion notes
**On the source-reversal Spirit-mediation framing throughout.** The Companion's framing language at each move keeps the locus of agency at the Spirit's level rather than at the body-as-source-of-wisdom level. *The body's hearing is the Spirit's; the somatic emergence is the Spirit's speech through the body's groaning; my attending is the faculty through which the Spirit's work in the body is received.* Without the framing, B14 produces real somatic work that may or may not be formation; with it, the somatic work operates under the source-reversal core discipline. The eleventh core instance of the source-reversal series (B14's *the body's hearing is the Spirit's*) operates here.

**On the Bilgere-prescribed slow, gentle, repeat-back pacing.** Bilgere's manual specifically prescribes a slow, gentle pacing with the Companion repeating back the participant's exact words. The pacing matters operationally — somatic emergence happens at a different speed than cognitive work; rushing the pacing loses the body's voice; the repeat-back discipline confirms to the participant that their somatic reading is being received accurately and supports the body's continued speaking. The pacing is theologically appropriate to the body-as-temple framing — the temple is engaged carefully and at the pace the Spirit's speech requires.

**On the Companion's discipline of not suggesting content.** The participant's body supplies the metaphor, the location, the age, the scene. The Companion's discipline is to ask the question-sequence and repeat back what the participant says; the Companion does not suggest metaphor content, location content, age content, or scene content. Suggestion-from-Companion substitutes Companion projection for participant somatic-reading; the discipline preserves the participant's somatic agency. Where the participant is stuck, the Companion may surface possibilities ("sometimes felt-sense like this is in the chest, throat, or gut — does any of those feel right?") with explicit naming of the suggestion as possibility rather than as answer; the participant's body governs.

**On the regression-language reframing.** "Regression" and "inner child" carry Jungian-Eriksonian philosophical commitments the corpus does not endorse. The core Christian-frame substitution uses "younger self," "earlier season," "how old are you in this place." The substitution preserves the operational mechanism (the body locating where the felt-sense was first installed) without importing the philosophical anthropology. New Companions sometimes default to the source-document's language; the discipline is to use the substituted language consistently across deployments.

**On routing when the somatic-first access surfaces material that exceeds B14's scope.** Common routings during B14 deployment:

• *Active flashback or severe dissociation surfacing during Move 3 conformation* → must shift to B15 with Companion III competency; or close the work cleanly and route to subsequent care.

• *Material that needs Companion-III-level competency surfacing at any point* → close B14 cleanly at the next natural breakpoint; route to appropriate Companion or subsequent care.

• *Sin-channel material surfacing during the work* → route to V2.Exp4 refined Confession / A6 Fifth Step after the carpet-work closes.

• *Image-of-God / blessing-deficit material surfacing during the work* → may shift to A24 within Move 6 or after the work closes.

• *Contract / agreement material surfacing during the work* → may shift to A25 within Move 6 or after the work closes.

The Companion's during-B14 discernment about routing is itself a Companion II / III competency.

**On verbal/visual processors deploying B14.** B14 is not exclusively for body-led processors; verbal/visual processors can deploy B14 as a complementary access mode when their verbal/visual work has gone flat. The deployment may feel mechanical for verbal/visual processors initially (the body's metaphor-supplying is not their habitual processing); the Companion's pacing supports the deployment as the participant's body finds its voice in the work. Many verbal/visual processors find B14 substantially valuable as a periodic access mode even if it is not their primary processing.

**On the corpus's broader treatment of the body.** B14 sits within the corpus's broader treatment of embodied formation (the body-as-temple emphasis throughout Vol 1 anthropology; the Garden of Your Heart somatic entry for fear in V2.Exp2; the V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked container's somatic-marker option; the broader carpet-work tradition's somatic-objectification opening in A17 + A23). B14 is the core somatic-first access mode; the broader body-formation territory operates across multiple V2 explorations.

## Materials
• **Space appropriate to carpet-work session** — per the standard carpet-work container.

• **Optional drawing materials** — some Companions provide drawing materials for the metaphor refinement at Move 2, allowing the participant to literally draw what their body is showing. The drawing supports the metaphor refinement and provides a take-home artifact of the work. Not required.

• **Materials specific to subsequent carpet-work protocols** — per Move 6's deployed protocol (each carpet-work protocol's Part VI entry supplies the specific materials).

## Time
• **Standard B14 deployment:** 45–90 minutes total. Allocation: Move 1 (localize) 5–10 min; Move 2 (metaphor refinement) 10–20 min; Move 3 (body conformation) 5–10 min; Move 4 (age) 5–10 min; Move 5 (scene) 5–20 min; Move 6 (carpet-work protocol) 20–60+ min.

• **Compressed B14 deployment:** 30–45 minutes total for lighter work that does not require full Move 6 carpet-work protocol deployment; the somatic emergence at Moves 1–3 may itself be the work for the gathering.

• **Extended B14 deployment:** 90+ minutes for complex carpet-work where Move 6 deploys a substantial protocol from the somatic-first ground.
