# B13 Front/Back Facilitator Team Structure

## What this protocol does
The Front/Back Facilitator Team Structure is a two-Companion group-leadership infrastructure for carpet-work and group-mode formation work. One Companion serves as *front* — works in close, does most of the talking, runs the protocol's substantive moves with the participant. One Companion serves as *back* — stays at the edge of the work, tracks the bigger picture and the group's mood, has explicit standing to tap the front Companion out when needed. Group members route ideas through the back Companion, who decides whether to bring them forward to the front. The structure operationalizes Pauline mutual-submission (Eph 5:21) at the Companion-team level, with the discipline that the front Companion *yields to the back Companion's discernment* when the back calls it — *"if my back facilitator says I'm in my stuff, I step back even when I'm sure they're wrong"* (Bilgere's discipline).

B13 is core infrastructure for group-mode carpet-work deployment within the session liturgy (V2.Exp8B). The two-Companion structure is required for group-mode carpet-work deployment per Decision 12.5 (Session 9, May 12, 2026); smaller settings (dyadic; very small group) may proceed with single-Companion deployment, with the V2.Exp8 refined witness-discipline holding the work in the absence of a second Companion. The corpus's broader practice has used single-Companion deployment as common default; B13's coreization establishes the two-Companion structure as the core infrastructure for group-mode work at scale, with the understanding that body-level Companion-staffing may not always permit two-Companion deployment and the V2.Exp8 refined single-Companion fallback remains operational.

**When to use it**

• **For group-mode carpet-work deployment within the session liturgy.** Core infrastructure. Whenever the work-component deploys carpet-work (A17, A23, B11, B14, B15, or combinations via B12) in a group-mode setting (typically four or more participants), the two-Companion structure is the core staffing default.

• **For larger group-mode formation work generally.** Sessions with substantial work-component depth, retreat-format deployments, Tool Time leader-formation gatherings (C10), Gateway events, and any group-mode work where the holding-capacity requires two-Companion attention benefit substantially from B13 deployment.

• **For Companion II-to-III competency development.** A Companion II practitioner serving as back Companion to a Companion III front Companion develops in observable apprenticeship; the back position is structurally available as the developmental position from which Companion III competency matures. Tool Time gatherings (C10) can deploy B13 as primary instructional infrastructure.

• **For high-stakes work-component deployments.** Where the work-option carries high theological load (B11 bridge-to-the-Father; A24 image-of-God depth-work; A25 contract breaking; B15 trauma specialty), two-Companion structure provides operational safety the work warrants.

• **In bodies developing Companion-staffing capacity.** As bodies mature and develop multiple Companion-competent members, B13 becomes available; the structure is part of what mature group-mode formation work looks like in the corpus's practice.

**When NOT to use it**

• **In smaller settings.** Dyadic work, very small group (3 or fewer participants), and intimate spiritual-direction-style gatherings can proceed with single-Companion deployment under V2.Exp8 refined witness-discipline. The two-Companion structure adds operational overhead disproportionate to the work's holding-requirements in these settings.

• **In contexts where Companion-staffing does not permit two-Companion deployment.** Bodies in earlier lifecycle stages may not have two Companion-competent members; the body's group-mode work proceeds with single-Companion deployment under V2.Exp8 refined fallback discipline. The core structure does not legislate two-Companion staffing where the body's staffing reality does not yet permit it; the core preference is that bodies develop the staffing-capacity over time.

• **In contexts where the two Companions have unresolved relational material between them.** The mutual-submission discipline at the heart of B13 requires functional Companion-relational health between the front and back; where unresolved material exists between the two, the team-structure can be operationally compromised. The pre-deployment Companion-team relational check (named below in Companion notes) governs whether the team is ready for the gathering.

• **When the back Companion has not yet developed the discernment-from-edge competency.** The back position requires specific discernment competency that develops with practice — tracking the bigger picture; reading the group's mood; recognizing when the front Companion is operating from their own stuff; calibrating when to tap the front out. A Companion II without this specific competency may technically occupy the back position without operationally fulfilling its function; the apprenticeship development through observed B13 deployments is the corpus's core pathway.

• **In contexts where a single Companion is operationally sufficient.** Not every group-mode work needs two Companions; lighter work-component deployments (PROAPT; Heart Prayer Time at group scale; clearings-heavy sessions; teaching-heavy gatherings) may proceed with single-Companion deployment. The Companion's discernment about whether the gathering's work-option warrants two-Companion structure governs.

## The protocol
B13 deployment runs across three phases: pre-gathering Companion-team preparation; during-gathering team-structure operation; post-gathering Companion-team debrief. The structural moves are operational discipline rather than rigid sequence; the Companion-team's developing competency shapes how the moves operate.

**Phase 1 — Pre-gathering Companion-team preparation (15–60 minutes before gathering)**

**Companion-team work:** The front and back Companions meet briefly before the gathering to:

• *Confirm roles for the gathering.* Who is front; who is back. The roles may rotate across gatherings (Companion A front for tonight; Companion B front next gathering) or may be stable (Companion A always front; Companion B always back for this body). The rotation pattern is the Companion-team's discernment; both stability and rotation have pastoral benefits.

• *Review the gathering's expected work-component.* What work-option is the Companion-team planning to deploy; what carpet-work protocols may be involved; what specific participants may need particular attention; what material from prior sessions is open and may continue surfacing.

• *Run a brief Companion-team relational check.* Per Eph 5:21 and the mutual-submission discipline, the team's functional relational health matters. *"Is there anything between us right now that would interfere with our holding this gathering together? Is there anything either of us is carrying from outside the body that we need to name before we enter the work?"* If material surfaces, the team handles it briefly — either clears it sufficient for the gathering or, if the material is substantial, the team postpones the gathering or substitutes single-Companion deployment with the V2.Exp8 refined fallback. The pre-gathering relational check is non-negotiable for the team-structure to operate per its discipline.

• *Pray together.* Brief Companion-team prayer naming the gathering's work to the Lord; asking the Spirit's leading for the team's holding of the body. The prayer is operational, not ceremonial — the Companion-team's posture is the Lord's-work-mediated-through-the-team-as-faculty, per the V2.Exp8B source-reversal core instance applied at the Companion-team level.

**Companion discernment.** The pre-gathering preparation is operationally constitutive of the team-structure's effectiveness. Skipping it ("we'll just figure it out as we go") substantially degrades the team's capacity to hold the gathering; the discipline is to do the preparation even when it feels redundant. As the Companion-team's working relationship matures, the preparation may shorten (5–10 minutes for an established team in a familiar body) but does not skip; the relational check in particular operates every gathering, since material accumulates between gatherings.

**Phase 2 — During-gathering team-structure operation**

**Front Companion work:** The front Companion runs the protocol's substantive moves with the participant. Within the gathering, the front Companion:

• *Holds the center of the work.* Carpet-work moves, Triangle deployments, protocol-specific framing language, participant-Companion direct engagement — all front Companion's territory.

• *Tracks the participant's state.* Where the participant is in the work; what's surfacing; what the next move should be.

• *Stays attentive to the back Companion's signals.* The front Companion knows the back is tracking the bigger picture and will signal if the front needs to step back. The front's posture toward the back is *receiving the back's discernment as gift*, not as critique.

• *Yields to the back Companion's call when it comes.* If the back signals (verbally, with a pre-agreed gesture, or by stepping in directly), the front steps back. The discipline is non-negotiable per Bilgere's framing: *"if my back facilitator says I'm in my stuff, I step back even when I'm sure they're wrong. AND IT'S ALWAYS WORKED OUT FINE."*

**Back Companion work:** The back Companion stays at the edge of the work and serves the bigger picture. Within the gathering, the back Companion:

• *Tracks the group's mood and energy.* Where are the other participants relative to what's happening on the carpet? Who's engaged; who's drifting; who's being triggered by what's surfacing; who needs attention later.

• *Tracks the front Companion's state.* Is the front operating cleanly? Is the front getting tangled in their own stuff? Is the front about to make a move that the back's discernment suggests would not serve?

• *Tracks the participant's longer-arc state.* The front is in close with the participant's immediate work; the back tracks the participant's broader formation arc — where the work fits in the participant's longer journey, what may be the next move after this session, what referrals or supplementary work may be needed.

• *Channels group input.* Group members route ideas, observations, or concerns through the back Companion, who decides whether to bring them to the front. The back is the group's hearing-channel into the work-component without disrupting the front Companion's direct engagement with the participant.

• *Calls the front Companion when needed.* When the back's discernment surfaces a concern — the front is about to deploy a protocol the back judges premature; the front appears to be operating from their own stuff; the work is heading into territory beyond the front Companion's competency for this work; a participant elsewhere in the group needs immediate attention — the back signals or steps in. The call is the back's discernment; the front yields per the team-structure discipline.

**Group-member work:** Group members participate in the work as the protocol calls (holding role-played figures; offering corporate witness at amplification moments; per the specific carpet-work protocol's structure). When group members have observations, questions, or concerns during the work, they route to the back Companion rather than directly to the front. The discipline preserves the front Companion's direct engagement with the participant while keeping the group's discernment channel open.

**Companion-team discernment.** The during-gathering operation is the team-structure's primary work. Specific discernment points:

• *When the back should call.* The back's calling judgment matures with practice. Early in the team's working relationship, the back may err toward calling more often than needed; the front's discipline is to yield even then. As the team matures, the back's calling becomes more discerning — more selective, more accurate.

• *How the back calls.* A pre-agreed signal (eye-contact + slight nod toward the door; a specific phrase like *"let me check in"*; a tap on the front Companion's shoulder) preserves the work's flow while transferring the front role. A direct verbal step-in ("can I take this for a moment") works when the moment warrants more direct intervention.

• *What happens when the back steps in.* The front steps back literally — moves to the edge; lets the back work in close. The front does not leave the gathering; the front remains present and may resume the front role later in the session if the back signals the front's return.

• *When both Companions need to step out.* Sometimes the discernment is that the carpet-work needs to pause — the participant is overwhelmed; the group's mood requires attention; the work has reached the edge of either Companion's competency. The team-structure's discipline includes the move of *closing the work-component cleanly* and routing the participant's continued work to subsequent gatherings or to outside care.

**Phase 3 — Post-gathering Companion-team debrief (15–45 minutes after gathering)**

**Companion-team work:** The front and back Companions meet briefly after the gathering closes to:

• *Review what surfaced for each Companion.* What the front saw and felt during the work; what the back saw and tracked from the edge; what each Companion is carrying out of the gathering.

• *Process any back-to-front signal moves.* If the back called the front during the gathering, the team processes what surfaced — what the back saw; what the front was doing; what the team learned. The debrief is non-evaluative; the discipline is learning together rather than critiquing.

• *Note follow-up for specific participants.* What individual participants may need going forward — Companion II / III check-in; routing to supplementary work; pastoral care or clinical referral; specific carpet-work that may be appropriate in subsequent sessions.

• *Pray together briefly.* Closing prayer naming what the Lord did in the gathering; committing the participants to the Lord; receiving the team's holding of the work back into the Lord's hands.

**Companion-team discernment.** The post-gathering debrief is where the team-structure's competency develops most substantially. The discipline is to do the debrief consistently even when it feels brief or obvious; the patterns the team learns to see through repeated debriefs are what mature the team's holding-capacity across multiple gatherings. Skipping the debrief degrades the team-structure's developmental trajectory.

## Companion notes
**On the front-yields-to-back discipline as ****core****.** The Bilgere discipline — *"if my back facilitator says I'm in my stuff, I step back even when I'm sure they're wrong"* — is the operational core of B13. Without it, the back position is decorative; the front Companion can dismiss the back's discernment and the team-structure produces no operational benefit over single-Companion deployment. The discipline is *non-negotiable for B13 deployment*: a Companion who is not committed to yielding to the back Companion's call should not deploy B13. The discipline is theologically grounded in Eph 5:21 mutual-submission and is operationally what makes the team-structure work.

**On the team-structure's relational health requirement.** B13 requires functional relational health between the front and back Companions. Where unresolved material exists — long-standing disagreement; recent rupture; competitive dynamics; unaddressed feedback — the team-structure cannot operate per its discipline. The pre-gathering relational check is what surfaces unresolved material; the team's handling of the material before the gathering is what makes B13 deployable. Where the material exceeds what the pre-gathering check can clear, the team postpones the gathering, substitutes single-Companion deployment, or routes the relational material to its own subsequent processing. The team-structure cannot operate as an end-around for unresolved Companion-team relational material.

**On the gendered framing in Bilgere's prose.** Bilgere's NWTA-lineage prose names the front-back relationship as "chain of command" with masculine-coded military framing. The core V2 adaptation substitutes "team-structure" or "co-discernment" language without losing the operational discipline. The Companion-team should use language that fits its pastoral context and the body's broader register; what matters is the front-yields-to-back discipline operating, not the specific verbal framing.

**On mixed-gender Companion teams.** B13 deployment in mixed-gender Companion teams operates per the same team-structure discipline as same-gender teams. The pastoral-pattern framing per Decision 5.1(c) does not specifically apply to B13 (no gender-specific operational guidelines in the source document at the Companion-team altitude); the team's relational health and the front-yields-to-back discipline govern. Mixed-gender Companion teams may carry additional dynamics worth the Companion-team's attention (cultural assumptions about gendered authority; participant projections onto the team; the team's own internalized assumptions about gender and leadership); these are pastoral patterns the team can attend to without core legislation.

**On Companion competency requirements.** Front Companion: Companion II minimum for general carpet-work; Companion III for high-stakes work-options (B11 bridge-to-the-Father; B15 trauma specialty; A24 image-of-God depth-work; A25 contract work). Back Companion: Companion II minimum, with the specific discernment-from-edge competency developed through observed B13 deployments. The Companion II to Companion III development trajectory specifically benefits from B13 back-position practice; the back position is structurally available as the apprenticeship position from which Companion III competency matures.

**On the team-structure's role in Companion-formation.** Beyond its operational role for the gathering it holds, B13 deployment is itself formative for the Companion-team. The mutual-submission discipline (front yielding to back's call); the relational health requirement (the pre-gathering relational check); the post-gathering debrief practice (learning together rather than separately) — all three operate as formation practices for the Companions involved. The team-structure's deepest work over time is the mutual-formation of the Companions themselves through repeated team-deployment across gatherings.

**On routing within the team-structure.** The back Companion's routing-channel function (group-member input flowing through the back to the front) requires the back's discernment about what to bring forward. Common patterns: *bring forward* — observations that would refine the front's protocol-deployment; concerns about a specific participant's state that the front may not see from in close; offers of corporate-witness when amplification is needed. *Do not bring forward* — secondary observations that would distract from the work; the group's anxiety about timing; group-member discomfort with what is surfacing if the front's discernment is that the discomfort is part of the work. The back's discernment about routing is itself a Companion II / III competency.

**On B13 as one specific form of broader Christian co-leadership patterns.** The two-Companion structure operates as one specific instance of Christian-traditional co-leadership patterns — the bishop-and-presbyter pattern; the spiritual-direction tradition's *anam cara* / soul-friend co-discernment; the Anglican two-person-pastor team structure of small-church practice; the broader Eph 5:21 mutual-submission discipline in Christian leadership generally. B13 does not innovate the broader pattern; it operationalizes the broader pattern at the Companion-team-during-carpet-work-gathering altitude. The core V2 prose names the convergence without canonizing a general structural-pattern claim.

## Materials
• **None required** beyond what the gathering's other infrastructure provides. The team-structure operates on Companion-team practice and discipline rather than on material accoutrements.

• **Pre-agreed back-to-front signal protocol** is operational rather than material. The Companion-team's agreement on how the back will signal the front (specific phrase; gesture; tap on the shoulder; eye-contact pattern) is established as part of the team's working agreement, refined through practice.

## Time
• **Pre-gathering preparation:** 15–60 minutes before gathering. Longer for new Companion teams (15–60 minutes); shorter for established teams in familiar bodies (5–15 minutes).

• **During-gathering team-structure operation:** Operates throughout the session-liturgy's deployment. No additional time beyond the session's standard duration.

• **Post-gathering debrief:** 15–45 minutes after gathering. Brief for routine gatherings; longer for substantial work-components or gatherings where the back called the front.

The team-structure's operational time-investment is roughly 30–105 minutes total per gathering on top of the session-liturgy's own duration. The investment is what makes the structure operationally beneficial; bodies considering B13 deployment should account for the Companion-team time-commitment as part of the deployment decision.
