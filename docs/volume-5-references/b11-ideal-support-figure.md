# B11 Ideal Support Figure

## What this protocol does
Ideal Support Figure is the core imaginative-figure-reparenting modality for grief-knots, tender shame-knots that are too tender for direct shadow work, and blessing-deficit material at the wound-and-false-self axis. The protocol brings a role-player into the work as an *Ideal Father / Ideal Mother / angel / color-of-light / Lord-figure* — whatever the participant can receive in this moment — and the participant works between the child position and the figure position to refine what the figure says and does until the participant can receive the support the figure offers, with the body's break into grief and acceptance.

B11 is theologically the most consequential protocol in the Cycle 4 cohort. The imaginative-figure work risks substituting an idealized human figure for the real Father whose love alone fully fills the blessing-deficit (Eph 3:14–15; Matt 11:27; John 14:9). The Christian-frame adaptation requires the *bridge-to-the-Father **core** discipline* — naming the figure throughout as therapeutic placeholder pointing to the real Father whose love is what the figure is reaching toward, not as substitute for Him. Per Decision 12.3, the bridge-to-the-Father framing is core Companion discipline; this Part VI entry supplies the runnable Companion-language script. *The reparenting is the Father's; the figure is the door through which His love enters the memory; the rest the figure offers is the Son's gift.*

## When to use it
• **For grief-knots at the wound-and-false-self axis.** When the participant carries grief from a parental wound — what was needed but not received; what was given that should not have been; the felt-sense of childhood lack the participant has carried into adult life — B11 supplies the core reparenting modality. The figure provides what was missing, in the imaginative space of the memory, with the bridge-to-the-Father framing holding the figure as door rather than as substitute.

• **For tender shame-knots that are too tender for direct shadow work.** Some shame-material is reachable through Gold/Shadow Hot Seat and the A23 Shadow and Projections track; some shame-material is too tender for direct shadow work — the participant has been carrying the shame so long, so deeply, that confrontation produces collapse rather than recovery. B11 provides operational entry for tender shame-knots by approaching through what the participant could not receive at the wound, rather than by approaching the shame directly.

• **For blessing-deficit material** — the specific case of carrying the lack-of-blessing as foundational identity-formation. Where a participant has experienced the absence of fatherly or motherly blessing as foundational to their sense of self, B11 supplies the imaginative-figure work that brings into the memory what was missing. When the work reaches the image-of-God depth (the participant's experience of fatherly or motherly blessing-deficit is shaping their experience of the heavenly Father), routing to A24 Blessing Receiving may be appropriate — B11 operates at the human-figure-reparenting altitude; A24 operates at the image-of-God depth-altitude.

• **For reparenting work generally** in the corpus's broader formation territory. The Christian-frame reparenting tradition (Eldredge's *Wild at Heart*; the broader inner-healing-prayer tradition; the contemporary Christian-inner-healing literature) supplies adjacent theological grounding for B11; the protocol is the corpus's core operational form of reparenting work at the carpet-work modality.

• **At Companion II competency for general deployment;** Companion III for high-stakes deployments where the blessing-deficit material is foundational and the bridge-to-the-Father discipline must hold across substantial work time.

## When NOT to use it
• **When anger work is what is needed.** Some grief is held by anger that has not yet been allowed; B11's reparenting work bypasses anger that needs its own time. The Companion's discernment: if the participant carries unaddressed legitimate anger at the wound-figure, A17 Healing and Forgiveness or A12 Splitting a Role may need to deploy first; B11 may follow after anger work has cleared the way for reparenting work.

• **When the bridge-to-the-Father framing cannot be held.** Where the Companion is not committed to the bridge-to-the-Father discipline, or where the participant's theological context cannot receive the bridge framing, B11 should not deploy. The protocol's theological load is non-trivial; without the bridge, B11 substitutes an idealized human figure for the real Father whose love alone fully fills the blessing-deficit. The Companion's pre-deployment discernment governs.

• **When direct prayer for the Father's presence is operationally available and the participant can receive it.** Some participants whose grief or blessing-deficit material is reachable through direct prayer for the Father's presence in the memory do not need the imaginative-figure intermediary. The Companion's discernment about whether the imaginative-figure intermediary is what the participant needs governs deployment. B11's value is specifically for participants who cannot yet directly receive the Father's love in the wound; the figure-as-placeholder is what they can receive.

• **For shame-material that needs direct shadow work.** Where the shame-knot is reachable through Gold/Shadow Hot Seat or A23 Shadow and Projections, the direct work may be more operationally effective than B11's reparenting. B11 is specifically for tender shame-material that direct work would collapse; the Companion's discernment distinguishes.

• **In contexts where the participant's blessing-deficit material is being weaponized.** Some participants have learned reparenting practices in secular or non-Christian-grounded contexts (Pesso-Boyden therapy; Hellinger constellations; popular-psychology inner-child work) and carry philosophical commitments about self-reparenting that the bridge-to-the-Father core discipline directly addresses. B11 deployment with such participants requires the bridge framing to be operationally clear and substantive; participants whose prior reparenting work has been entirely self-source-grounded may need framing-development before B11 deploys cleanly.

## The protocol
B11 runs across five operational moves, typically 60–90 minutes for a complete deployment. The protocol may operate as standalone work-component or as a Move 6 routing from B14 Kinesthetic Metaphor when the somatic-first work surfaces absence-as-wound material.

**Move 1 — Establish the participant in the wound-memory (10–15 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"We're going to bring you into the place where the wound lives — the memory or season where what you needed wasn't there. Let your body find the place. Not the answer your mind would give — where does the body show you the wound is from? How young are you in this place? What does the moment look like?"*

**Participant work:** Locate the wound-memory. The participant identifies the age, the scene, the specific moment of lack. *"I'm seven; I'm coming home from school and no one is there"; "I'm five and Dad is yelling at Mom and I'm alone in the bedroom"; "I'm twelve and I'm bringing my report card home and Dad just nods without looking up"; "I don't know what age — it's the felt sense of every time Mom was emotionally somewhere else"*. The specificity matters because the reparenting work happens in the memory's specific moment; vague generalizations do not give the figure-work somewhere to land.

**Companion framing for the Spirit-mediation:** *"Before we bring in the figure, let me name what we're doing. The Lord is here in this memory now — He was here when it happened, and He's here now. He's not absent from the wound; He's been there throughout. What we're going to do is bring in a figure that lets your child-self receive what the Father has been wanting to give you all along. The figure is not the Father — the figure is the door through which the Father's love enters this memory. Are you ready?"*

**Companion discernment.** Move 1 establishes both the wound-memory and the theological framing. The Companion's framing is operationally essential — without explicitly naming the bridge-to-the-Father before the figure is brought in, the protocol is more likely to drift into figure-as-substitute territory during the work. The framing language above is core; the specific wording may vary, but the substantive content (the Father is here; the figure is the door, not the Father; the work is the Father's love entering the memory) is non-negotiable.

If the participant's wound-memory carries surfaces that would route elsewhere (active flashback territory; sin-channel material; trauma intensity exceeding Companion competence), the Companion routes per the routing logic before continuing with B11. The reparenting work depends on stable participant access to the memory; unstable access requires its own work first.

**Move 2 — Identify what the participant could not receive at the wound (10–15 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"In this place, with this child-self, what is the thing that wasn't there that should have been? What did you need that you couldn't get? What would have changed everything if it had been there?"*

**Participant work:** Name what was missing. *"Someone to see me; to know I existed"; "to be told I mattered, that I was good, that I belonged"; "for someone to come in and stop the yelling; to make me safe"; "to be held without it being asked for, just because"; "to hear that I was a delight, that I brought joy, that I was wanted"*. The participant names the specific lack — the substantive content of the blessing that was missing.

**Companion discernment.** This move is operationally what the figure-work needs to address. The participant's naming supplies the content the figure will offer; without the naming, the figure's offering is generic and operationally weaker. The Companion may pace the move — sometimes the participant surfaces the missing-content immediately; sometimes the participant needs sustained attending to the wound-memory before the content becomes clear. The Companion's discipline is to wait for the participant's specific naming rather than to supply suggestions.

If multiple lacks surface, the Companion supports the participant in naming the most foundational — the one that, if it had been given, would have changed the most about the child-self's experience. The figure-work proceeds from the foundational lack.

**Move 3 — Bring the figure into the scene (5–10 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Now we'll bring in a figure that can offer what was missing. What kind of figure can your child-self receive right now? It might be an ideal father; ideal mother; an angel; a color of light; the Lord Himself if your child-self can take Him in directly. What can the child-self in this memory receive?"*

**Participant work:** Identify what figure the child-self can receive. The choice may surprise the participant — sometimes the obvious figure (the Lord directly) is not what the child-self can take in; sometimes a less direct figure (an ideal grandparent figure; a guardian angel; a being-of-warm-light) is what the child-self can let close enough to receive the offering. The participant's child-self governs the choice rather than the participant's adult theology.

**Companion framing for the bridge:** *"Whatever figure your child-self can receive — that figure is the door through which the Father's love enters this memory. The Father has been wanting to give your child-self what was missing for as long as your child-self has been carrying the lack. The figure is the door through which He gives it now. The figure isn't the Father, but what the figure offers is what the Father is giving. Who will you ask to hold the figure?"*

**Participant work:** The participant chooses a group member (in group-mode deployment) or imaginatively-instantiates the figure (in dyadic deployment without role-players). The chosen role-player is briefed: *"You'll hold the figure of [Ideal Father / Ideal Mother / angel / Lord-figure / etc.] for [participant]. You'll offer what [participant] tells you the figure says and does. You're not channeling the Father; you're holding the figure as door. The Father's work is the Father's; the figure is the placeholder through which [participant]'s child-self can receive the Father's love."*

**Companion discernment.** The Move 3 framing carries the core bridge-to-the-Father discipline at its most operationally consequential moment. The Companion's language is structurally constitutive — without explicit naming of *the figure as door rather than as Father*, the role-player and the participant are both more likely to drift into figure-as-substitute territory during the work. The core framing is in the source-document language and in the corpus-specific Christian-frame addition; the Companion uses both.

The role-player chosen for the figure matters pastorally. Sometimes the participant chooses someone whose own theological commitments make the bridge-discipline operationally clean; sometimes the participant chooses someone for whose own healing this role may be substantive work (which can be valuable but requires the back Companion's tracking per B13). The Companion's pre-deployment discernment about which role-player can hold the figure with the bridge-discipline intact governs.

**Move 4 — Refine the figure's offering until the child-self can receive (15–30 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Now we'll let the figure offer what your child-self needs. The figure will say or do something; you, in the child-self position, will let me know whether your child-self can receive it. If your child-self can't yet receive what the figure is offering, we'll refine — sometimes the words need to be different; sometimes the way the offering is given needs to change; sometimes there's something else the figure needs to do first. Take your time. Let the work happen at the pace your child-self can receive it."*

**Participant work:** Move between the child-self position and the figure position to refine the figure's offering until the child-self can receive the support. The work has a specific shape:

• *Participant in child-self position:* The participant is in the wound-memory's specific moment. The figure (role-player) offers something — speaks the blessing the child-self needed; performs the act of presence the child-self needed; gives the comfort or attention or affirmation the child-self needed.

• *Participant attends to whether the child-self can receive.* The participant feels into whether the offering lands; whether the child-self can take it in; whether what the figure said or did reaches where the lack lives.

• *If the child-self cannot yet receive, the participant switches to the figure position.* The participant becomes the figure briefly, and from the figure position refines what needs to be said or done so the child-self can receive. *"The figure needs to say it differently — instead of 'I love you,' the figure needs to say 'I see you'"; "the figure needs to sit down beside instead of standing across"; "the figure needs to look directly at the child-self and not look away"; "the figure needs to wait before saying anything, just stay with the child-self in silence first"*.

• *The refined offering is communicated to the role-player,* who offers it again. The participant returns to the child-self position. The work iterates until the child-self can receive what the figure is offering.

The crescendo move is the child-self's break into reception. The participant's body shifts — the rigid posture of the child-self in the wound softens; the breath changes; sometimes tears come; sometimes a deep release; sometimes a quiet settling. The reception is somatic rather than purely cognitive — the participant's child-self lets the figure's offering in.

**Companion discernment.** Move 4 is the protocol's substantive work. The Companion paces the iteration — too fast loses the participant's somatic process; too slow loses the work's energy. The repeated framing throughout Move 4 — *"the figure is the door; the offering is the Father's"* — keeps the bridge discipline operational. The Companion attends to:

• *The child-self's pacing.* The work happens at the pace the child-self can receive; the Companion does not push the participant past the child-self's capacity.

• *The figure's framing.* If the role-player is drifting into channeling-the-Father territory rather than holding-the-figure-as-door territory, the Companion gently re-frames: *"Hold the figure for [participant] — the figure is the placeholder; the Father is doing the work through the figure."* The discipline is the Companion's; the back Companion (per B13) supports the discipline-maintenance.

• *The participant's child-self position.* The participant should feel in their body what the child-self is taking in; reception is somatic and felt. If the work is happening entirely cognitively ("I think the child-self could probably receive that"), the Companion brings the participant back into the embodied child-self position.

If the child-self cannot receive after extensive refinement — sometimes the offering is right but the child-self has been carrying the lack so long that reception itself is the wound; sometimes the participant's adult-theology is interfering with the child-self's somatic readiness — the Companion may pause the work and route to subsequent care. Not every B11 deployment produces full reception in a single session; some require multiple sessions across weeks or months as the participant's child-self develops capacity to receive.

**Move 5 — Bridge to the Father explicitly and close (10–20 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"What your child-self just received from the figure is what the Father has been wanting to give you. The figure was the door; the Father's love is what came through the door. Let me ask: can the child-self now receive directly from the Father what came through the figure? Is the Father here in this memory; can you let Him close enough to receive directly?"*

**Participant work:** With the child-self now able to receive the figure's offering, the work explicitly bridges to the Father. The participant attends to whether the child-self can receive the Father directly now — not as figure but as Father Himself. Sometimes the bridge is immediate (the child-self, having received through the figure, can now receive directly from the Father); sometimes the bridge requires the figure-presence as continued intermediary for a longer time before the child-self can receive the Father directly; sometimes the bridge surfaces image-of-God material that routes the work to A24 Blessing Receiving at the next session or as immediate follow-up.

The work closes with the participant's child-self resting in what was received — the body's softening; the tears; the deep release; the quiet settling. The Companion holds the closing without rushing.

**De-roling per A14 with B11-specific bridge discipline:** The role-player who held the figure de-roles with explicit bridge-language. *"You are not the Father; you held the figure for [participant] to receive what the Father gives. The Father is the Father; you are [role-player's name]."* The participant responds: *"You are not the Father; you are [role-player's name]. The Father holds Himself. Thank you for holding the figure."* The B11-specific de-roling language is non-negotiable — without explicit release of the role-player from any residual identification with the figure-of-the-Father role, the role-player may carry weight from holding the figure that needs releasing.

**Closing affirmation per A13:** The participant names what the Lord spoke in the work. The body's corporate witness amplifies per V2.Exp9. *"The reparenting is the Father's; the figure was the door; the love that entered through the door is what the Father has been giving from the beginning."* The closing-affirmation language carries the source-reversal core instance (B11's *the reparenting is the Father's*) into the closing.

**Companion discernment.** Move 5 completes the protocol's theological work. The bridge to the Father at the closing is operationally what makes B11 Christian-frame rather than secular-imaginative-figure-reparenting. Skipping the explicit bridge or rushing past it loses the protocol's substantive theological move. The Companion paces the closing carefully; the bridge to the Father is given the time it needs.

Where the work has surfaced image-of-God material that routes to A24, the Companion names the routing during Move 5 closing: *"What surfaced today opens into image-of-God territory — the Father's heart that your child-self has been reaching toward connects to your image of God in foundational ways. We can hold that for now and bring it forward to subsequent work; A24 may be the next move when the body is ready."* The routing is the Companion's discernment; the participant's continued formation arc holds the routing through subsequent gatherings.

## Companion notes
**On the bridge-to-the-Father ****core**** discipline as the protocol's substantive theological work.** The bridge is what makes B11 Christian-frame. Without the bridge, the protocol is Pesso-Boyden / Hellinger / Bradshaw imaginative-figure-reparenting under Christian vocabulary; with the bridge, the protocol operates under the corpus's source-reversal core discipline at the *reparenting is the Father's* altitude. The Companion's framing at each move maintains the bridge; the role-player's holding of the figure-as-door maintains the bridge; the de-roling explicitly releases any residual figure-as-Father identification. The discipline is operational rather than ceremonial — the Companion does not just *name* the bridge once and proceed; the Companion *maintains* the bridge throughout, in framing language, in re-orientations when the work drifts, in the closing's substantive bridge-completion.

**On the role-player's care.** The role-player who holds the figure is doing substantive work — being a vessel through which the Father's love enters another person's wound is theologically meaningful and pastorally weighty. The role-player may carry weight from the work that needs its own debrief. The Companion-team's post-gathering debrief per B13 attends to the role-player's care; in dyadic deployments without role-players, the Companion's care of themselves through the work attends to the same dynamics.

**On the participant's child-self capacity for reception.** Some participants whose blessing-deficit has been foundational and long-carried may need multiple B11 deployments across weeks or months before full reception is somatically available. The first deployment may produce partial reception; subsequent deployments deepen what was begun. The Companion's framing does not demand full reception in a single session; the work happens at the pace the child-self can receive.

**On the source-reversal ****core**** instance.** B11 joins the source-reversal core series at the ninth core instance: *the reparenting is the Father's*. The verbal parallelism extends across the eleven-instance series: *the affirmation is the Lord's / the mission is the Lord's / the resonance is the Lord's / the blessing is the Lord's / the renunciation is in Jesus's name / the attending is the Spirit's / the session is the Lord's / the clearing is the Lord's / the reparenting is the Father's / the body's hearing is the Spirit's / the redemption is Christ's.* The source-reversal at B11 is theologically the most consequential of the cohort's three new instances because the figure-as-substitute risk would directly compromise the source-reversal at the protocol level; the bridge-to-the-Father discipline is what makes the reversal land.

**On routing to A24 Blessing Receiving.** B11 operates at the human-figure-reparenting altitude; A24 operates at the image-of-God depth-altitude. The two protocols share theological territory but address different levels of the same wound. Where B11 surfaces blessing-deficit material that reaches image-of-God depth — the participant's experience of the missing fatherly or motherly blessing is shaping their experience of God — A24 may deploy as subsequent work in the participant's formation arc. The Companion holds B11 as the human-figure-reparenting protocol and A24 as the image-of-God depth-protocol; the two operate sequentially rather than substituting for each other.

**On the corpus's broader father-wound / blessing-deficit territory.** B11 sits within the corpus's broader engagement with father-wound and blessing-deficit material (A24 Blessing Receiving; the Eldredge / *Wild at Heart* lineage; the broader Anglican-evangelical men's-work tradition; the inner-healing-prayer tradition). B11 is the carpet-work modality form of this broader territory; A24 is the image-of-God depth-form; A21 Say Your Mission and A26 Tell Your Story carry related territory at different altitudes. The corpus's father-wound / blessing-deficit work operates across multiple protocols; B11 is one specific operational form.

**On secular reparenting traditions the corpus does not endorse.** Pesso-Boyden System Psychomotor, Hellinger Family Constellations, and the broader popular-psychology inner-child literature (Bradshaw, Whitfield) carry philosophical commitments the corpus does not import — Pesso-Boyden's developmental-deficit framework as primary; Hellinger's family-systems metaphysics; the inner-child literature's self-reparenting locus. B11's operational pattern is preserved from these traditions; the philosophical anthropology is set aside under V2.Exp0 Tool Import Discipline. The Christian-frame adaptation operates on outside-the-self-locus-of-healing grounding; the participant does not self-reparent; the Father reparents through the figure-as-door.

## Materials
• **Space appropriate to carpet-work session** — per the standard carpet-work container.

• **Role-player for the figure** — group-mode deployment recruits the role-player from the gathering's participants. Dyadic deployment without role-player operates with the participant imaginatively-instantiating the figure (sustainable but operationally lighter than role-player deployment).

• **Materials specific to the wound-memory's scene** — sometimes the participant's somatic reading surfaces a specific physical detail (a chair to sit in; a particular position; a specific object) that supports the work. The Companion accommodates within the gathering's available materials.

## Time
• **Standard B11 deployment:** 60–90 minutes total. Allocation: Move 1 (wound-memory) 10–15 min; Move 2 (lack-naming) 10–15 min; Move 3 (figure introduction) 5–10 min; Move 4 (figure refinement) 15–30 min; Move 5 (bridge and closing) 10–20 min.

• **Compressed B11 deployment:** 45–60 minutes for lighter wound-material or follow-up sessions building on prior B11 work. The structural moves all operate; the pacing within Move 4 compresses.

• **Extended B11 deployment:** 90+ minutes for foundational blessing-deficit material where the child-self's reception capacity requires sustained refinement work. Some B11 deployments naturally extend to two-hour-plus sessions when the work is foundational.
