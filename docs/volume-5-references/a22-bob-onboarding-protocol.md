# A22 BoB Onboarding Protocol

## BoB Onboarding Protocol (V2.Exp8 — Container, with cross-refs to V2.Exp9, V2.Exp9A, V2.Exp5, V2.Exp10)
## What this protocol does
A22 is the core IJH operational protocol for how a new member is invited into a BoB / FotH group, what is named with them before commitment, and how the first three meetings let both the candidate and the existing body discern fit before commitment is formalized. The protocol's load-bearing function in the corpus is **integration**, A22 is the core bridge between the new member's first arrival and the corpus's full developmental repertoire, naming what the new member is being invited into and how the corpus's tools and practices come into the new member's life across the first weeks, months, and years.

The protocol's primary downstream effect is V2.Exp8 container-formation at the level of new-member integration without disruption of the existing container. Secondary downstream effects are V2.Exp9 amplification capacity preservation (the existing body's amplification work depends on member-level trust that the onboarding establishes); V2.Exp9A corporate-witnessed identity work begun (meeting 1's Who Are You per A27); V2.Exp5 mission articulation begun (meeting 2's mission clarity per A31); V2.Exp10 training-plan upstream-conditions established.

**When to use it**

• **When a new candidate is being considered for membership in a BoB / FotH group.** The core deployment context — *the candidate has expressed interest, an existing member has surfaced their name to the body, the body has begun discerning fit, and the pre-membership discussion is about to begin*. A22 governs from the pre-membership conversation through the third meeting's commitment-or-opt-out.

• **When the existing body has capacity for new-member integration.** Adding a new member is non-trivial work for the existing body; the body should have container conditions sufficient to hold both its current life and the new-member discernment. Companions discern whether the body is in a state where onboarding is workable.

• **Level II Companion competency for full deployment of the inviter role.** The inviter's discernment-of-fit, the count-the-cost framing of the deal-points conversation, the V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices work on both sides, and the holding of opt-out as live possibility throughout — all are Level II competencies that develop with reps.

• **Level I Companion competency for participation in the three meetings as a member of the existing body.** The body members who participate in the candidate's meetings need V2.Exp8 container-discipline competency and the cohort-B protocol competencies the meetings deploy (A26, A27, V2.Exp8, A1/A2, A31).

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

• **When the existing body is in container crisis.** If the body's container conditions per V2.Exp8 refined are not currently sufficient (recent interpersonal rupture among members; loss of focused attention; confidentiality breach), the body is not in a state to onboard. Restore the body first; onboarding follows.

• **When the candidate is in acute crisis.** A candidate in fresh trauma, active psychiatric instability, or major life-rupture state may not be in a place where BoB membership can land as formation rather than as additional load. The deal-points conversation should surface this honestly; deferral to a later season is sometimes the right move, with pastoral support in the interim.

• **When the inviter has not done sufficient discernment of fit before the pre-membership conversation begins.** The conversation itself is a discernment event, but the inviter should come into the conversation with prior discernment work done. A candidate brought into the conversation without prior discernment becomes the body's discernment burden in ways that compromise the conversation's integrity.

• **When the body's discernment process is implicitly biased toward acceptance.** The opt-out structure is constitutive only if both-sides opt-out is live possibility throughout. Bodies that have implicitly decided to accept the candidate before the three meetings begin produce performative onboarding rather than discernment. The Companion's discipline is to hold opt-out as live possibility for the body, not just for the candidate.

• **When the pre-membership conversation drifts toward salesmanship.** The deal-points are *named honestly* — including the costs. A conversation that overstates the gifts and understates the costs produces commitment on partial information; the new member discovers the costs they were not told and the commitment frays. Hold the deal-points honestly.

• **When the candidate is invited primarily because the body needs a new member rather than because the candidate is a fit.** Recruitment-driven onboarding undercuts the discernment. The body's discernment of its own state and need is upstream of the candidate-discernment.

• **Running the three meetings without honoring the opt-out close.** If the third meeting does not include the explicit invitation-or-opt-out conversation, the structure has been compressed and the discernment is incomplete. The opt-out conversation is the close that makes the prior meetings serve their function.

**The protocol — pre-membership discussion**

The pre-membership conversation is the operational front of the onboarding. Typically one-on-one between the inviter (usually a current member, sometimes the Companion) and the candidate; sometimes with the Companion present as additional witness. Approximately 60–90 minutes.

1. **Opening: orientation.**

- Inviter: *"[Candidate], I want to walk you through what membership in this Band of Brothers actually involves before we go further. There are six things I want to name with you so your discernment can be informed. After we talk through these, we'll see if you want to take the next step — which would be coming to three meetings before committing — or if this isn't the right fit for you. We may also discern that it's not the right fit. The opt-out is real on both sides."*

- Candidate: receive the framing.

2. **Deal-point 1: Not drop-in.**

- Inviter: *"This is committed membership, not drop-in attendance. The body forms through meeting together — Hebrews 10 says don't neglect the meeting together — and what we do as a group depends on every member being present in a way that supports the body's life. Missing meetings happens occasionally for real reasons, but pattern-of-attendance matters. Are you in a place where you can commit to consistent attendance?"*

- Candidate: respond from where they are.

3. **Deal-point 2: Not accountability/head-knowledge focus.**

- Inviter: *"We are not a Bible study group. We are not an accountability group with check-ins on whether you read your morning devotional. We are doing heart work — what God is showing you in your life, where you are being formed, what you are hearing from the Spirit. If you're looking for accountability or Bible study, those are good things, but they're not what this is."*

- Candidate: respond.

4. **Deal-point 3: Mission of leading other men.**

- Inviter: *"The body's mission is to develop men who can lead other men into formation. You're being invited into a body whose mission is outward and developmental, not inward and self-maintaining. The work we do **together is in service of you becoming someone who leads other men into the kind of formation we're doing. Is that something you're open to?"*

- Candidate: respond.

5. **Deal-point 4: Distinguishing head from heart knowledge.**

- Inviter: *"We work in heart-knowledge territory rather than head-knowledge territory. A man who can articulate the doctrine of justification but can't articulate where the Spirit is at work in his own life this week is at head-level only. The body's work is at the level of what you're hearing and being formed by, not at the level of doctrinal content you can recite."*

- Candidate: respond.

6. **Deal-point 5: Deep trust requirement.**

- Inviter: *"Deep trust is required, and it will be built across our time together. The body cannot do what it does without trust; the trust is real and earned over time. You'll be invited into vulnerability over time — telling your story, working with wounds, hearing what the body sees in you. The trust has to be real."*

- Candidate: respond.

7. **Deal-point 6: Frothy edge of creativity.**

- Inviter: *"This is BoB-internal idiom — the frothy edge of creativity is where formation actually happens. Where you are uncomfortable enough to be growing, where the Spirit's movement is visible in your life, where the body is producing real change rather than maintaining a comfortable Christian club. This is where we operate. Are you in a place where you can step into that?"*

- Candidate: respond.

8. **Mutual discernment.**

- Inviter: ask the discernment question explicitly. *"Based on what we've talked through, what is the Lord showing you about whether this is right for you now?"*

- Candidate: respond from prayer-and-discernment, ideally; if the candidate is uncertain, propose a brief period (a few days to two weeks) for prayer before the next step.

- Inviter: share their own discernment honestly. *"What I'm hearing is X. What I want you to know about my discernment is Y."* Honest naming of the inviter's own discernment serves the candidate's discernment.

9. **Close: next step.**

- If both sides are clear that the next step is the three meetings: confirm the date for meeting 1, name what to expect, name who else will be present.

- If the candidate or the inviter discerns this is not the right time or fit: name the discernment honestly; honor the candidate's process; offer continued relationship in whatever form fits.

- If the candidate needs prayer time before deciding on the next step: name the deferral period; arrange the follow-up conversation.

## The protocol — three meetings
The three-meeting structure follows the pre-membership discussion. Each meeting is a full meeting of the existing body; the candidate participates as a structured-discernment participant rather than as a member. Opt-out is live possibility throughout.

**Meeting 1: Introduction to confidentiality, story-telling, identity.**

1. **Confidentiality framing.** Companion or designated body member names the confidentiality discipline to the candidate explicitly. *"[Candidate], the body operates under strict confidentiality. What is said in this **room stays in this room. Including by you, beginning tonight, regardless of what discernment happens at the end of the three meetings."* The candidate confirms understanding.

2. **Story-telling (A26).** The candidate tells some of their story per the A26 Tell Your Story protocol — typically the four-question framework (Footprints of God, wounds, battles, victories). Body members listen as witnesses per A26's framing. The body's first substantive encounter with the candidate happens through their story.

3. **Who Are You (A27 — Mission-Arc family).** The body runs the five-question Who Are You trio sequence with the candidate as responder. Existing body members serve as questioner and recorder; the candidate moves through the questions. By the end of the sequence, the candidate has begun the work of identity-discovery and the body has received their first articulation of who they are and where they are going.

4. **Meeting close.** Standard V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-closing for the session. The candidate may stay for any post-meeting fellowship if appropriate; debriefing of the meeting between the body and the inviter typically happens after the candidate has left.

**Meeting 2: Container conditions, clearings, mission clarity.**

1. **Container conditions framing.** Companion names the four container conditions per V2.Exp8 refined (Claim 5) explicitly for the candidate. *"[Candidate], the body operates under four conditions — confidentiality, willingness, focused attention, and formally-marked opening and closing of the work. Last week you experienced the confidentiality piece. Tonight you'll see how the other three operate."*

2. **Clearings (A1 / A2).** The body runs whatever clearings are needed at the meeting's opening per A1 / A2. The candidate observes how interpersonal friction among members is surfaced and addressed at the meeting's start. The body's discipline of clearing-before-substantive-work is operationally visible.

3. **Mission clarity (A31 — Mission-Arc family).** The body deploys A31 Mission Stretch Clarity with the candidate. The Vision and Action elements developed in meeting 1's Who Are You provide upstream material; meeting 2 deepens the Vision+Action=Mission articulation work. The Stretch step's full deployment is deferred to later in the candidate's developmental arc; meeting 2 introduces the clarity work without pressing for in-the-moment commitment.

4. **Meeting close.** Standard V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-closing. Debriefing as in meeting 1.

**Meeting 3: Do whatever we're working on, with opt-out.**

1. **Opening framing.** Companion names the meeting's structure for the candidate. *"[Candidate], tonight is the third meeting. We're going to do whatever we're working on as a body — whichever member is at the center of the work, whichever protocol fits the moment. You participate as a body member for tonight. After the meeting, you and I (and the body) will discern whether the next step is formal commitment. The opt-out is real on both sides."*

2. **The body's substantive work.** The body does whatever it would have done this night if the candidate were not present. The work that happens depends on what is current in the body — possibly a Carpet-Style session (A17 or A23), possibly Tell-Show Release work (A18, A19, or A20), possibly Hot Seat (A32), possibly What's at Risk (A33), possibly PROAPT heart Bible study (A28), possibly Mission-Arc work for a current member, possibly something else. The candidate participates as a body member, contributing what they contribute, observing what they observe.

3. **Meeting close.** Standard V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-closing.

4. **Invitation-or-opt-out conversation.** Typically after the meeting, sometimes within the meeting if the body's pattern supports it. The candidate's discernment and the body's discernment are both surfaced. If both are clear and the Spirit confirms, the candidate is invited into formal membership; the invitation is received as faith-action per Hebrews 11:1. If either side discerns this is not the right fit, the opt-out is honored honestly; the relationship continues in whatever form fits, but membership does not extend.

## Closing and transition
The protocol closes at the invitation-or-opt-out conversation. If commitment is extended and received, the new member enters the body's ongoing life from the next meeting forward. There is no further specific onboarding-protocol structure; the cohort-B and adjacent protocols come into the new member's life across subsequent months and years per Companion discernment. See the Companion notes below for typical-pattern sequencing.

If opt-out is exercised (by either side), the relationship continues in whatever form is appropriate — friendship, occasional connection, possible re-invitation in a different season — but the new-member integration does not happen. The candidate's experience of the three meetings is honored as formation in itself, regardless of the commitment outcome.

### Companion notes
A22 is the corpus's entry-point protocol. New Companions should expect to learn it through reps as both a body member (participating in onboardings) and as inviter (after Level II competency is established). The inviter role specifically is high-leverage — what happens in the pre-membership conversation shapes both the candidate's discernment and the body's discernment for the entire three-meeting structure that follows. A pre-membership conversation done well produces three meetings that operate cleanly; a pre-membership conversation done badly produces three meetings that struggle to recover their discernment function.

The deal-points conversation requires honest naming of the costs. The impulse to overstate the gifts and understate the costs is real, especially with candidates who are not initially leaning toward commitment. Resist the impulse. The candidate's discernment depends on honest information; commitments made on partial information produce members who discover the costs they were not told. Hold the deal-points conversation as count-the-cost discipline per Luke 14:27–33.

The opt-out as live possibility throughout the three meetings is the discipline that makes the discernment real on both sides. Body members who have implicitly decided to accept the candidate before the meetings begin compromise the discernment; the body's discipline is to hold the body's own opt-out as live possibility, not just the candidate's. The Companion's role through the meetings is to surface the body's discernment honestly — including discernments toward opt-out — without pressing toward any particular outcome.

The cohort-B integration sequencing question (Founder-attention flag 1 in the Phase 2 entry) lands here in the Companion notes per recommended option c. Typical-pattern guidance for when each protocol enters a new member's life:

• **Story-telling (A26):** Begins meeting 1; continues across the member's first year as more of their story gets told.

• **Identity discovery (A27 Mission-Arc family):** Begins meeting 1; deepens across the first months as the five questions mature.

• **Mission articulation (A31 Mission-Arc family):** Begins meeting 2; full Stretch step deployment typically within first six months.

• **Shadow Mission (A30 Mission-Arc family):** Introduced typically 3–6 months in, once initial mission articulation has matured enough for shadow-mission identification to operate cleanly.

• **PROAPT scripture engagement (A28):** Introduced shortly after commitment; becomes part of member's ongoing rhythm.

• **Any Doubts (A29):** Introduced as Apply-step impressions surface; sometimes meeting 4 or 5, sometimes later, per Companion discernment.

• **Hot Seat (A32):** Substantial periodic focused-attention work; introduced typically within first year. Mini Hot Seat may deploy earlier.

• **What's at Risk (A33):** Introduced when specific mission-decision hesitation surfaces; deployment-frequency varies.

• **Clearings (A1 / A2):** Ongoing from meeting 2 forward as interpersonal frictions surface.

• **Carpet-Style family (A17 / A23):** Introduced when wound or shadow work is needed; sometimes early, often later in the developmental arc.

• **Tell-Show Release family (A18 / A19 / A20):** Deployed as blockages surface; varies per member.

• **Affirmation (A13):** Used in closing-affirmation work throughout member's life.

This sequencing is *typical pattern*, not core prescription. Companion discernment determines deployment timing based on the member's actual developmental movement and what the body is hearing about what the Lord is doing in them.

Two FotH workshopping notes for A22's adaptation:

**For women's-FotH facilitation:** practitioners report that the deal-points conversation operates well with substantive verbal adaptation. The substantive principles transfer — committed membership, heart-work focus, outward-developmental mission, head-vs-heart knowledge distinction, deep trust, formation-edge — but the specific wordings and the *frothy edge of creativity* idiom in particular may land differently. Some women's-FotH groups have developed alternative language for the sixth deal-point; the practice is ongoing. The three-meeting structure transfers without modification; meeting 1 / 2 / 3 work substantially the same in women's-FotH context. The mountain-climbing metaphor (CtR-specific) does not generally transfer; women's-FotH groups typically develop their own group-mission articulation per A16 work.

**For mixed-gender-FotH facilitation:** practitioners report similar verbal adaptations to women's-FotH, with additional attention to the body-life dynamics across genders in the pre-membership conversation. The mission-of-leading-other-men deal-point (#3) requires substantive adaptation — the principle (the body's mission is outward and developmental) transfers, but the gendered specifics (leading *men*) need re-articulation as *leading others* or *leading those the Lord brings* or some other formulation that fits the mixed-gender group's actual mission. The three-meeting structure transfers with modifications around cross-gender vulnerability sequencing — particularly meeting 1's story-telling work; some mixed-gender-FotH groups slow the trust-development cadence in early meetings.

**One specific FotH workshopping note** about the deal-points conversation generally: practitioners across both women's-FotH and mixed-gender-FotH report that the deal-points serve well when held as *principles to be re-articulated for the specific group's context* rather than as *fixed phrasing to be transferred verbatim*. The BoB-core phrasing is the historical form; FotH groups develop their own phrasing of the same substantive principles. The Companion's discipline is to hold the principles while adapting the phrasing.

The §5.1(c) workshopping posture means: these adaptations are practitioner-reports from active FotH practice, held provisionally; the core BoB form is what is documented above; subsequent revisions to this Part VI entry may update the workshopping guidance as the FotH practice matures.
