# A33 What's at Risk Protocol

## What's at Risk Protocol (V2.Exp5 — Connect with Mission, with cross-refs to V2.Exp6, V2.Exp3, V2.Exp7A)
## What this protocol does
What's at Risk is the core IJH structured mission-decision discernment protocol. It addresses *the man hesitating at the edge of his mission, going back and forth, giving excuses*. The participant is placed in a structured isolated position (BoB form: standing on the floor isolated from group; FotH-adapted forms vary — see Companion notes); a scribe in the group writes verbatim what the participant says; the Companion drives the participant down both paths of the mission decision (the path of doing the thing; the path of not-doing the thing) with the *"what's at risk for you if you ___?"* question; each path is driven to its end; the load-bearing pastoral move (the Imagined-vs-Known step) names which path is *known by experience* and which is *imagined by fear*, inviting the participant to take a risk on testing the imagined.

The protocol's primary downstream effect is mission-decision clarification at the level of *what is actually at stake on both paths*. The secondary downstream effects are V2.Exp3 lie-clearing at forward-looking-fear level (the Imagined-vs-Known move operationalizes V2.Exp3's lie-identification applied to imagined-future-consequences), V2.Exp6 fear-knot work (A33 sits with A7 Counter-Intention in fear-knot territory but does different work), and V2.Exp5 mission-action movement (when the protocol completes well, the participant moves from hesitation to action on what the Lord has shown).

**Scripture-for-meditation:** 1 Corinthians 3:10–15 (the wood/hay/straw vs. gold/silver/precious-stones passage) is core per C12 source (a). The eschatological framing — *what is built will be revealed by fire* — keeps the protocol from drifting into self-as-arbiter-of-mission territory; the participant's discernment is provisional, and the final revelation is the Lord's.

**When to use it**

• **When a participant has heard their mission (or a specific mission-action step) and is hesitating at the edge of acting on it.** The core deployment context — *the man going back and forth, giving excuses, unable to move*. The mission is real; the hesitation is real; A33 is the structured form for working with the hesitation.

• **For specific mission-actions surfaced through the Mission-Arc family.** A31 (Mission Stretch Clarity) Stretch step sometimes surfaces a specific stretch-action that the participant agrees to in principle but cannot commit to operationally. A33 is a frequent next-move tool in this case — the both-paths work on the specific stretch-action clarifies what is actually at stake.

• **When fear-rooted hesitation is the primary blockage.** A33 operates well on fear-rooted hesitation that is forward-looking. For past-wound-rooted hesitation, route upstream to A17 family or other wound-work first. For sin-rooted hesitation (the participant is hesitating because the path of doing would require confession or repentance they are avoiding), route to V2.Exp4 / A6 first.

• **Level I Companion competency for the basic both-paths structure.** The structural moves (set up the isolated position, run the scribe, ask the *"what's at risk?"* question on each path, drive each path to its end) are operationally simple after a few reps.

• **Level II Companion competency for the Imagined-vs-Known move deployment.** The V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices competency required to distinguish imagination-rooted fear from Spirit-rooted prudence matures with reps. New Companions can deploy the move with the explicit understanding that the discernment will deepen; Companion II maturity is required for high-care contexts.

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

• **When the participant's hesitation is rooted in past-wound material the participant has not engaged.** A33 surfaces what is at stake on both paths; if the participant's actual blockage is a wound (an unhealed memory; a still-operative false-self pattern), the both-paths work surfaces wound-symptoms without addressing the wound itself. The downstream route is the A17 carpet-style family or other wound-work; deploying A33 prematurely produces wound-surfacing without wound-clearing. The Companion's discernment before deployment.

• **When the participant's hesitation is rooted in unconfessed sin.** A33 surfaces what is at stake on both paths; if the participant's actual blockage is a sin pattern that the path of doing would require confession or repentance to address, the protocol surfaces the sin-shaped resistance without addressing it. The downstream route is V2.Exp4 / A6 sin-channel work. Sometimes the same session has both — A33 surfaces sin-rooted resistance, the work pauses for confession, then continues; the Companion's discernment determines pacing.

• **When the Imagined-vs-Known move would press against Spirit-rooted prudence rather than against imagination-rooted fear.** The single most consequential failure mode for the move. Sometimes the participant's *imagined* path is actually correct prudence — the Spirit is showing them what the path of doing actually costs, and pressing the risk-taking would be pressing against Spirit-conviction. The V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices is non-optional before the move is deployed: is the imagined-voice accusation (resist), internalized lie (the move clears it), Spirit-conviction (heed the warning; do not press), or genuine prudential reasoning (honor the wisdom; the protocol completes without the risk-taking)? Failure mode is producing participant-harm by pressing against what the Spirit is showing.

• **When the protocol is run on a decision the participant has not actually heard from the Lord.** A33 is mission-discernment work on a mission-action the participant has heard but is hesitating to act on. If the participant has not heard the mission — if they are running A33 to determine whether to take on a mission they are considering — the protocol does the wrong work. The upstream route is V2.Exp7 hearing practices (PROAPT, Garden of Your Heart, Mission-Arc A27) to hear before deciding; A33 then handles the hesitation-at-acting after the hearing.

• **When the container's V2.Exp8 four conditions are not adequately established.** A33 produces substantial surfacing; if the container is insufficient (the scribe's verbatim record could be shared inappropriately; the group is not in focused-attention mode), the protocol produces surfacing without safe holding. The Companion's discernment is whether the container is sufficient before deployment.

• **Compressing the both-paths driving.** New Companions sometimes drive the easy path (the one the participant is leaning toward) to depth and accept a thin version of the other path. The both-paths driving is operationally constitutive of the protocol; thin-on-one-side produces an Imagined-vs-Known move that lands without grounding. Hold the both-paths driving rigorously.

• **Pressing the Imagined-vs-Known move when the participant is depleted or in acute affective state.** The move requires the participant to receive a re-statement of both paths' end-results and engage the imagination-vs-known distinction; if the participant is exhausted or in acute affect, the move lands flat or produces shame. Hold the protocol open for a subsequent session; defer the move.

## The protocol
The core operational sequence. Steps 1–4 are setup and orientation; steps 5–8 are the both-paths driving; step 9 is the Imagined-vs-Known move; step 10 is the risk-taking close; step 11 is the role-clarification close.

1. **Container setup.**

- Companion: confirm V2.Exp8 four conditions are met for the session. Mark the container's opening formally per V2.Exp8 refined (Claim 5).

- All participants: take positions. The participant designated for A33 work takes the structured isolated position (BoB form: standing on the floor separated from group; see Companion notes for FotH adaptation). A scribe is designated from the group.

- Discernment: confirm the container is sufficient and the participant is ready for the work.

2. **Scripture-for-meditation framing.**

- Companion: read or invite reading of 1 Corinthians 3:10–15. *"Hear this passage as we begin: 'Each one's work will become manifest, for the Day will disclose it ... If the work that anyone has built on the foundation **survives, he will receive a reward. If anyone's work is burned up, he will suffer loss, though he himself will be saved, but only as through fire.'"* Brief silence after the reading.

- All participants: receive the passage.

- Discernment: the scripture frames the protocol's eschatological register. What is at stake on each path is what will be revealed by fire; the participant's discernment is provisional, not authoritative.

3. **Orientation questions.**

- Companion: ask. *"[Participant], why are you here? What do you want?"*

- Participant: respond. The orientation surfaces what the participant has heard about their mission and the specific mission-action they are hesitating around.

- Discernment: probe for reality and energy in the answers. Sometimes the words come flat — the participant says the mission with words but no energy; that may signal the mission has not actually landed and the upstream work is V2.Exp7 hearing practices, not A33. Sometimes the words come with energy that confirms the mission is real and the hesitation is real. If flat, pause and re-orient.

4. **Scribe initialization.**

- Companion: confirm the scribe's role aloud. *"[Scribe's name], your role is to write what [participant] says verbatim — not paraphrasing, not editing, just the words as they come. The verbatim will go to [participant] at the close for their own discernment work."*

- Scribe: confirms readiness; takes paper or device.

5. **Path A: What's at risk if you do it?**

- Companion: ask. *"[Participant], what's at risk for you if you do this thing?"*

- Participant: name what is at risk. The first answer is rarely the full content; the Companion drives deeper. *"And then what? After that what happens? A year from now? Five years from now?"* Drive the path to its end-state.

- Scribe: writes verbatim.

- Discernment: hold the path-driving. Companions often want to alleviate the participant's affective load by cutting the driving short; resist the impulse. The path's end-state matters operationally.

6. **Path B: What's at risk if you don't do it?**

- Companion: ask. *"[Participant], what's at risk for you if you don't do this thing?"*

- Participant: name what is at risk. The Companion drives this path to its end-state same as Path A. *"And then what? After that what happens? A year from now? Five years from now?"*

- Scribe: writes verbatim.

- Discernment: drive Path B with the same rigor as Path A. The path the participant is currently walking (typically Path B — the path of not-doing) is the path of known consequences; this driving makes the known consequences visible alongside the imagined ones from Path A.

7. **Additional probes for self-knowledge avoidance.**

- Companion: ask, as the Spirit prompts. *"What is it about you that lets you avoid seeing this? What is the fear that stops you from seeing yourself?"*

- Participant: respond if there is response; sometimes silence is response.

- Discernment: these probes are deployed as the Spirit prompts during the protocol's running, not as scripted steps. The Companion may use them once, several times, or not at all in a given session — the discernment is whether what is being surfaced needs additional naming or whether the both-paths material is sufficient for the Imagined-vs-Known move.

8. **Restating both paths' end-results.**

- Companion: read aloud (or invite the scribe to read) the end-results of both paths from the verbatim record. *"On the path of doing, you said this is what your life looks like ... On the path of not-doing, you said this is what your life looks like ..."*

- Participant: receive the restating.

- Discernment: the restating consolidates the both-paths material into the form the Imagined-vs-Known move will operate on.

9. **The Imagined-vs-Known move.**

- Companion: name the move directly. *"[Participant], now one of these you KNOW to be true and one you IMAGINE to be true. The path of not-doing — you know that life by direct experience because you are currently walking it; you know its consequences. The path of doing — you have not walked that path yet; what you have said about it is what you imagine, not what you know."*

- Participant: receive the naming.

- Discernment (V2.Exp7A governs): before pressing the next step, the Companion must discern which kind of voice is producing the imagined-path's end-result. Is it an accuser (catastrophizing-imagination; resist; press the risk-taking)? Is it an internalized lie (the move's primary target; the naming itself often produces the clearing)? Is it Spirit-conviction showing the participant what the path actually costs (heed; do not press the risk-taking; honor what the Spirit is showing)? Is it genuine prudential reasoning (honor the wisdom; let the protocol complete without the risk-taking pressing forward; the count-the-cost work itself is the protocol's contribution)?

10. **Risk-taking invitation (when discernment confirms imagination-rooted fear).**

- Companion: invite. *"What I am inviting you to consider is taking a risk on testing the imagined path — to see if what you have been imagining is actually what would happen, or whether the imagination has been operating with the authority of known-fact when it is imagination. This is Hebrews 11 faith — the conviction of things not seen. The risk is faith-action."*

- Participant: receive the invitation; respond from where they are. Sometimes the response is *"I'll do it"*; sometimes it is *"I need to pray about it"*; sometimes it is *"I am still hesitating"*. All responses are honored; the protocol does not require commitment-in-the-moment.

- Discernment: do not press for in-the-moment commitment. The risk-taking is faith-action; faith-action is Spirit-led and may happen after the protocol closes, not within it.

11. **Role-clarification close.**

- Companion: mark the close of the structured isolated position. *"[Participant], A33 is complete. The structured position we've held is finished; we're back in fellow-pilgrim mode. The verbatim record from [scribe] will come to you for your own discernment and prayer."*

- Participant: rises from the isolated position; the structured position ends.

- Scribe: hands the verbatim to the participant or sets it aside for the participant to collect at session's end. The scribe role ends.

- Companion: close the session per the standard V2.Exp8 refined marked-closing form. V2.Exp9A corporate-witness affirmation may apply if appropriate to the session's work.

**Closing and de-roling**

**Role-clarification close** per step 11. The de-roling per A14 / refined V2.Exp8 applies in attenuated form — the participant's transition out of the structured isolated position is marked formally; the scribe's transition out of the structured recording role is implicit in the protocol's close. The role-clarification close is operationally smaller than A14's reciprocal formula (no figure has been held) and operationally analogous to A32 Hot Seat's close-pattern (marked transition out of structured listening position). The V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-closing applies for the session as a whole following the role-clarification close.

## Companion notes
The V2.Exp7A discernment at step 9 (before the Imagined-vs-Known move) is where to put your attention as you grow into running A33. The other steps are structurally simple after reps; the Imagined-vs-Known move is theologically and pastorally the load-bearing piece, and it depends on the discernment of whether the participant's *imagined* path is rooted in imagination-fear (where the move clears the lie) or in Spirit-rooted prudence (where pressing the risk-taking would press against what the Spirit is showing). The discernment matures with experience; new Companions should hold the move cautiously and consult with a Level II / III Companion when the discernment is unclear. Pressing against Spirit-conviction is the single most consequential failure mode.

The both-paths driving in steps 5 and 6 is structurally constitutive of the protocol. Companions often want to alleviate the participant's affective load by cutting the driving short on whichever path is harder for the participant. Resist the impulse. The path-driving makes the end-states visible; without sufficient driving, the end-states are vague and the Imagined-vs-Known move at step 9 has nothing to operate on. The driving is operational, not pedagogical scaffolding.

The scribe's verbatim role matters more than it appears. The participant comes out of A33 with the verbatim record in their hands for ongoing discernment-and-prayer work; the discernment work that follows the protocol often produces more clarity than the protocol session itself. A paraphrased record loses the Spirit's specific surfacing through the participant's own words. Hold the verbatim discipline.

The risk-taking close at step 10 should not press for in-the-moment commitment. The risk-taking is faith-action and is Spirit-led; faith-action may happen after the protocol closes, sometimes days or weeks later. A participant who takes the risk-taking commitment in-the-session because the Companion has pressed for it produces performance, not faith-action. Hold the invitation as invitation; let the response be what it is.

A pacing note. A33 typically runs 30–45 minutes for a full both-paths work with the Imagined-vs-Known move. Compressing it produces incomplete both-paths discernment; extending past 45 minutes typically produces diminishing returns. The Companion's discernment determines the right pacing.

## FotH adaptation guidance — §5.1(c) ongoing-workshopping
The BoB physical positioning — *standing isolated from the group on the floor* — carries men's-work-tradition resonance. The core structural element is the *participant's marked separation from the group*; the specific BoB form is the standing-isolated-on-floor positioning. FotH adaptation for women's-group and mixed-gender-group settings is in active development. Current FotH-practitioner reports suggest two adaptations to date.

**For women's-group facilitation:** practitioners report that *standing isolated on the floor* can produce isolation-overwhelm rather than productive separation, particularly in groups where the relational-trust pattern has been less embodied-physical and more verbal-relational. Alternatives in active use: a designated separate chair set apart from the group; a marked seat at the head of the table; sometimes simply standing or seated in the same physical position with the group's attention focused as the marker of separation. The core structural element (marked separation) preserves; the form varies.

**For mixed-gender-group facilitation:** practitioners report similar adaptations to the women's-group form, with additional attention to cross-gender dynamics in the isolated positioning. The standing-on-floor form is less common in mixed-gender groups; the seated-in-designated-chair form is more common. The §5.1(c) workshopping posture: the form is adapting in active practice; the core structural element (marked separation) is what matters.

**One specific FotH workshopping note** that practitioners have surfaced: the *scribe role* in A33 carries a gendered resonance in its origin (the BoB form was developed with men-as-scribe; the verbatim writing has been a quiet act of brotherhood in BoB practice). For women's-group and mixed-gender-group settings, the scribe role operates well across genders; no specific adaptation is required beyond ensuring the scribe is someone the participant trusts to hold their material faithfully. The verbatim discipline preserves; the gendered association does not.

The §5.1(c) workshopping posture means: these adaptations are practitioner-reports from active FotH practice, held provisionally; the core BoB form is what is documented above; subsequent revisions to this Part VI entry may update the workshopping guidance as the FotH practice matures.

**Bilgere-set delineation — relation to A7 Counter-Intention**

A33 and A7 share the Bilgere root-question *"what's at risk?"* but deploy it for different work. Per founder decision Session 3 Option A, the A7 editorial refinement to clarify the delineation is deferred to a separate near-term editorial pass; this Part VI entry records the current core delineation for Companion reference.

**A7 Counter-Intention** asks *"what's at risk if you do have it?"* as a single load-bearing question to surface the counter-intention — the part of the participant that does not want their stated goal. The work is parts-pluralist internal-division-integration; the Vol 2 home is V2.Exp6 (Tool Map) under fear-knot territory.

**A33 What's at Risk** drives the participant down both paths of a mission decision (the path of doing; the path of not-doing) by asking *"what's at risk?"* on each path. The work is mission-decision-clarification with the Imagined-vs-Known move as lie-identification at forward-looking-fear level; the Vol 2 home is V2.Exp5 (Connect with Mission) with cross-ref to V2.Exp6.

**The Companion's discernment:** is the participant stuck around a *stated goal* with internal-parts division (deploy A7) or hesitating at the *edge of a mission decision* with imagination-rooted fear (deploy A33)? The two often co-occur; when in doubt, A33's mission-context-and-both-paths-structure is the more containing protocol because the structured both-paths driving produces material the Imagined-vs-Known move can operate on. A7's single-question surfacing is appropriate when the territory is clearly internal-parts-integration around a stated goal (career goal, addiction recovery, relational pattern) rather than mission-decision-edge work.
