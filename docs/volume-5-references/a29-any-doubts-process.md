# A29 Any Doubts Process

## Any Doubts Process (V2.Exp7 — Hearing with Understanding)
## What this protocol does
Any Doubts is the core IJH doubt-clearing practice. It operates on a specific stated reality — a scripture or Spirit-given truth — that the participant intellectually assents to but has not yet received as lived trust. The protocol surfaces the below-the-surface doubt that has been holding the truth at a distance, brings the doubt into full awareness through deliberate exposure-and-amplification, lets the Spirit clear what has been exposed, and re-presents the truth so the participant can receive it cleanly. The primary downstream effect is hearing-faculty development at the specific point the doubt has been blocking; the secondary downstream effects are V2.Exp1 heart-soil clarification (the doubt being surfaced is below-the-surface heart material), V2.Exp2A false-self loosening (the wound-rooted attachment is exposed and named), and V2.Exp3 cognitive blockage clearance (the internalized lie loses its grip).

The protocol has two operational variants. The **standard form** ends in *"no more doubts"* and is deployed when the participant needs to clear a doubt about a specific truth. The **mission/priority/goals variant** ends in peace/love/harmony/joy (Galatians 5:22–23) and is deployed when the participant needs to surface and clear doubt-rooted blockage in mission-or-priority territory.

The theological grounding — JGT's *Working Hypothesis* (D1 source document, folded into the V2 entry's insertion text) — frames the practice as exposure-and-Spirit-access: the wound-rooted emotional attachment that holds the doubt in place is brought into awareness, the Spirit clears the specific point of blockage, the *fig leaf* comes off, the participant lives more freely in the area the doubt was governing. *"Praise God for the fig leaves that come off, one at a time."*

**When to use it**

• **When a participant has heard a specific scripture or Spirit-given truth but is not yet trusting it.** The most common deployment context. The participant says they believe the truth; the participant's life shows that the truth is not yet operating at the wound-level. Any Doubts is the bridge between intellectual assent and lived trust.

• **As an optional closing for A28 (Heart Bible Study with PROAPT).** When a participant's Apply-step impression in PROAPT surfaces a specific truth they want to live but are not fully trusting, the close-form Any Doubts runs the cycle on the heard truth before the session closes. Cross-reference to *Vol5_Part_VI_A28_HeartBibleStudy.md* §10.

• **For mission-and-priority territory** in the mission/priority/goals variant. When a participant has heard something specific about their mission or their priorities and cannot move forward because of underlying doubt about who they are or what the mission requires. The variant operates diagnostically alongside A30 (Shadow Mission)'s confessional approach to the same territory.

• **Standard form: Level I Companion competency** for facilitating. The eight-step coach script is operationally simple; the structural moves can be learned in a few reps. The voice-discernment at the doubt-naming step per V2.Exp7A is itself a competency that develops with experience.

• **Mission/priority/goals variant: Level II Companion competency.** The discernment of which fruit (peace, love, harmony, or joy) is the right close, and the discernment of when to deploy the variant rather than the standard form, requires Companion II maturity.

**When** NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)

• **Running the protocol on a self-statement rather than scripture or Spirit-given truth.** The most consequential failure mode. *"I am worthy"* as a self-affirmation produces self-confirmation, not truth-clearing. *"You have called me by name; I am yours"* (Isaiah 43:1) as a scripture-given reality produces actual doubt-clearing. The Companion's discernment before the protocol begins: is the stated reality genuinely scripture-rooted or Spirit-given? If not, the upstream work is finding the scripture (PROAPT) or hearing the Spirit (V2.Exp7 hearing practices) before running Any Doubts on what is heard.

• **Pressing the exaggeration step when the doubt-voice is accusation rather than internalized lie.** V2.Exp7A governs. The exaggeration step amplifies the doubt-voice deliberately; if the voice is the accuser of the brethren rather than an internalized lie, the amplification gives the accuser's whisper more space rather than exposing material for Spirit-clearing. The Companion's discernment before pressing exaggeration is non-optional. Accusation-voice doubt is resisted (V2.Exp9A standing-identity), not engaged through this protocol.

• **Pressing the exaggeration step when the doubt is Spirit-conviction.** Sometimes the *"doubt"* surfacing in response to a stated reality is the Spirit's conviction that the stated reality is not yet the participant's. This happens occasionally when the stated reality is a high promise the participant has not yet been formed enough to receive (*"You are the salt of the earth"* spoken to a participant whose life is showing the opposite). The Companion's discernment: is this lie-rooted doubt or Spirit-conviction? Spirit-conviction is heeded; the right move is to lay the protocol aside and engage what the Spirit is showing through the participant's resistance.

• **Treating the protocol as exposure-therapy.** The Bilgere original frames the practice mechanistically as psychological exposure-and-discharge. The Christian-frame adaptation reframes the mechanism: the exposure gives the Spirit access; the Spirit does the clearing. If the Companion runs the protocol in the exposure-therapy register, the practice drifts toward a psychological-mechanism approach the corpus does not endorse. Hold the pneumatological framing.

• **Letting the participant perform a *****"no more doubts"***** state rather than arriving at it.** The closing report is the participant's; the Companion's discernment is whether the report is genuine or performed. If the participant is reporting *"no more doubts"* because the practice has run long and they want to be done, honor the report at face value (do not press) but note the pattern and re-engage at a subsequent session. Performed clearance leaves the actual doubt in place to do its work later.

• **Running the protocol without the de-roling close-line.** The close-line (*"I'm not your projections, I'm your brother, [name]"*) is operational, not ceremonial. It marks the relational shift from the structured pilgrim/coach roles back to the fellow-pilgrim baseline. Skipping it leaves the structured-role dynamic in place after the practice closes; the participant may carry the dynamic out of the session in ways that interfere with the practice's full work.

• **Importing inner-work protocols mid-Any-Doubts.** If the exaggeration step surfaces wound material (rather than clearing as internalized lie), the right move is to complete or pause Any Doubts and route the wound material to its downstream protocol (A17 family for carpet-style work; V2.Exp4 / A6 for sin-channel work). Turning Any Doubts into an empty-chair or carpet-style work mid-protocol is a category error.

## The protocol — standard form (eight-step coach script)
The standard form's coach script. The "pilgrim" is the participant working through the doubt; the "coach" is the Companion or another group member running the script.

1. **Create the reality.**

- Coach: state the truth aloud to the pilgrim. *"[Pilgrim's name], hear this: '[scripture or Spirit-given truth].'"* The wording is direct; the truth is presented as truth, not as proposition to be evaluated.

- Pilgrim: receive. Eyes may be open or closed; whatever helps the receiving.

- Discernment: confirm before this step that the truth is genuinely scriptural or Spirit-given. If the pilgrim's stated reality is a self-statement, route upstream first.

2. **Any doubts?**

- Coach: ask. *"Any doubts?"*

- Pilgrim: respond honestly. *"Yes"* or *"No"* — sometimes the pilgrim cannot tell at first; the coach holds silence to let what is there surface.

- Discernment: if the pilgrim reports *"no doubts"* on the first cycle, ask one more time after a beat — *"are you sure?"* Sometimes the first answer is the polite answer; the real answer surfaces in the silence.

3. **What's the doubt?**

- Coach: ask. *"What's the doubt?"*

- Pilgrim: name the doubt. *"I don't believe God is good when I'm afraid."* *"I don't trust that I'm safe."* *"I don't think this applies to me."* Specificity matters; the doubt named at this step is what the protocol will work on.

- Discernment: (V2.Exp7A) what kind of voice is this? Internalized lie (proceed); accusation (do not amplify — resist instead, route to A8 or to V2.Exp9A standing-identity work); Spirit-conviction (lay the protocol aside and engage the conviction). Most doubts surfacing in this protocol are internalized lie; the discernment is the safeguard.

4. **Exaggerate.**

- Coach: invite. *"Make that doubt bigger. Louder. Stronger. Let it fill more space."*

- Pilgrim: do the exaggeration. Speak the doubt with more conviction; let the body posture express more of it; lean into the doubt fully.

- Discernment: this step is counterintuitive but operationally important. The exaggeration moves the doubt from background to foreground, from below-the-surface to in-the-light. The Spirit's access depends on this exposure.

5. **What feeling?**

- Coach: ask. *"What feeling do you experience with that doubt?"*

- Pilgrim: name the feeling. *"Fear." "Shame." "Loneliness." "Despair."* Single-word naming is good; if the pilgrim qualifies (*"sort of sad"*), gently ask for the more direct word (*"what is it really?"*).

- Discernment: hold receiving mode; do not interpret the feeling for the pilgrim. The feeling-naming is part of what gives the Spirit access.

6. **Exaggerate the feeling.**

- Coach: invite. *"Let that feeling get bigger. Feel it more fully. Stay with it."*

- Pilgrim: dwell in the feeling at peak intensity. This is operationally where the exposure does its work; the feeling reaches its full charge and starts to discharge.

- Discernment: stay present; do not rush. The Companion's role is witness (Claim 10) — receiving without reframing. The discharge happens at its own pace.

7. **Create the reality again.**

- Coach: re-state the truth aloud to the pilgrim. *"[Pilgrim's name], hear this: '[scripture or Spirit-given truth].'"* Same wording as step 1; the truth has not changed; what has changed is the pilgrim's capacity to receive it.

- Pilgrim: receive again. Notice what has shifted.

- Discernment: receive what arises in the pilgrim. Sometimes the doubt has cleared entirely on one cycle; sometimes it has cleared partially; sometimes a different doubt surfaces.

8. **Any doubts? — loop or close.**

- Coach: ask. *"Any doubts now?"*

- Pilgrim: respond.

- If *"yes" or partial doubt remains:* loop back to step 3 with the remaining or new doubt as the target.

- If *"no more doubts":* proceed to the close.

- Discernment: confirm the *"no"* is genuine not performed. If the pilgrim seems uncertain, ask once more — *"are you sure?"* Honor whatever the pilgrim says.

## The protocol — mission/priority/goals variant
The variant is used when the doubt-territory is mission-or-priority blockage rather than scripture-trust blockage. Steps 1–6 run as in the standard form; the variant diverges at steps 7–8.

1–6. **As in the standard form.** The stated reality is a mission-or-priority truth (*"My mission is to teach younger men in our church"*); the doubt-naming surfaces the underlying blockage (*"I'm not qualified"*); the feeling-naming and exaggeration expose the wound or lie underneath.

7a. **What virtue might that feeling be called?**

- Coach: ask. *"That feeling you're feeling — what virtue might it be called?"*

- Pilgrim: name the virtue. The fear-of-being-unqualified, exposed, often reveals as *humility*, or *reverence for the calling*, or *care for the people who would be affected*. The feeling reframes from blockage to gift.

- Discernment: hold the reframing gently. The Companion is not forcing a positive spin on a real feeling; the Companion is helping the pilgrim see what virtue the feeling is the negative-shape of. Not all feelings reframe this way; if the reframing does not land, return to step 6 and let the feeling discharge further before trying again.

7b. **What is it about you that cares so much?**

- Coach: ask. *"What is it about you that cares so much about this?"*

- Pilgrim: respond. The caring underneath the doubt typically points back to the participant's actual mission or calling — *"because I know how much it cost me to find what I have to teach them."* The question makes the mission visible behind the doubt.

- Discernment: this step often surfaces material that reinforces the mission against the doubt. The Spirit's work moves through the caring; the caring is itself a clue to what the participant has been given.

8a. **Close in fruits, not in doubt-clearing.**

- Coach: invite the close. *"What are you feeling now?"*

- Pilgrim: name what is present. Peace, love, harmony, joy (Galatians 5:22–23). The variant's close is not *no more doubts* but the fruit that has emerged once the obstacle cleared.

- Discernment: the Companion may close with a brief affirmation per V2.Exp9A — *"that's the Spirit's work. Hold onto that."* The variant's close does not loop the way the standard form does; it lands at the fruit and rests there.

**Pilgrim's rules (eight)**

The C1 source document specifies eight rules for the pilgrim, governing how to receive the practice:

1. Trust the coach. The coach is running the script; the pilgrim's job is to engage what arises.

2. Tell the truth. Name the actual doubt and the actual feeling, not the polite or expected version.

3. Stay with the feeling at peak. Do not bail when the feeling intensifies; the discharge happens at peak.

4. Let the doubt be specific. *"I don't trust God when X happens"* is workable; *"I have generic doubts"* is not.

5. Receive the closing reading. The truth being re-stated is for you; receive it as such.

6. Do not edit yourself for the group. If a doubt sounds bad or shameful, name it anyway; what is named gets cleared, what is hidden does not.

7. Stay until the protocol completes. Do not stop mid-cycle when discomfort rises; the discomfort is the work.

8. Trust the Spirit's timing. The clearing happens when the Spirit clears; the pilgrim's job is to engage, not to produce the clearing.

**Coach's rules (nine)**

The C1 source document specifies nine rules for the coach, governing how to run the practice:

1. Use the script. The wording matters; do not paraphrase the *"any doubts?"* or *"create the reality"* steps.

2. Hold the pilgrim's gaze where possible. Eye contact is part of the structured-listening role.

3. Do not rush. Silence between steps is functional; let the pilgrim find what is there.

4. Receive without reframing (Claim 10). The coach's job during steps 3 and 5 is to receive the doubt and the feeling without interpretation, advice, or pastoral redirection.

5. Press the exaggeration steps gently but explicitly. The pilgrim may resist; the resistance is part of the work. Hold the structure.

6. Discern voices (V2.Exp7A). Before pressing exaggeration, confirm the doubt-voice is internalized lie not accusation or Spirit-conviction.

7. Watch for wound material surfacing rather than lie material clearing. If the protocol is producing surfacing-without-clearing, complete or pause and route to downstream wound-work (A17 family).

8. Run the de-roling close-line at the close. *"I'm not your projections, I'm your brother, [name]"* — said by name, deliberately.

9. Close the container formally (per V2.Exp8 refined). The de-roling line is part of the marked closing, not a substitute for it.

**Closing and de-roling **

The protocol closes with the **de-roling close-line** — the coach saying, by name, to the pilgrim: *"I'm not your projections, I'm your brother, [coach's name]."* The line operationally clarifies that the structured pilgrim/coach roles end at the practice's close and the relationship returns to fellow-pilgrim baseline. The line is shorter than A14's full reciprocal formula because no figure has been held (the coach has not played the role of any external character for the pilgrim); the de-roling is *role-clarification* rather than *figure-release*.

Note that the close-line wording — *"projections"* — is BoB-internal and carries Bly/Moore lineage analogous to the *shadow* terminology in A23 / A30. For FotH-women's-group and FotH-mixed-group settings, the core wording may need §5.1(c) adaptation. Founder-attention flag 3 in the Phase 2 entry addresses this.

After the de-roling close-line, the container closes per the standard V2.Exp8 refined marked-closing form. If the practice has been run within a group context, the corporate-witness pattern per V2.Exp9A may apply at the close (the group affirms briefly what the Spirit did in this work).

## Companion notes
The V2.Exp7A discernment at step 3 is where to put your attention as you grow into running this protocol. The eight-step script is structurally simple; a Companion can run it adequately after a few reps. What separates an Any Doubts session that does its work from one that produces no clearing — or worse, that amplifies accusation — is whether the doubt-voice at step 3 has been correctly discerned as internalized lie before you press into exaggeration. Lie-voice doubt clears through the protocol. Accusation-voice doubt is *resisted, not engaged*; pressing exaggeration on accusation gives the accuser more space. Spirit-conviction-voice doubt is *heeded, not transformed*; pressing exaggeration on conviction softens what the Spirit was saying. Hold the discernment consciously.

The exaggeration step is the second place to put your attention. New Companions often shy away from it because asking a pilgrim to make their doubt bigger feels like adding to suffering. The exaggeration is operationally what brings the doubt from below-the-surface to the surface where the Spirit can work on it. Done gently but explicitly, it serves the work; done timidly or skipped, the protocol does not clear what it could clear. Hold the structure even when the pilgrim's discomfort rises; the discomfort is part of the work, not a sign that the work is going wrong.

The de-roling close-line is operationally smaller than A14's reciprocal formula but functionally analogous. Say it deliberately, by name, with eye contact. Treat the moment as the formal close it is. A Companion who runs the de-roling perfunctorily communicates that the structured-listening role the practice has required is not actually a real thing — and the pilgrim absorbs that communication. The practice's container holds because the closing holds.

A note on what voice surfaces. The most common doubt-voice in this protocol is internalized lie — wound-rooted, often parental-voice in origin, sometimes culture-rooted. The protocol clears these well. Less common but real: accusation, which sounds like *"this scripture doesn't apply to you"* or *"who do you think you are"* — the accuser's whisper. Resist; do not engage through this protocol. Even less common but real: Spirit-conviction, which sounds like *"this truth is what I am asking of you; the resistance is your own work I am **pointing at."* Heed; lay the protocol aside; engage what the Spirit is showing. The three-fold discernment is one of the most operationally consequential competencies the corpus asks of Companions.

The mission/priority/goals variant is a Level II move. The variant's two additional steps (*"what virtue might that feeling be called?"* and *"what is it about you that cares so much?"*) require Companion II maturity to deploy well. New Companions should run the standard form many times before attempting the variant. When the variant is right, it lands beautifully and the pilgrim leaves with the fruit of the Spirit visible rather than just with doubt cleared.
