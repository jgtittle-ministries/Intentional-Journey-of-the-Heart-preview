# B18 Heart Prayer Time

**V2 anchor:** V2.Exp7 (Hearing with Understanding) — primary home; V2.Exp5 (Connecting with God) — substantive cross-reference; V1.Exp2 refined (the spirit as deliberate attending-and-choosing) — anthropological grounding; V2.Exp0 (Tool Import Discipline); V2.Exp7A (Discernment of Voices); V2.Exp8 refined (Container — formally marked close).

**Companion deliverable to:** *IJH_Vol2_Phase2_B18_HeartPrayerTime.md* (Cycle 2, Track 1).

## What this protocol does
Heart Prayer Time is the corpus's principal practice for exercising the spirit's faculty of deliberate attending and choosing in the prayer territory. The practice operationalizes V1.Exp2 refined — *the spirit is the faculty by which a person deliberately attends and chooses, operating in active communion with the Holy Spirit* — through a structured five-step prayer sequence. Each step's work establishes the conditions for the next. Repeated over time, the practice trains the spirit's attending-and-choosing capacity in the prayer territory and supports the broader continuous-attending discipline the May 2006 source document names (the reframing of *pray without ceasing* in 1 Thess 5:17 as continuous attending of the Father-connection).

The protocol's authority comes from the Spirit, not from the participant's cultivated attentional skill. The Spirit directs the attending; the Spirit informs the choosing; the participant's deliberate engagement is the faculty of communion. The Companion's framing language at each step keeps the Spirit-mediation explicit. *The attending is the Spirit's.*

**When to use it**

• **As a regular daily, weekly, or rhythm-of-life practice** for any practitioner whose prayer life would benefit from structure. The protocol is foundational, not occasional.

• **At significant decision-points or moments of discernment** where the participant wants to attend deliberately to what the Father is saying about a specific subject.

• **In group context** as a Companion-led practice that develops the body's shared discipline of deliberate attending in prayer.

• **As preparation for other deeper formation work** — Heart Prayer Time is operationally upstream of much of the corpus's other work; what surfaces in step 4 (*Enacted prayer*) may route to A24 (Blessing Receiving), A25 (Breaking the Contracts), A17 (Carpet-Style Inner Work), A29 (Any Doubts), V2.Exp4 (Confession), or other protocols as the routing logic indicates.

• **As the structured-prayer-time form that supports a broader continuous-attending discipline** throughout the day (per V2.Exp5 practice-theology footnote, Track 2).

• **At Companion II competency for new-practitioner facilitation;** Level I appropriate for supporting established practitioners and for solo deployment guidance with periodic Companion II check-in.

**When NOT to use it**

• **When the participant is in active dissociation, dissociative material is in front of them, or significant trauma material is unprocessed and the container conditions for processing are not in place.** Heart Prayer Time's deliberate-attending discipline can intensify what is already in awareness; if dissociative or trauma material surfaces during the protocol, the Companion stops the protocol, deploys appropriate containment (per V2.Exp8 refined), and routes to trauma-competent care.

• **When the participant is in acute mental-health crisis** that warrants clinical attention. Heart Prayer Time is a formation practice; it is not psychotherapy and not a substitute for clinical care.

• **When step 2 (*****Father, where are You?*****) consistently produces no felt-sense of communion across multiple deployments and the participant experiences this as Father-absence.** This may signal V2.Exp2A (false-self / wound) material requiring upstream therapeutic work, or V2.Exp4 (sin-channel) material requiring confession-work, or V2.Exp1B (wound/sin diagnostic) work that distinguishes which is operational. The Companion discerns and routes; Heart Prayer Time does not work on the absence-of-communion presenting issue directly.

• **When the participant is bypassing other operational work that requires direct engagement** (a specific reconciliation needed, a confession needed, a relational repair needed) by using prayer as substitute for the operational work. Heart Prayer Time's step 5 (*Closure-into-obedience*) is intentionally part of the protocol to prevent this drift; if the participant repeatedly cannot land the obedience-direction, the Companion may surface that prayer is being used as substitute for the operational work itself.

## The protocol
The protocol runs in roughly 15–30 minutes for solo deployment; group deployment (where each participant runs the protocol with a Companion's framing) typically adds 30–45 minutes of group processing afterward. The five steps do not skip.

**Step 1 — *Who am I?***

**Companion framing:** *"Before we begin to pray, we land in who you are in Christ. This is not a self-description exercise. We attend to the identity the Lord has given you — what He has spoken about you, what scripture declares of you, who He says you are. Let this be the ground on which the rest of the prayer operates."*

**Participant work:** Take 1–3 minutes of quiet attending. The participant lands in identity-in-Christ — *I am a beloved son / daughter; I am hidden with Christ in God; I am in active communion with the Holy Spirit through Christ.* If a particular identity-claim has been recently affirmed (per V2.Exp9A core witness; per A13 closing-affirmation; per A24 blessing-round), the participant may land specifically there. The grounding is what makes the rest of the prayer the prayer of the participant in Christ, not the prayer of whatever fragment of self happened to be in the room.

**Companion discernment.** If the participant cannot land in identity-in-Christ in this step — repeatedly tries and finds only false-self material, or finds emptiness, or finds shame-voice or accuser — the Companion considers whether the participant needs upstream work (A24 Blessing Receiving, A8 Transforming a Shaming Voice, A17 Carpet-Style work, V2.Exp4 Confession, A12 Splitting a Role) before continuing. The first step is the precondition; if it cannot land, the rest of the protocol cannot do its work.

**Step 2 — *Father, where are You?***

**Companion framing:** *"Now we attend toward the Father. The address is to the Father in the register Christ taught us — 'Our Father.' Not philosophically — not 'God is everywhere' — but particularly: 'Father, where are You with me, right now, in this room, in this circumstance?' We attend toward where He is. We rest in what surfaces."*

**Participant work:** Address the Father directly. Attend toward Him with the deliberate-attending faculty V1.Exp2 refined names. What surfaces may be a sense of warmth, an image, a felt nearness, a passage of scripture rising in memory, a posture, sometimes silence. The participant rests briefly in what surfaces; the communion is being mediated by the Spirit (Rom 8:26). Do not rush past this step into the prayer's content. The communion is the ground; without it, step 4 operates without anchor.

**Companion discernment per V2.Exp7A.** What surfaces may be the Spirit speaking, may be the accuser, may be an internalized lie or remembered voice, may be the participant's own anxious imagination. The Companion's framing reminds the participant that discernment is needed for what arises; the V2.Exp7A discipline operates within the protocol. *Spirit-conviction is heeded; accusation is resisted; internalized lie or remembered voice is named and addressed through the relevant tool family later in the work.*

**Step 3 — *What am I praying about?***

**Companion framing:** *"With the communion established, we direct our attending to specific subject. This is not general piety — not 'thank you for the day, please bless this group.' We name specifically what we are praying about. What is the matter? What concerns me right now? What am I bringing to the Father?"*

**Participant work:** Name specifically what is being prayed about. The naming can be a circumstance, a relationship, a decision, a wound, a calling, a question, a person, a request. The specificity matters; without it, the prayer drifts into general piety and the spirit's deliberate-attending-and-choosing faculty is not exercised in focused application. Most prayer-practice in contemporary church life starts at step 4 (*Enacted prayer*) without doing this step's work; the protocol's discipline is to do the directing-attention work explicitly.

**Companion discernment.** If the participant cannot name specifically, the Companion may gently surface — *what is the most pressing thing?* — but does not direct what the participant should pray about. The naming is the participant's deliberate attending; the Companion holds the space for it.

**Step 4 — *Enacted prayer***

**Companion framing:** *"Now we pray. The prayer arises from the foregoing three steps. We may pray aloud or silently; we may pray in our own words or with scripture's words; we may pray briefly or at length. What matters is that the prayer is the participant's prayer in active communion with the Spirit — not floating utterance, not religious performance. We attend to what the Spirit prompts as we pray; we attend to what the Father responds; we attend to what is happening in the prayer itself."*

**Participant work:** Pray. The prayer may take any form — petition, intercession, lament, thanksgiving, confession, supplication, listening-prayer. The character of the prayer flows from steps 1–3. The participant attends throughout: to what is being prayed, to what the Spirit prompts, to what arises in communion. The continuous-attending discipline that V2.Exp5's practice-theology footnote (Track 2) describes operates within this step. The prayer is the deliberate exercise of the spirit's attending-and-choosing faculty in active communion.

**Companion discernment per V2.Exp7A.** What arises in prayer is tested. If Spirit-conviction surfaces, the protocol pauses and the participant heeds (sometimes the obedience-direction surfaces in step 4 rather than step 5). If accusation surfaces, the participant stands in identity in Christ (Rom 8:1) and resists. If internalized lie or remembered voice surfaces, the Companion notes it for routing to appropriate downstream work after the protocol closes. The V2.Exp7A discipline is not a separate step; it operates within step 4 (and step 2) continuously.

**Step 5 — *Closure-into-obedience***

**Companion framing:** *"The prayer is not complete until the obedience-direction has surfaced. What is the Lord asking us to do as a result of this prayer? Sometimes it is a specific action. Sometimes it is a posture or a stance. Sometimes it is to continue attending in this direction for the next week. We name what the obedience is. We commit to it. We close."*

**Participant work:** Name the obedience-direction. The naming operationalizes V1.Exp5 (Obedience Channel) — what was prayed becomes what is enacted. The obedience may be: a specific behavior (call someone; have a conversation; make a decision; refrain from something); a posture (forgive in a sustained way; trust in this specific area; release this concern); a continuing attending (keep attending toward this for the week; come back to this in the next Heart Prayer Time deployment). The participant commits to the obedience. The protocol formally closes via V2.Exp8 refined's *formally marked close* (a brief closing prayer, a verbal naming of the close, a moment of silence — the Companion's discernment selects the form).

**Companion discernment.** If the obedience-direction does not surface, the Companion may surface gently — *what is the Lord asking?* — but does not direct. If the protocol consistently produces no obedience-direction across multiple deployments, the Companion considers whether prayer is being used as substitute for the operational work itself (per the *When NOT to use it* note); routing to V1.Exp5 work or pastoral conversation may be appropriate.

## Companion notes
**On the Spirit-mediation framing throughout.** The protocol's authority comes from the Spirit, not from the participant's cultivated attentional skill. This is the source-reversal that distinguishes Heart Prayer Time from the secular contemplative / mindfulness adaptations that share the operational form. The Companion's framing language at each step keeps the Spirit-mediation explicit. Without the framing, the practice can drift toward contemplative-cultivation as self-project even when the participant is Christian. The framing is not optional preface; it is operationally constitutive of what the practice is.

**On the discipline of not skipping steps.** Practitioners under time pressure sometimes start at step 3 or step 4, treating the first two steps as preliminary. The skipped steps' work — establishing identity ground and communion — does not get done; the resulting prayer operates from whatever default-self happened to be in the room. The Companion at first deployments names that all five steps are operational; once the participant has internalized the discipline (typically after 5–10 deployments), the Companion can step back to periodic check-in mode.

**On the continuous-attending discipline that the discrete protocol supports.** Heart Prayer Time as a structured 15–30 minute practice is the discipline's discrete form. The broader work the practice supports is the continuous attending throughout the day that V2.Exp5's practice-theology footnote (Track 2) describes. Brother Lawrence's *practice of the presence of God* and Frank Laubach's *minute-by-minute experiment* are convergent witness. The Companion may name to a maturing practitioner that the discrete protocol is in service of the continuous practice, not as a substitute for it.

**On the triangulation discipline (Dave Smith, April 2006).** A practitioner working through step 2 (*Father, where are You?*) or step 4 (*Enacted prayer*) may find that the felt-nearness in communion includes a perspective on a circumstance that the participant's own viewpoint had not been able to hold — *Christ sees this differently than I have been seeing it.* The Smith framing names this as triangulation — stepping outside one's own viewpoint to receive the viewpoint Christ holds. The discipline operates within the protocol naturally; the Companion may name it explicitly for a maturing practitioner whose prayer life is ready to receive the language. The triangulation framing here is at the prayer-discipline level; see A10 (Triangle / Triangling Protocol) for the facilitation-move-level application.

**On routing material that surfaces beyond the protocol's scope.** Heart Prayer Time is upstream of much of the corpus's other work. The Companion holds the routing logic:

• *Shame-at-depth material* with image-of-God dimensions → A24 (Blessing Receiving).

• *Contract or binding-agreement material* → A25 (Breaking the Contracts).

• *Wound material requiring carpet-style work* → A17 family (with V2.Exp6A + V2.Exp6B coupled-pair governance).

• *Specific doubt-of-truth material* → A29 (Any Doubts Process).

• *Sin-channel material* → V2.Exp4 (Confession).

• *Mission-articulation or shadow-mission material* → A27 / A30 / A31 (Mission-Arc family) or A21 (Say Your Mission).

• *Discernment-of-voices uncertainty* → V2.Exp7A operational at depth; may require Companion II consultation if not resolved within the protocol.

The protocol's *Closure-into-obedience* step often becomes *and the next move is [route]* rather than a discrete obedience-action; the Companion's discernment handles this.

**On Companion II / Level I competency framing.** New-practitioner facilitation requires Companion II competency at the V2.Exp7A discernment level; the new practitioner is learning the discipline at the same time as engaging the protocol, and the Companion's discernment-and-routing competency is operationally needed. Established-practitioner support and solo deployment guidance is Level I-appropriate — the practitioner has internalized the basic V2.Exp7A discipline and can run the protocol largely on their own with periodic Companion II check-in. The two-level framing is honest about what each deployment context requires.

**On the practice as training-discipline rather than single-experience.** Per 1 Tim 4:7–8 and the core conception of V1.Exp2 refined as the spirit's faculty operating through repeated deliberate practice, Heart Prayer Time's deepest work happens not in any single deployment but in the long-term accumulation of repeated deliberate attending against System-1 default (per Vol 5 Part III Kahneman descriptive mechanism — see Track 3 supplementary materials). The Companion may name to a practitioner that the practice forms the spirit over time; daily-or-weekly deployment is the structural support for that formation. A practitioner who deploys the protocol three times and concludes *nothing happened* may need the long-form framing — the formation is gradual and cumulative, like physical training, not transformational-event work.

## Materials
• None required beyond a quiet space and (optionally) scripture or a journal. The participant may prefer to journal during step 3 (naming what the prayer is about) and step 5 (naming the obedience-direction) to support memory and commitment. The protocol can be done sitting, standing, walking, kneeling, or in any physical posture conducive to the participant's attending; the Companion may name posture options at first deployments without prescribing.

## Time
• **Solo deployment:** 15–30 minutes typical; can run shorter (5–10 minutes for brief deployments at significant moments) or longer (45–60+ minutes for retreat-style or deep-discernment deployments). The five-step discipline holds across the time-range; what varies is the depth of each step.

• **Group deployment with Companion framing:** 45–75 minutes total — the participants run the protocol simultaneously with Companion framing at each step (allowing 3–5 minutes per step), then 30–45 minutes of group processing or sharing afterward.

• **Daily-rhythm deployment:** Many BoB / FotH practitioners run a brief daily form (10–15 minutes) supplemented by a weekly fuller deployment (30–45 minutes).
