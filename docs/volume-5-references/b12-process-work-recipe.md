# B12 Process Work Recipe

## What this protocol does
The Process Work Recipe is a five-step sequencing-architecture for any single carpet-work process: (1) find what the participant wants to have happen; (2) represent the internal state with role-players; (3) get direction via Triangle; (4) work the dynamic — whatever the discernment surfaces (anger, grief, metaphor, shadow); (5) get the affirmation and de-role. Bilgere himself flags the recipe explicitly as "a recipe for the first few attempts; experienced facilitators improvise within and around the structure." The recipe is structural scaffolding rather than rigid sequence; the discipline is the *order of moves* (intent → represent → discern → engage → close), not the specific content within each step.

B12 operates as carpet-work-sequencing-architecture within the broader session liturgy's work-component (V2.Exp8B). When the Companion has discerned that carpet-work is the work-option for the gathering, B12 supplies the five-step recipe that sequences whatever specific carpet-work protocol the Companion deploys (A17 Healing and Forgiveness; A23 Shadow and Projections; B11 Ideal Support Figure; B14 Kinesthetic Metaphor; B15 Pull-out Work; or any combination the discernment surfaces). The recipe is *one specific form* of the broader discernment-engagement-closure pattern that operates across multiple Christian-traditional practices (PROAPT; *lectio divina*; Ignatian spiritual direction's noticing-and-engagement pattern) and is named as such in the core V2 prose. The work is the Lord's; the recipe holds the form within which the Lord's work unfolds. *The session is the Lord's*; B12 supplies the carpet-work-sequencing within the session-liturgy frame.

**When to use it**

• **As default architecture for any single carpet-work process in BoB / FotH practice.** The recipe is the operational default; departures from the recipe (experienced-facilitator improvisation; specific protocols that supply their own architecture, such as A17 and A23 which carry the nine-step opening as built-in architecture) are deliberate Companion choices, not the operational default.

• **For new Companions developing carpet-work competency.** Bilgere's framing ("a recipe for the first few attempts") names the recipe's pedagogical role; Companions developing carpet-work competency through their Companion II training benefit from running the recipe explicitly through their early carpet-work deployments before improvising within it.

• **For complex carpet-work situations** where the Companion's discernment about what protocol to deploy will emerge during the work itself (step 3's Triangle move) rather than being pre-determined. The recipe's open step 4 (work the dynamic with whatever the discernment surfaces) is operationally helpful when the carpet-work's specific protocol cannot be selected in advance.

• **As pedagogical instrument for Companion-training conversations** about carpet-work — the recipe gives Companions explicit operational language for what the carpet-work session does and how the moves sequence.

• **At Companion II competency.** The recipe presupposes carpet-work competency (the participant-Companion relationship is established; the container is held under V2.Exp8 refined; the Companion can deploy A10 Triangle, A11 Metaphoric Listening, A12 Splitting a Role, A13 Affirmation, A14 De-roling as discrete moves). Level I Companion accompaniment of Companion II primary facilitation is appropriate as apprenticeship.

**When NOT to use it**

• **When the carpet-work protocol carries its own structural architecture.** A17 (Healing and Forgiveness) and A23 (Shadow and Projections) each carry a nine-step opening that *is* the carpet-work architecture for that specific protocol; B12's recipe duplicates work A17 / A23 already does structurally. The Companion's choice when A17 or A23 is the work-option is to run the protocol's own structure rather than wrapping it in B12's recipe.

• **For session-liturgy-level work-options that are not carpet-work.** PROAPT (A28), Council deliberation (A5), Tell Your Story (A26), Heart Prayer Time (B18), and other non-carpet-work-options have their own core structures; B12 does not wrap them. B12 is specifically the carpet-work-sequencing-architecture; deploying it for non-carpet-work-options is category error.

• **For experienced Companions whose carpet-work practice has internalized the recipe.** Bilgere's framing names this directly: "experienced facilitators improvise within and around the structure." A Companion whose carpet-work practice has matured may run carpet-work without explicit recipe-deployment, with the recipe operating as internalized structural sense rather than as explicit five-step protocol. The pedagogical role recedes as competency develops.

• **When the participant cannot engage cognitively.** B12's recipe presupposes cognitive engagement — the participant can name what they want to have happen (step 1), can attend to role-player-represented internal state (step 2), can participate in Triangle (step 3). When the participant cannot engage cognitively (active flashback; severe dissociation), the carpet-work shifts to B15 Pull-out Work specialty; the recipe does not wrap B15.

• **In contexts where carpet-work is not appropriate.** The recipe does not make carpet-work appropriate where it would not otherwise be; the Companion's pre-deployment discernment about whether carpet-work is what the gathering needs governs whether the recipe deploys at all.

## The protocol
B12 runs across the five steps with pacing typical of 60–120 minutes for a single complete carpet-work process. The recipe is not a strict timer; the Companion's discernment governs pacing within each step.

**Step 1 — Find what the participant wants to have happen (5–15 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Before we begin the work itself, let me ask: what is it you're wanting to have happen here? What's the shift you're hoping for, the place you'd want to land? This is not a contract — we don't always get where we thought we were going, and the work the Lord does is sometimes different from the work we expected. But naming what you're reaching for orients us."*

**Participant work:** Name the desired outcome. The participant articulates as specifically as they can what shift they are hoping the work will produce — *"I want to be free of this anger toward my dad"; "I want to know what God wants me to do about this fear"; "I want to find what's underneath this shame I keep feeling around my brother"; "I want to receive what I didn't get when my mom died."*

**Companion discernment.** The step 1 naming serves three operational purposes: (a) it engages the participant's deliberate attending-and-choosing faculty per V1.Exp2 refined (the participant chooses the orientation rather than drifting into whatever surfaces); (b) it gives the Companion data about which carpet-work protocol the work may need (anger toward dad → A17 family or A12 Splitting a Role; fear about a decision → V2.Exp7 hearing work; shame around brother → A23 family or B11 Ideal Support Figure if the shame is the kind reachable through reparenting; receiving what wasn't received at the wound → B11 Ideal Support Figure); (c) it begins the participant's settling into the work and the container.

The Companion's framing names the desired-outcome as orientation, not as outcome guarantee. The Spirit's work in carpet-work often goes places the participant did not anticipate; the orientation-naming holds the participant's deliberate intention without locking the work into a pre-determined endpoint. The Companion's discernment governs whether to redirect a desired outcome that signals operational misframing (the participant wants to *get someone else* to change; the participant wants to *prove* something to themselves; the participant wants to *bypass* what the work would actually surface).

**Step 2 — Represent the internal state with role-players (5–15 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Let's bring what you're describing into the room. We'll have someone hold [the angry voice / the wounded child / the figure you're reaching toward / the shaming voice / the role you're naming]. Who do you want to ask to hold [that role]? Once we have the role-players in place, we'll see what surfaces."*

**Participant work:** With Companion guidance, identify what parts of the internal state need to be externalized through role-players. The participant chooses which group members will hold which roles. Common externalization moves: the wound-figure (e.g., the parent who wounded); the wounded younger-self of the participant; the present-day part that carries the charge; an internal voice (the shaming voice; the accuser; the encouraging voice); the figure-being-reached-toward (e.g., the Lord; an ideal-support figure; the future-self the participant is becoming).

**Companion discernment.** The step 2 representation is where the carpet-work moves from naming to embodiment. The Companion's discernment governs which parts to externalize first — too many at once produces confusion; too few produces flat work. Sometimes only one role-player is needed to start (the wound figure; the wounded younger-self); other figures enter as the work progresses. The Companion attends to who the participant chooses for each role; sometimes the choice itself surfaces material (the participant chooses someone in the room who carries the same gendered or relational energy as the original wound-figure, which the Companion may or may not name explicitly during the work). De-roling per A14 is non-negotiable at the close of the work (step 5); the Companion does not need to remind the role-players of this at step 2, but the Companion holds the de-roling requirement in mind as a structural feature of the entire process.

**Step 3 — Get direction via Triangle (5–15 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"Let's step out and look at what we have. [Companion brings the participant six to eight feet out of the scene to the Triangle position; places a role-player in the participant's just-vacated position.] What do you see? What's accurate? What's missing? What's the next move?"*

**Participant work:** From the disassociated Triangle position, the participant observes what has been set up and discerns direction. Per A10 (Triangle Phase 2), the move serves multiple functions: (a) get direction; (b) develop characters (refine what the role-players are holding); (c) get the participant out of feeling and into discernment; (d) confirm consent before proceeding to deeper work; (e) end the process gracefully when going deeper would not serve.

**Companion discernment.** Triangle is operationally the most consequential single step in B12 — it determines what protocol step 4 will deploy. The participant from the Triangle position will typically surface one of three outcomes:

• *Clear direction for step 4 protocol selection.* The participant sees the scene, names what needs to happen next, and the Companion routes to the appropriate carpet-work protocol — A17 family (wound-healing); A23 family (shadow / projection work); A12 (role-splitting); B11 (ideal-support reparenting); B14 (somatic-first if the participant's processing is body-led); A8 (transforming a shaming voice). The Companion's competency at protocol-selection-from-Triangle is itself a Companion II discernment skill.

• *Recognition that going deeper would not serve.* The participant from Triangle sees that the work has reached the point it can reach for this session. The Companion gracefully closes the process via step 5 without forcing a step 4 deployment.

• *Need for further character development before step 4.* The participant from Triangle sees that the role-players are not yet holding what is needed (the wound-figure needs to say more; the shaming voice needs to be voiced more specifically; an additional figure needs to enter the scene). The Companion refines the scene per the participant's discernment from Triangle and re-enters Triangle as needed before stepping to step 4.

V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices governs throughout: the Companion attends to whether what surfaces in Triangle is the participant's own discernment, the Spirit's leading, or material from internal voices (accuser; internalized lie) that requires its own handling. The Companion's framing during Triangle keeps the Spirit-mediation explicit: *"What is the Lord showing you about this scene? What is the next move the Spirit is bringing forward?"* The Triangle's disassociated position is structurally available as the participant's spirit-faculty-engaged attending position per V1.Exp2 refined — the deliberate attending-and-choosing the participant exercises from Triangle is where the work's direction is discerned.

**Step 4 — Work the dynamic (20–60+ minutes; the longest step)**

**Companion framing:** Companion deploys the specific carpet-work protocol the Triangle discernment surfaced. *"Step back into the scene. We're going to [run A17 Healing and Forgiveness sequence / run A23 Shadow and Projections sequence / deploy B11 Ideal Support Figure work / shift to B14 Kinesthetic Metaphor on what's holding here / split the role per A12 / work with the shaming voice per A8]. Here's how we'll do this..."*

**Participant work:** Engage the deployed carpet-work protocol. The participant works within whatever protocol the Triangle surfaced, with the Companion running the specific protocol per its core Vol 5 Part VI entry.

**Companion discernment.** The Companion's primary work in step 4 is to run the selected carpet-work protocol per its core structure (each carpet-work protocol's Part VI entry supplies the operational specifics). The Companion's secondary work is to hold the Spirit-mediation framing throughout and to discern when the work needs to shift to a different protocol mid-step-4. Routing logic during step 4:

• *Work that surfaces deeper wound mid-protocol* → may shift from A23 to A17 (projection-recovery surfaces a wound that needs healing first).

• *Work that surfaces shaming-voice material* → may shift to A8 within step 4 or after step 4 closes.

• *Work that surfaces blessing-deficit material* → may shift to B11 within step 4 or after step 4 closes.

• *Work that surfaces somatic stuck-ness* → may shift to B14 within step 4.

• *Work that surfaces active flashback* → must shift to B15 with the appropriate competency and containment moves; or, if the Companion is not at Companion III competency, must close cleanly and route to subsequent care.

The Companion may run multiple Triangle moves within step 4 to refine direction; the recipe's step 3 / step 4 boundary is flexible during deep work. The closing of step 4 happens when the participant from within the work (or from a Triangle move within step 4) has reached the work's natural completion — the wound has been received and healed; the projection has been owned; the shadow has been brought to light; the figure has offered what the participant could receive; the somatic externalization has been completed. The Companion's discernment governs when step 4 closes.

**Step 5 — Get the affirmation and de-role (10–25 minutes)**

**Companion framing:** *"We're going to begin to close this work. First the affirmation, then the de-roling. [Step into closing-affirmation framing.] What did the Lord speak to you in this work? What is true now that wasn't fully held before? Let it land in the room."*

**Participant work:** Receive the closing affirmation per A13 — the participant names what the Spirit spoke during the work; the body's corporate witness amplifies per V2.Exp9 + V2.Exp9A; the affirmation is the Lord's, not the participant's self-affirmation. The closing affirmation grounds the participant in identity-in-Christ before the role-play release.

**Companion framing for de-roling:** *"Now we de-role. [Per A14 De-roling protocol.] Role-players, please line up in front of [participant]. Each of you, name yourself out of the role you held. [Participant], you'll respond to each."*

**Participant work:** Run the reciprocal de-roling per A14. Each role-player names themselves out of the role: *"I am not [the angry parent / the wounded child / the figure / the shaming voice]; I am [their own name]."* The participant responds: *"You are not [the role]; you are [their name]."* Where B11 was deployed in step 4 (Ideal Support Figure), the de-roling for the figure-role-player requires particular care per B11's bridge-to-the-Father core discipline: *"You are not the Father; you held the figure for me to receive what the Father gives. The Father is the Father."*

**Companion closing framing:** *"The work is closed. The container is released. Receive what the Lord did in this room into the week ahead."* The B12 recipe's step 5 typically operates within the larger session-liturgy closing per V2.Exp8B Component 5; the recipe's de-roling and closing-affirmation feed into the session-liturgy's closing sequence rather than substituting for it.

**Companion discernment.** Step 5 is structurally constitutive per V2.Exp8 refined paragraph 2 (*work that is opened but not closed, or closed sloppily, dissipates rather than integrates*). The Companion's pacing of step 5 matters — too fast loses the integration; too slow loses the marked-ness. Twenty minutes is core default; thirty appropriate where step 4 was substantial; fifteen appropriate for lighter step-4 deployments. The de-roling is non-negotiable; the affirmation is non-negotiable; the participant's transition from carpet-work back to standing-in-Christ-identity is what makes the work formative rather than merely cathartic.

## Companion notes
**On the recipe as form within the session-liturgy frame.** B12 is *one step deeper* than the session-liturgy's work-component frame — V2.Exp8B's work-component supplies the *what* (which protocol the gathering deploys); B12 supplies the *how the protocol unfolds in carpet-work form*. The recipe operates within the work-component, not as substitute for it. The Companion's framing at step 1 may briefly recall the session-liturgy context: *"We're moving into the work-component now; tonight's work is carpet-work; let's begin with the recipe..."*

**On the recipe's pedagogical role.** Bilgere's framing — "a recipe for the first few attempts" — names B12's primary pedagogical function. Companions developing carpet-work competency benefit from running the recipe explicitly through their first 10–20 carpet-work deployments before improvising within it; the explicit structure scaffolds the competency that eventually internalizes. Companion-training conversations about carpet-work can use B12 as the structural-vocabulary the conversation operates within.

**On the discernment-engagement-closure pattern's broader convergence.** The recipe's structure (intent → represent → discern → engage → close) operates at the same structural pattern as PROAPT's six-step pattern (Pray → Read → Observe → Apply → Pray-again → Tell), *lectio divina*'s four-step pattern (lectio → meditatio → oratio → contemplatio), Ignatian discernment's broader pattern (preparation → engagement → discernment → application), and the corpus's broader contemplative-engagement pattern. The convergence is named in V2 prose (Track 1 §step 2a) without canonizing a general structural-pattern claim. The Companion's awareness of the convergence supports teaching B12 alongside PROAPT and *lectio divina* in Companion-training contexts where multiple practices are introduced together.

**On step 4 as the variable element.** The recipe's structural rigor is at steps 1, 2, 3, and 5; step 4 is the variable element where whatever specific carpet-work protocol the discernment surfaces deploys. The recipe does not pre-determine step 4's content; the Companion's competency at protocol-deployment-within-step-4 is what makes the recipe operationally useful rather than merely structural.

**On the source-reversal Spirit-mediation framing throughout.** The recipe's framing language at each step-transition keeps the locus of agency at the Lord's level. *The work is the Lord's; we are gathered as the body in His name; the Spirit's work in the access points the recipe opens is what makes the work formative; the recipe is structural scaffolding holding the form within which the Spirit's work unfolds.* Without the framing, B12 produces real carpet-work that may or may not be formation; with it, the carpet-work operates under V2.Exp8B's source-reversal discipline.

**On routing during step 4.** The Companion's during-step-4 discernment about whether to shift protocols mid-step requires Companion II competency. Common routing patterns: A23 → A17 (projection-recovery surfaces wound requiring healing); A17 → A24 (wound-healing surfaces image-of-God / blessing-deficit material); A8 → A23 (shaming-voice work surfaces shadow material); B14 → A17 (somatic-first work surfaces a wound the participant can now access); A17 → V2.Exp4 / A6 (wound-healing surfaces sin material requiring confession); B11 → A24 (reparenting work surfaces image-of-God material — note: B11 and A24 share theological territory; the routing here is one-way from B11's grief / shame-knot work to A24's image-of-God depth-work when the participant's blessing-deficit lands at the depth-axis A24 addresses); active flashback → B15 (Companion III only; otherwise close cleanly and route to subsequent care).

**On Companion II competency development through the recipe.** New Companions learning carpet-work benefit substantially from running the recipe explicitly through their first 10–20 deployments. The recipe gives the new Companion: (a) a structural sense of how carpet-work sequences; (b) explicit operational language for what each step does; (c) discernment-development scaffolding at step 3 (Triangle); (d) a framework within which protocol-selection-at-step-4 develops as competency. Companion III practitioners running Companion II training can use B12 as the primary instructional instrument for carpet-work-deployment training, with the recipe operating as both pedagogical structure and conversational vocabulary.

## Materials
• **Space appropriate to carpet-work session** — circle seating with room for participants to move into and out of the carpet (the physical center where the work happens). Group sizes up to 8–10 work cleanly; larger groups require modifications.

• **Optional materials per the specific carpet-work protocol deployed at step 4** (per each carpet-work protocol's Part VI entry).

• **Optional Triangle-position marker** — some Companions use a specific physical marker (a small rug; a particular chair) for the Triangle position at step 3. The marker operationalizes the disassociation-into-discernment move at a sensory level. Not required; the Companion's verbal framing handles the move without physical marker if preferred.

## Time
• **Standard B12 deployment:** 60–120 minutes total. Allocation: Step 1 (intent) 5–15 min; Step 2 (represent) 5–15 min; Step 3 (Triangle / direction) 5–15 min; Step 4 (work the dynamic) 20–60+ min; Step 5 (affirmation + de-role) 10–25 min.

• **Compressed B12 deployment:** 45–60 minutes total. Truncates step 4 to single-protocol deployment without mid-step routing; shortens step 5 closing. Useful when the body's time-window does not support full deployment.

• **Extended B12 deployment:** 120+ minutes for complex carpet-work involving multiple protocol routings or deep work-component engagement. Sometimes the work asks for the full extended deployment; the Companion's discernment governs.

• **Retreat-format deployments:** Multiple complete B12 deployments per day, spaced with rest and integration time. The recipe scales cleanly to retreat pacing.
