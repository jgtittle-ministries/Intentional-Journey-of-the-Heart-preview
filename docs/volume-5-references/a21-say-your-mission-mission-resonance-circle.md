# A21 Say Your Mission / Mission Resonance Circle Protocol

## Say Your Mission / Mission Resonance Circle Protocol (V2.Exp5 — Connecting with Mission, with cross-ref to V2.Exp9 Corporate Witness)
## What this protocol does
Say Your Mission is a brief public-declaration practice in which each man, as he is willing, steps to the center of a circle of his fellow-pilgrims, holds a simple structural marker (a stick is the historical W@H form; any single object can serve), and says his mission in one or two sentences. The remaining men in the circle are instructed to listen — not to evaluate the words but to attend to their own hearts and wait for the heart's *Yes.* When a man hears his own heart's Yes — when something in what was said registers in him as recognition of something true the Spirit is doing — he silently steps in to stand with the man at center. The gesture is *resonance,* not commitment to join. The facilitator goes first to model the practice. The closer is a prayer drawn from Ephesians 3:14–21, optionally extended with an Acts 13:3 sending word when the moment fits.

The protocol's primary downstream effect is that the declared mission is held in the corporate witness of the body of fellow-pilgrims in a way that the same mission held privately is not. The Deut 19:15 / Matt 18:16 / 2 Cor 13:1 corporate-witness pattern is structural ground for this — what is established before witnesses has corporate substance the same declaration does not have when held alone. The secondary downstream effect is that the *listening men* are trained in the discipline of attending to their own hearts rather than to the speaker's words — a Discernment of Voices (V2.Exp7A) competency that builds across many A21 cycles and transfers to other corpus protocols.

The active operational mechanism is *embodied resonance.* The listener's body moves before the listener's words have formed; the heart's Yes is registered first in the body and only sometimes, only later, becomes verbal. The protocol explicitly removes the peer-verbal-evaluation move that the naive pattern would supply and substitutes the Spirit-mediated heart-recognition move that the corpus's refined V1.Exp2 anthropology (attending and choosing) names as the spirit's proper operation.

**When to use it**

• **As the closer of a retreat or extended event** where men have spent substantial time articulating their missions and are leaving with declarations that need a corporate-witness moment before they re-enter ordinary life. The W@H Gateway Event is the historical origin context; the protocol generalizes to any retreat or extended-event closer in the BoB or FotH tradition.

• **As the closing protocol of an A31 (Mission Stretch Clarity) cycle** when the refined mission articulation has emerged with sufficient specificity that public declaration is fitting. A21 is often the natural closer of the Mission-Arc family work.

• **At a group's annual retreat or rhythm milestone** when each man re-articulates the mission he is walking in the coming year. The recurring use builds the body's longitudinal record of each man's mission-trajectory.

• **At a group-lifecycle closing** (B22 territory) when each man names where he is going next as the group concludes. The protocol's brevity and structural cleanness make it fitting for closing moments.

• **As an intermediate-meeting practice within a BoB onboarding sequence (A22)** when a new member's mission articulation has developed sufficient clarity in the three-meeting onboarding to warrant public declaration. Often appropriate at the close of the third onboarding meeting or shortly after.

• **As a one-off practice** in any moment where a man has done substantial mission-articulation work and is coming to a point of decision or commitment that warrants the body's witness. The Companion's judgment about whether the moment is ripe is the discernment.

• **Level I Companion competency** for the facilitating role. The Companion's primary moves — framing the listener instruction, modeling first, holding the silence between declarations, closing with the prayer — are operationally simple. The discernment about *when* to deploy A21 develops with experience but is not gating; a Level I Companion can run the practice from this entry alone.

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

• **Before the upstream mission-articulation work has produced enough clarity.** Running A21 with under-articulated missions produces shallow corporate-witness and noisy embodied-resonance signal. The listeners' heart-Yes responses become unreliable because the declarations themselves are vague. The Companion's pre-flight check: is each man's mission articulated with enough specificity that a heart-Yes is a meaningful response, or are the missions still in development? If the latter, route to V2.Exp5 articulation work, A27 Who Are You, or A31 Mission Stretch Clarity before deploying A21.

• **Substituting verbal evaluation for the embodied-resonance instruction.** The most common failure mode. A Companion forgets to give the listening instruction, or gives it weakly, and the men default to verbal affirmation after each declaration (*"that's powerful, brother," "I see that in you"*). The peer-affirmation move that the protocol explicitly removes returns by default. The Companion's framing of the listening instruction before the first declaration is non-negotiable, and the Companion should restate or re-cue the instruction if peer-verbal-affirmation begins to creep in during the practice.

• **Pressing men to step in.** The protocol allows for a declaration that receives no stepping-in, or only one man stepping in, or many men stepping in. The number of stepping-in men is information about how the body's heart registered the declaration; it is not a target to be optimized. A Companion who notices that few men have stepped in for a particular man and silently signals (eyebrow-raise, glance) for more men to step in has corrupted the protocol. The Spirit-mediated heart-recognition is what it is; the Companion's discipline is to receive it, not direct it.

• **Pressing men to declare.** Each man's participation is voluntary. Some men will not step to the center during a given A21. The reasons vary — some have under-articulated missions; some are in a season of waiting; some have missions they are not ready to declare publicly. The protocol honors each man's discernment. A Companion who applies social pressure for participation (*"come on, brother, we want to hear yours"*) has converted the practice into a coercive moment that no longer carries the body's free witness.

• **Verbal processing of declarations after the practice closes.** The protocol's closing is the Eph 3:14–21 prayer, optionally with a sending word. After the close, the men disperse — back to ordinary group rhythm, or out to travel home from a retreat. *Processing* the declarations afterward — *"why do you think only four **men stepped in for so-and-so?"* — converts the embodied corporate-witness into post-hoc analytical critique, which is the opposite move. The Companion's discipline is to close cleanly and let the practice's effects do their work in the men's lives over the days that follow.

• **Running it without an adequately formed container.** A21 requires V2.Exp8 container conditions (Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional) and the formally-marked-opening discipline. Running A21 in a group whose container is shaky produces noisy embodied-resonance signal because the men are not fully present to attend to their own hearts; they are partly attending to social safety, social judgment, or external distraction. The Companion's pre-flight check: are the container conditions in place?

• **Running it as a one-off without integration into broader rhythm.** A21 deployed once and then never again can read as a special-occasion performance rather than a corpus-core practice. The corpus's intention is that A21 enters the body's repeating rhythm where appropriate — annual retreats, mission-cycle closings, group-lifecycle milestones — rather than living as a single Gateway Event memory.

## The protocol
1. **Container check and opening.**

- Companion: confirm the container conditions are in place per V2.Exp8 refined (Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional). Open the session with the formally-marked opening per the group's established practice (silence; a brief naming of what the session is for; a prayer; the marking that the work has begun).

- Discernment: if any of the four container conditions are weak, address before continuing. The witness-discipline (V2.Exp8 third paragraph, refined May 2026) is specifically load-bearing for A21 — the listeners are being asked to witness in the deepest sense.

2. **Framing the practice.**

- Companion: name what is about to happen. *"What we are about to do is called Say Your Mission. As each of you is willing, you'll step to the center, pick up the stick, and say your mission in one or two sentences. Just the mission, no preamble — the practice is in the saying, not in the explaining. When you're done, set the stick down and step back out. I'll go first to show you the form."*

- Participants: receive the framing. Questions about the form can be answered now; the practice itself runs without verbal interruption.

3. **Framing the listening instruction.**

- Companion: this is the non-negotiable move. *"For the rest of us, while a man is declaring, our job is not to evaluate his mission. Our job is to attend to our own hearts. Listen for your own heart's Yes — for something in what he says that registers in you as recognition of something true the Spirit is doing in him. If you hear it, step in silently and stand with him. The stepping-in is resonance — your heart said yes — not commitment to join his mission. If your heart is quiet during a declaration, stay where you are. There is no wrong response. Some men will have many stand with them; some will have few; some will have none. That is information about how each declaration landed in the body, not a measure of the man. The work is the attending."*

- Participants: receive the listening instruction. The Companion may pause briefly to let the instruction settle.

4. **Facilitator models.**

- Companion (becoming declaring man for one cycle): step to center, pick up the stick, say his own mission in one or two sentences, set the stick down, step back out. The other men attend per the listening instruction; some may step in.

- Discernment: the Companion's modeling is real — the Companion is also a declaring man, also subject to the body's heart-witness. The Companion does not over-perform the declaration or under-perform it to be polite; the discipline is to declare as a fellow-pilgrim.

5. **Subsequent declarations.**

- Companion: invite the next man, or open the floor for any man as he is willing. The order is not strictly fixed; the Companion's judgment about whether to invite specific men or open the floor depends on the group's pattern. Generally, opening the floor and letting the men come as the Spirit moves them works cleanly.

- Declaring man: step to center, pick up the stick, say his mission in one or two sentences, set the stick down, step back out. Remain at the edge of the circle as a fellow-pilgrim for subsequent declarations.

- Listening men: attend per the instruction. When a heart's Yes is heard, step in silently. Remain standing with the man for the duration of his declaring-moment. When the declaring man steps back out, the men who stepped in also return to the edge of the circle.

- Companion: hold the silences between declarations. A pause of several seconds before the next man steps to center is appropriate; the practice's pace is unhurried.

- Discernment: if a man begins to declare and his words trail off or he stops, the Companion lets the silence hold; the declaring man may resume or may step back out. If he steps back out before completing the declaration, the Companion does not press for completion — the man's discernment is his own.

6. **When all who will have declared have declared.**

- Companion: discern when the practice has run its course. There may be a pause where no one steps forward and the body's stillness signals that the practice is complete. The Companion may name this — *"if there is more, let us continue; if we have come to the close, let us close together"* — or may simply move to closing if the discernment is clear.

7. **Closing prayer.**

- Companion: invite the men into the closing posture (standing in a circle, hands open, eyes closed if fitting). Pray aloud, drawing from Eph 3:14–21:

*"For this reason we bow before the Father, from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named, that according to the riches of his glory he may grant these men — and grant us all — to be strengthened with power through his Spirit in the inner being, so that Christ may dwell in their hearts through faith. We pray that they, being rooted and grounded in love, may have strength to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, that they may be filled with all the fullness of God. Now to him who is able to do far more abundantly than all we ask or think, according to the power at work within us, to him be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus throughout all generations, forever and ever. Amen."*

- Optionally — when the moment fits (retreat closer; group-lifecycle closing; men departing for travel) — add a brief sending word, drawn from the Acts 13:3 pattern:

*"The Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit send you out in the strength of the Lord. Go in peace to love and serve the Lord."*

8. **Formally-marked session close per V2.Exp8.**

- Companion: close the session per the group's standard V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked closing. The prayer above is the protocol's close; the session's close (which may include the standard de-roling check, the Companion-led affirmation if appropriate, the practical-housekeeping for next gathering) follows.

## Companion notes
• **Length.** A21 typically runs forty to ninety minutes for a group of eight to twelve men, including framing, the declarations themselves, and the closing prayer. Larger groups stretch longer; smaller groups can complete in thirty to forty-five minutes. The Companion plans accordingly and does not rush.

• **Pacing.** The silences between declarations are not awkward gaps to be filled; they are constitutive of the practice. The Companion's discipline is to let the silence be silence and to trust the men's own pacing in coming to center.

• **The stick (or alternative marker).** Any single object can serve. Historical W@H form is a stick; carved crosses, Bibles, designated stones, or any other simple structural marker work. The marker should be substantive enough to feel intentional (not a paper plate; not a coffee cup) but not so precious that it distracts attention to itself. A walking stick, a shepherd's staff, a wooden plinth — these work cleanly.

• **The Companion's modeling.** The Companion goes first as a fellow-pilgrim, not as a special figure. The Companion's mission gets declared and gets the body's heart-witness response same as every other man. The Companion does not perform the modeling differently or seek to demonstrate "how it should be done" — the discipline is to declare honestly and let the men learn the form by watching.

• **Reading the body's response.** The Companion attends to the body's response without directing it. If a declaration produces no stepping-in, this is information; the Companion notices but does not intervene to fill the silence or invite others to step in. If a declaration produces many stepping-in, this is information; the Companion notices and lets the corporate-witness moment hold. The information is held by the Companion for subsequent pastoral judgment about each man's mission-trajectory, not announced in the moment.

• **Adapting for women's groups (FotH).** The §5.1(c) gender-framing pastoral-pattern applies. The protocol's structural moves transfer cleanly; the listening instruction's reference to "*your own heart's Yes*" is gender-neutral and need not be modified. Companion judgment about specific framing language in the listener instruction may add or substitute words appropriate to the group's culture. The closing prayer is gender-neutral as drafted. The historical W@H Gateway Event origin is a men's-tradition note; the protocol is operationally available for women's-tradition deployment with no major adaptation.

• **What if a man's declared mission is one the Companion finds theologically problematic?** Hold the practice; do not intervene mid-declaration. The protocol's instruction is for the men to attend to their own hearts; the Companion's heart will register what the Companion's heart registers. After the practice closes, if there is genuine pastoral concern about a declared mission, the Companion addresses it in subsequent pastoral conversation with the man — not in the corporate-witness moment. The protocol is not a Companion-evaluation move; it is a body's-heart-witness move.

## Materials
• A single structural marker — a stick (historical W@H form), or alternative simple object — held by each declaring man at center.

• Sufficient floor space for a circle of the participating men plus center room for declaration and stepping-in.

• No paper, notes, or technology required.

## Time
• Forty to ninety minutes typical, scaling with group size. The Companion plans for the longer end of the range; if the practice completes faster, the remaining time can hold open silence or a brief Companion-led closing reflection.
