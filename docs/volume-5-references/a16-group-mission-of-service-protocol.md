# A16 Group Mission of Service Protocol

## Group Mission of Service Protocol (V2.Exp5 — Connecting with Mission, with substantive cross-reference to V2.Exp9 Community as Amplifier)
## What this protocol does
The Group Mission of Service Protocol is a Companion-led multi-session corporate-discernment practice in which a body of fellow-pilgrims collectively listens for what the Lord has prepared for the body, as a body, to do beyond itself — a *Vision* of the body's outward calling and an *Action* by which the Vision becomes concrete commitment over a definite time horizon. The protocol's operational mechanism (multi-session consensus-building toward outward action) is inherited from the MKP I-group Mission Protocol; the protocol's agency framing reverses the MKP source. *Our group's project of self-expression in the world* (MKP) becomes *the body's discernment of what the Lord has prepared for it to do* (Christian frame; Eph 2:10 applied corporately; Acts 13:1–3 corporate-discernment pattern).

The protocol is the core home in the corpus for the discipline of group-mission articulation. Specific group-mission articulations developed by particular bodies — the CtR Band of Brothers' *Climbing the Mountain of God Together* metaphor with seven aspects; the 2008 ASC Manual's Thanksgiving-food-basket and church-painting examples; each FotH body's own articulation as it develops — are worked exemplars of the discipline that A16 describes. The core entry describes the discipline; the exemplars illustrate the discipline in worked form.

The protocol's primary downstream effect is that the body has a named, scripturally-grounded, body-discerned outward mission that it is enacting in real action. The MKP framing of *small-group narcissism* is the structural concern the protocol counters: bodies left to themselves tend to turn inward, deepen the existing members' bond at the cost of outward action, and lose the corporate-mission orientation that gives the body its purpose beyond mutual support. *You are the light of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hidden* (Matt 5:14). The body's outward action is constitutive of its witness, not optional decoration on its interior work.

**When to use it**

• **When the body has reached maturity sufficient to do corporate work that exceeds aggregated individual work.** The V2.Exp9 amplification claim is operationally load-bearing here. A body whose corporate hearing has not reached the relevant maturity will produce group-mission discernment that reads as group-mission choosing — surface consensus on what the existing members collectively prefer, scaled up. The pre-flight Companion discernment is whether the body's corporate hearing is mature enough. Typical readiness signals: members have done substantial individual-mission work (A21, V2.Exp5 articulation, Mission-Arc family); the body has run multiple cycles of substantive interior work (carpet-style, clearings, story-telling) successfully together; corporate-prayer practice is operational in the body's rhythm.

• **When the body has noticed inward drift.** Bodies that have been doing good interior work but have begun to sense the inward turn — the members are growing closer in friendship but the body's sense of corporate calling beyond itself has dimmed — are appropriate for A16. The MKP framing of small-group narcissism is the relevant diagnostic; the body itself recognizing the inward drift is often the readiness signal.

• **At a body's formational milestone.** Bodies that have completed a major arc of interior work (extended carpet-style cycles; a Mission-Arc cycle for each member; a major retreat) are often ready to articulate what the work has produced as a corporate calling.

• **When the body is being asked to consider new outward work** — a particular ministry opportunity has surfaced; a need in the broader community has come to the body's attention; a member has discerned individual mission that overlaps with potential group mission. The body uses A16 to test whether what is being asked is its corporate calling or a different kind of opportunity.

• **As a rhythm at intervals of a year or more** for bodies that have established corporate-mission practice. The articulation can be refined or replaced as the body's calling matures across years; A16 deployed periodically rehearses the discipline and keeps the corporate-mission orientation operational.

• **Level II Companion competency recommended** for facilitating A16 deployment. The agency-source reframing operating across multiple sessions requires Companion maturity that develops with reps; a Level I Companion can participate in an A16 body led by a Level II+ Companion, and can co-facilitate as an apprenticeship move.

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

• **When the body's corporate hearing has not reached V2.Exp9 amplification maturity.** The single most consequential contraindication. A body whose corporate hearing is not yet mature will run through the protocol's steps and arrive at *something* — a Vision-and-Action consensus that looks like group mission. But the Vision will tend to reflect what the existing members already collectively want to do (scaled up); the Action will tend to be what feels comfortable rather than what is being called to. The body will not have practiced corporate-discernment; the body will have practiced *group-choosing labeled as discernment.* This is worse than no deployment at all because it teaches the body bad habits about what group-mission discernment is. The Companion's pre-flight discernment governs.

• **Without sustained spiritual posture across the sessions.** The Acts 13 pattern is *while they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said.* The spiritual posture is what creates the conditions for the Spirit's specific direction. A16 deployed as a planning meeting (or series of planning meetings) without corporate prayer, scripture, worship as the sustained ground of the work produces planning outcomes, not discernment outcomes. The Companion's framing each session re-establishes the posture.

• **Compressing the multi-session pacing.** Bodies sometimes pressure the protocol to complete in a single session because the calendar is tight or the body wants resolution. The multi-session pacing is operationally constitutive — the discernment matures across the sessions as members listen and re-listen, as scripture and prayer settle into the body's collective sense, as consensus emerges or hesitates. A single-session A16 produces a quick consensus that is more likely group-choosing than discernment. The Companion's discipline: hold the multi-session pacing even when the body wants to compress.

• **Treating consensus as the goal rather than as a check on the discernment.** False consensus is the most common failure mode after spiritual-posture failure. The body verbally agrees to a Vision-and-Action for the sake of moving forward; some members have not actually received the discernment but assent because dissent feels obstructive. The discernment's authority comes from its Spirit-source, not from its consensus-completeness. Moments of hesitation or partial consensus are information about the discernment, not obstacles. Acts 13: the Spirit spoke; the body recognized; the body acted. The recognizing was the consensus; the speaking was the source.

• **Allowing one strong member's individual mission to become the body's group mission by default.** Bodies often contain a member whose individual mission is so clear and so compelling that the body's discernment work risks coalescing around that member's calling rather than the body's calling as a body. The Companion's discipline: the body's mission is the body's mission. A member's strong individual calling may inform the body's discernment; it does not substitute for it. 1 Cor 12: the body has its own integrity distinct from its members.

• **Scaling up the body's existing interior work as the Action.** The Action must be genuinely outward. *Doing more of what the body already does* (more story-telling, more clearings, more interior work) is not group-mission-of-service in the corpus's sense. Matt 5:14–16 names the outward orientation — *let your light shine before others; that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father.* The Action is good works seen by others, not interior work intensified.

• **An Action too ambitious for the body's actual capacity.** Bodies excited by the discernment can over-commit at the Action level — naming a project that exceeds what the body can sustain. The Companion's pacing discipline: the body's first group-mission Action should be modest in scope; the discipline of articulation-to-action-to-completion-to-reflection builds the body's capacity for richer subsequent group-mission work. The 2008 ASC Manual's worked examples (a single Thanksgiving food-basket outing; painting one church) are appropriately modest as first-cycle Actions.

• **In a body where unresolved interpersonal tensions are operationally dominant.** Bodies carrying unresolved conflict among members will struggle to discern corporately because the corporate posture is fragmented. The appropriate move is to address the interpersonal work first — through A1/A2 Clearings, pastoral conversation, or where appropriate the broader carpet-style work — and return to A16 when the body's corporate posture is sufficiently restored.

## The protocol
The protocol runs across multiple sessions, typically two to four. The session-level structure varies but the four typical sessions, named by their work, are described below. Bodies that have done A16 before may complete in fewer sessions; bodies for whom A16 is new typically benefit from the full four-session pacing.

**Session 1 — Posture and Surfacing**

1. **Container check, opening, framing.**

- Companion: confirm V2.Exp8 conditions. Open the session with the formally-marked opening per the body's established practice. Frame the multi-session work: *"We are going to spend [number] sessions together listening for what the Lord has prepared for this body to do beyond itself. This is not a planning meeting; this **is a discernment. The Antioch church pattern is our scriptural shape — Acts 13: while they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said. Our work across these sessions is to maintain the posture and listen for what the Spirit is showing about our corporate calling."*

2. **Corporate prayer and scripture.**

- Companion: lead extended corporate prayer (15–20 minutes). Read one or more of the core scriptural texts aloud — Eph 2:10, Acts 13:1–3, Matt 5:14–16, 1 Cor 12:12–27 — and hold silence for receiving.

- Discernment: do not rush this. The posture is the work; the surfacing flows from the posture.

3. **Individual surfacing round.**

- Companion: invite. *"As we have prayed and listened, what is each of you sensing the Lord may be preparing this body to do? Not what you personally are called to; what the Lord may be calling us to as a body. Brief answers; speak as you have them; pass if you have nothing yet. We are surfacing, not deciding."*

- Members: speak as they have it. Some members will surface specific possibilities; others will surface themes or directions; others will pass.

- Discernment: hold the surfacing in V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices framing. Receive without analysis in the moment.

4. **Companion summary and close.**

- Companion: briefly name what has been surfaced (without evaluating or synthesizing prematurely). Hold the surfacing as the material the body will continue to listen with between sessions. Encourage members to pray with what was surfaced.

- Close the session per V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked closing.

**Session 2 — Listening and Discernment-Testing**

1. **Container check, opening, framing.**

- Companion: confirm V2.Exp8 conditions; re-establish the spiritual posture; re-frame the multi-session work.

2. **Corporate prayer and scripture.**

- As Session 1, with attention to the material surfaced in Session 1 and held in prayer between sessions.

3. **Continued surfacing and testing round.**

- Companion: invite. *"What has the Lord shown each of you in the time between our last session and now? What of the previous surfacing has clarified, sharpened, or fallen away? What new has emerged?"*

- Members: speak as they have it.

4. **Discernment-testing exchange.**

- Companion: open the body for mutual discernment-testing per 1 Cor 14:29. *"Let us weigh together what we have surfaced. What does this surfacing align with in scripture? What does it align with in the body's existing work? What capacities and limitations does the body bring to it? What is the Spirit confirming; what is the Spirit not yet confirming?"*

- Members: discern together; ask each other clarifying questions; test surfaced material against scripture, body-rhythm, and capacity.

- Discernment: this is the work the multi-session pacing exists to enable. Hold time; let the discernment unfold rather than press for resolution.

5. **Companion summary and close.**

- Companion: name what has clarified and what is still developing. Hold the body in the discipline of continued listening between sessions.

- Close per V2.Exp8.

**Session 3 — Vision Articulation**

1. **Container check, opening, framing.**

- Companion: confirm V2.Exp8; re-establish posture.

2. **Corporate prayer and scripture.**

- As prior sessions.

3. **Vision articulation work.**

- Companion: invite. *"As we have listened and tested, what is the Vision the body is hearing? What is the Lord preparing this body to do? Let us articulate it together."*

- Members: articulate. The work is iterative — proposed language; testing of the language; refinement; re-testing. The body works toward articulation that the body recognizes (consensus-as-check), not consensus that the body constructs (consensus-as-outcome).

- Discernment: hold the verbal parallelism with A13. *"What did the Lord prepare for this group to do?"* The framing keeps the agency-source reversal active. If the body's articulation drifts toward *"what we want to do,"* gently re-cue.

4. **Vision check.**

- Companion: read the articulated Vision aloud. Hold silence. *"Does this articulate what we have been hearing? Where does it need refinement? Where does it not yet land?"*

- Members: respond. The Vision is iterated until the body recognizes it.

5. **Companion summary and close.**

- Companion: hold the body in the Vision; encourage members to continue listening with it between sessions, attending to what the Lord may add or clarify.

- Close per V2.Exp8.

**Session 4 — Action Articulation and Sending**

1. **Container check, opening, framing.**

- Companion: confirm V2.Exp8; re-establish posture.

2. **Corporate prayer and scripture.**

- As prior sessions.

3. **Action articulation work.**

- Companion: invite. *"The Vision is articulated. Now we ask: what concrete Action operationalizes the Vision in commitment over a definite time horizon? What will this body actually do in the next [weeks / months / season] that enacts the Vision? Modest scope appropriate to where we are. The Action is what makes the discernment a sending."*

- Members: articulate the Action together. Iterate as with the Vision. Hold realistic scope.

4. **Action check.**

- Companion: read both Vision and Action aloud. Hold silence. Test the integration: does the Action actually enact the Vision, or is it tangential? Does the Action match the body's capacity?

- Members: respond. Refine as needed.

5. **Sending.**

- Companion: lead the body in a corporate sending prayer drawing on the Acts 13 pattern. *"As the Antioch church laid hands on Barnabas and Saul and sent them, so we mark this body's sending into the work the Lord **has prepared. May the Spirit go before us; may the Father strengthen us; may Christ be glorified in our walking. Amen."* The Companion may include explicit laying-on-of-hands if the body's tradition welcomes it; for bodies whose tradition does not, the verbal sending is sufficient.

6. **Companion summary and close.**

- Companion: hold the body in the sent posture. Name the practical next-step (when the Action begins; who carries what responsibility; how the body will know it is done). Plan a brief reflection session at the Action's completion to mark what was learned.

- Close per V2.Exp8.

## Companion notes
• **The agency-source reframing language is non-negotiable across all sessions.** Practice the verbal parallelism with A13 (*"what did the Lord prepare for this group to do?"*) until it is natural. A Companion whose framing drifts toward group-as-source produces group-as-source outcomes regardless of what the protocol intends.

• **Watch for the V2.Exp9 amplification dependency.** Pre-flight discernment is critical. If you are unsure whether the body's corporate hearing is mature enough, consult a more senior Companion before deploying. If the body's hearing turns out to be insufficient during Session 1, the right move is often to suspend A16 and route to V2.Exp9 development work rather than complete A16 with thin discernment.

• **Multi-session pacing is constitutive.** Resist compression. Bodies that complete A16 in a single session have not practiced corporate discernment; they have practiced group choosing.

• **Consensus is a check, not a goal.** When the body's discernment work surfaces hesitation or partial consensus, hold the hesitation as information. The Spirit may be leading toward further listening rather than toward resolution. Do not press the body toward verbal agreement.

• **The Vision and Action are iterated, not arrived at in single attempts.** Both Sessions 3 and 4 may need multiple iterations of articulation-test-refine before the body recognizes the language. This is normal.

• **Modest first Actions are appropriate.** The 2008 ASC Manual's worked examples (a Thanksgiving food-basket outing for a specific neighborhood; painting a particular church) are appropriately scoped. A body's first A16 cycle should produce an Action the body can complete in two to twelve weeks and reflect on; later cycles can produce longer-horizon Actions.

• **The CtR BoB *****Climbing the Mountain of God Together***** metaphor is available as worked exemplar** if the body finds richly developed group-mission articulations useful as orientation. Other bodies' articulations may also serve. The discipline is that each body develops its own articulation in its own context; worked exemplars illustrate the shape, not the form.

• **For women's groups (FotH), the §5.1(c) gender-framing pastoral-pattern applies.** The protocol's structural moves transfer cleanly. Companion judgment about specific framing language at certain moments may add or substitute words appropriate to the group's culture; the agency-source reversal and the Acts 13 pattern are gender-neutral and apply without adaptation.

• **Reflection at the Action's completion is operationally important.** When the Action has been enacted, schedule a brief session (often within the next regular body meeting) to reflect: *what did we learn? what did the Lord show us in the walking? what is being called next?* The reflection closes the discernment-action loop and prepares the body for subsequent group-mission cycles.

## Materials
• A quiet room appropriate for sustained corporate work.

• Scripture (each member can have a Bible or shared printed text of the core scriptural cluster).

• Notepaper for members who journal; corporate work itself is verbal, not written.

• Plan for the practical implementation of the Action (calendar, capacity, communication with any external parties involved) once the Action is articulated.

## Time
• Each session typically runs 75–120 minutes.

• The multi-session pacing typically spans 4–10 weeks for a full four-session cycle.

• The Action itself runs across whatever time horizon the body articulates (often 2–12 weeks for first Actions).

• A brief reflection session after the Action's completion adds 30–60 minutes.
