# B17 PIES Communication and Clearing Model

**V2 anchor:** V2.Exp8 refined (Container — clearing-method refinement of condition C, Clear). Substantive cross-references: V2.Exp8B (The Session Liturgy — B17 operates within the clearings sub-component); V2.Exp2 + V2.Exp2A (Emotional Knots + False Self — story-externalization); V2.Exp7A (Discernment of Voices — spiritual-quadrant work); V2.Exp0 (Tool Import Discipline); V1.Exp2 refined (deliberate attending-and-choosing — anthropological grounding for story-externalization).

**Companion deliverable to:** *IJH_Vol2_Phase2_B17_PIES.md* (Cycle 3, Track 3).

## What this protocol does
PIES (Physical / Intellectual / Emotional / Spiritual) is a four-quadrant communication and clearing model that supplies operational clearing-protocol structure for the clearings sub-component of the session liturgy (V2.Exp8B) and for any clearing work that surfaces in the body's practice. PIES is one of three core clearing-method options under V2.Exp8 refined condition C (Clear), alongside A1 MKP Clearing and A2 NVC OFNR. The Companion's discernment selects which method fits the body and the friction.

PIES's two distinctive operational moves beyond A1 and A2: the four-quadrant frame (separating Physical observation from Intellectual interpretation from Emotional response from Spiritual intention), and the story-externalization move (*"the story I told myself was..."*) that operationalizes V1.Exp2 refined and V2.Exp2A core anthropology at the clearing-method level. The protocol's authority is the Lord's; the clearing is the Lord's reconciling work in the body; the participant's structured speech is the faculty through which the work happens. *The clearing is the Lord's.*

**When to use it**

• **As the ****core**** clearing-method when the friction's interpretive layer is operationally significant.** PIES's four-quadrant frame and story-externalization move are most operationally helpful when the friction involves substantive interpretation work — where the participant's story-telling about what happened is doing as much work as what happened.

• **For new bodies developing clearing competency.** PIES's explicit four-quadrant structure gives new participants explicit operational language for the clearings work; the structure scaffolds the clearing competency the body develops over its first 4–8 sessions. As the body internalizes the clearings discipline, the Companion may select A1 or A2 for simpler clearings in subsequent practice.

• **For clearings touching the participant's own false-self material.** The story-externalization move is structurally identical to what V2.Exp2A canonizes; PIES is the clearing-method form of the broader false-self work. When a clearing's interpretation layer surfaces material that is more about the participant's own false-self than about the receiving participant, PIES's structure makes that visible.

• **For mixed-gender clearings where the pastoral-pattern framing matters operationally.** The PIES source document carries gender-aware guidelines that Founder Decision 5.1(c) frames as common patterns the Companion can expect rather than rules; PIES is the core clearing-method that carries this framing in core V2 form.

• **At Companion II competency for facilitating PIES clearings in new bodies;** Level I appropriate once the body has internalized the basic PIES form and can run clearings largely under their own discipline with Companion attending.

**When NOT to use it**

• **For simple operational frictions** that are better addressed by direct conversation outside the session-liturgy frame. Not every friction requires a formal clearing; the body's between-session communication discipline often handles operational frictions without formal clearings work.

• **For clearings touching trauma material** that exceeds the clearings sub-component's scope. PIES is a clearings discipline, not a trauma-processing protocol; if the *story I told myself* move surfaces trauma material requiring substantive therapeutic work, the Companion routes per the routing logic (Companion II / III consultation; A17 family if appropriate; pastoral care or clinical referral as needed). PIES gives the participant operational structure for naming what is happening; it does not process the underlying trauma.

• **For clearings that have escalated to relational rupture** beyond the body's capacity to hold in the clearings component. When friction has accumulated past the clearings-component's structural scope, the Companion's discernment may move the work to a separate dyadic conversation outside the session-liturgy frame, with Companion III or pastoral support.

• **As a substitute for the substantive work-component.** Clearings clear what would interfere with the work; clearings do not substitute for the work itself. When the clearings component runs long and threatens the work-component's time, the Companion's discernment governs — sometimes the clearings work is what the gathering needs (and the work-component yields); sometimes the clearings work is preventing the gathering from accessing the work the body needs (and the Companion redirects).

• **In acute pastoral or clinical crisis.** PIES is a clearings discipline; acute crisis warrants stepping outside the session-liturgy frame per the V2.Exp8B Companion-discernment guidance.

## The protocol
PIES runs in dyadic form (one participant clearing with another specific participant) when the friction is between two specific people; in group form (participant addressing the body) when the friction concerns the body's broader practice. The five-move sequence holds in both forms. The receiving participant attends per V2.Exp8 refined witness-discipline (paragraph 3); the receiving participant may respond briefly after the speaking participant has completed all five moves.

**Move 1 — Physical (P): *When I saw / heard / observed...***

**Companion framing:** *"Begin with the Physical quadrant. Name what you actually observed — what you saw, what you heard, the raw sensory data. Not what it meant, not how you interpreted it, not how you felt about it. Just what happened, as concretely as you can describe it. 'When I saw...' or 'When you said...' Keep it brief and specific."*

**Speaking participant work:** Name the observable event in specific concrete terms. *"When I saw you check your phone three times during my Hot Seat work last week..."* or *"When you said in our group chat that the men's-work weekend wasn't your priority..."* Resist the urge to interpret here; the next move is for interpretation. The Physical move is the ground on which the rest of the clearing operates.

**Receiving participant work:** Attend. Do not respond yet. The receiving participant's discipline is to hear the Physical move as accurate description of what happened, not as accusation. The receiving participant may have a different perception of what happened; this is held for the response after all five moves are complete.

**Companion discernment.** If the Physical move includes interpretation, the Companion gently redirects: *"Let me ask you to come back to just what you observed — the interpretation is the next move."* Keeping the Physical move clean of interpretation makes the Intellectual move's externalization land.

**Move 2 — Intellectual (I): *The story I told myself was...***

**Companion framing:** *"Now the Intellectual quadrant — the story you told yourself about what you observed. This is the interpretation, the meaning-making, the narrative your soul constructed about what happened. Name it externally — 'the story I told myself was...' — so we can attend to it as a story rather than as the truth of what happened. The story may be accurate; it may be partially accurate; it may be mostly false; it may serve some need in you that has nothing to do with the actual person you're clearing with. The story-externalization move makes the story visible so the clearing work can address it. This is what V1.Exp2 refined and V2.Exp2A name — the deliberate attending to the story the soul has told."*

**Speaking participant work:** Name the story externally. *"The story I told myself was that you didn't value the men's-work because you weren't fully present to my Hot Seat..."* or *"The story I told myself was that you don't see this body as your spiritual home..."* The externalization move requires deliberate attending — System-1 default is to experience the story as reality; the deliberate-attending discipline names the story as story.

**Receiving participant work:** Attend. Do not respond yet. The receiving participant is hearing the story-as-story; the discipline is not to confirm or refute it, but to attend to what the story names about the speaking participant's experience.

**Companion discernment.** The story-externalization is the clearing's substantive operational work. If the speaking participant cannot externalize — if the story is being narrated as direct truth-claim rather than as story — the Companion gently models: *"Try saying it as 'the story I told myself' — naming it externally as one interpretation among possible interpretations. Even if you think it's accurate, the externalization is what makes this clearing work rather than escalating."* The discipline matters; without it, PIES becomes accusation wearing a four-quadrant frame.

**Move 3 — Emotional (E): *I felt...***

**Companion framing:** *"The Emotional quadrant — what you felt as a result of the interpretation. Not what you think, not what you decided to do about it — what arose in you emotionally. Name the feeling specifically: hurt, angry, dismissed, ashamed, sad, anxious. Keep it brief; the feeling is named, not unpacked."*

**Speaking participant work:** Name the spontaneous emotional response to the story. *"I felt dismissed..."* or *"I felt grief..."* or *"I felt angry..."* The Emotional move is brief; the work is naming, not analysis.

**Receiving participant work:** Attend. The Emotional move informs the receiving participant about the impact of the friction on the speaking participant.

**Companion discernment.** Discernment-of-voices per V2.Exp7A may operate here — the Emotional response may be the spontaneous Spirit-level response, may be the wound's response, may be the false-self's response. The Companion's discernment governs whether the emotional naming is operationally complete or whether something deeper is asking for attention. In most clearings, the Emotional move is direct and brief; in clearings touching deeper material, the Companion may route to A8 or other work after the clearing completes.

**Move 4 — Relational impact: *How this affects our relationship...***

**Companion framing:** *"Now name how this experience affects your relationship — both at the body level and at the dyadic level. What has shifted between us as a result of what happened and the story I told myself about it? This is operationally important because it locates the clearing in the relational fabric, not just in the speaking participant's internal experience."*

**Speaking participant work:** Name the relational impact. *"What's shifted between us is that I'm holding back from bringing real work into the room when you're here..."* or *"It's affected my trust that this body is a place where my work is taken seriously..."* The move is honest naming of relational consequence; not threat, not ultimatum.

**Receiving participant work:** Attend. The relational-impact naming is what makes the clearing necessary — without it, the friction can be treated as the speaking participant's internal experience that the receiving participant doesn't need to address. With it, the friction is named as relational, and the body's response is operationally required.

**Companion discernment.** Where relational impact escalates beyond the clearings component's scope (significant rupture; trust-failure that exceeds the body's holding capacity; sin-channel material between the participants), the Companion routes per the routing logic — Companion II / III consultation, V2.Exp4 refined Confession if sin-channel material is surfacing, pastoral care outside the session-liturgy if needed.

**Move 5 — Spiritual (S): *What I want / what I'm asking the Lord for...***

**Companion framing:** *"Now the Spiritual quadrant. This is where the clearing's locus-reversal lands — what you want, what you're asking the Lord for in this clearing. Not what you want the other to do; not what you want as your due; what you discern the Lord is asking for the body's good in this friction. The work is intention-discernment per V2.Exp7A — what is the Spirit asking for here? Sometimes it's specific: 'I want you to know that your phone-checking affected my work, and I'm asking the Lord to clear what's between us so we can be present to each other in the next gathering.' Sometimes it's broader: 'I'm asking the Lord to deepen our trust in this body.' The discipline is intention-discernment rather than needs-advocacy."*

**Speaking participant work:** Name what is being asked for, as discerned intention rather than as self-advocacy. The move is the clearing's locus-reversal in action — the request is what the participant discerns the Lord asking for, not what the participant is securing for themselves.

**Receiving participant work:** Attend. The Spiritual move closes the speaking participant's five-move sequence. The receiving participant may now respond.

**Companion discernment.** If the Spiritual move drifts into self-advocacy (*"I want you to stop doing X"* without the Lord's-good framing; *"I want you to apologize"* as ultimatum rather than as discerned-for-the-body's-good intention), the Companion's pastoral judgment may name the drift: *"Let me ask — is this what you discern the Lord asking for the body's good, or what you're advocating for yourself? Sometimes those overlap; sometimes they're different."* The pastoral redirect is gentle; the V2.Exp7A discernment discipline operates.

**The receiving participant's response**

**Companion framing:** *"[Receiving participant], you've attended to all five moves. Now respond briefly. The discipline is not to argue with the story — the story is the speaking participant's, not yours to confirm or refute. The discipline is to attend to what the clearing surfaced, to name what you heard, to respond to the Spiritual-**quadrant request as discerned-for-the-body's-good rather than as ultimatum, and to offer what you can offer honestly. The receiving response may be brief; the work is not to fix the friction but to receive what was named."*

**Receiving participant work:** Respond briefly. The core form: *"I heard you say [recap the substance of the five moves]. What I can offer is [honest naming of what is possible from where the receiving participant stands]. I'm with you in asking the Lord for [reframe the spiritual-quadrant request in the receiving participant's voice]."* The response is honest, brief, and oriented toward the body's good rather than toward defense of the receiving participant's prior actions.

**Companion discernment.** The receiving response is operationally where the clearing either lands or doesn't. If the receiving participant's response is defensive, dismissive, or argumentative with the story, the clearing has not landed; the Companion may pause and name what is happening, or may close the clearings sub-component and route to a separate conversation outside the session-liturgy frame. If the receiving response is honest and oriented to the body's good, the clearing has landed; the body proceeds to the work-component.

## Companion notes
**On the pastoral-pattern framing for gender-aware material (Decision 5.1(c)).** The PIES source document carries specific guidance for women-clearing-with-women patterns, men-clearing-with-men patterns, and mixed-gender-clearing patterns. Per Decision 5.1(c), these are framed as *common patterns the Companion can expect rather than rules*. Pastoral patterns the Companion may notice operationally (without these becoming core rules):

• **In women-clearing-with-women patterns,** the clearing often carries strong emphasis on connection-and-repair; the Spiritual-quadrant request tends toward relational restoration; the Emotional move may need more time and space than the Companion's default pacing accommodates.

• **In men-clearing-with-men patterns,** the clearing often carries emphasis on boundary-setting and clarity; the Spiritual-quadrant request tends toward operational specificity; the Intellectual move may need more time as men's clearings often touch interpretive material that operates without verbal articulation in default communication.

• **In mixed-gender clearings,** both emphases may be operative; the Companion's discernment is particularly important; the pastoral-pattern framing acknowledges that the body's gender composition shapes the clearing's operational dynamics without legislating specific moves.

These are pastoral patterns the Companion may notice and respond to; they are not rules. The Companion's continuing practitioner-level workshopping refines how PIES operates across BoB and FotH bodies; the corpus is open to refinement of these patterns through continuing practice.

**On the Companion-as-witness discipline****.** Throughout PIES, the Companion's stance is witness rather than reframing. The naming work is the participants' work; the Companion holds the room while the naming happens; the Companion offers the Lord's presence rather than the Companion's interpretation; the Companion defers any pastoral redirection to a subsequent conversation. This discipline applies particularly in the story-externalization move (the story is the speaking participant's to name; the Companion does not assess its accuracy) and in the receiving response (the receiving participant's response is what it is; the Companion does not edit it).

**On routing clearings that exceed scope.** The clearings sub-component has structural scope; some material may exceed it. Routing logic:

• *Trauma material surfacing in the story-externalization* → Companion II / III consultation; A17 family if appropriate for the body's practice; pastoral care or clinical referral as needed.

• *Sin-channel material between the participants* → V2.Exp4 refined Confession; A6 Fifth Step if substantive disclosure work is required.

• *Significant relational rupture exceeding the body's holding capacity* → separate dyadic conversation outside the session-liturgy frame; Companion III or pastoral support.

• *Internal-voice material surfacing in the story (the story is being driven by an internal accusing or shaming voice, not by genuine observation)* → A8 Transforming a Shaming Voice may be appropriate as separate work; the V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices discipline operates.

The Companion's discernment of when to route is itself a Companion II / III competency; the clearings sub-component is where the discernment work happens in the session-liturgy structure.

**On clearings between Companion and participant.** When the friction is between the Companion and a participant, the Companion's discernment governs whether to: (a) run the clearing with a different Companion holding the room; (b) defer to a separate conversation outside the session-liturgy frame; or (c) name the friction publicly without running formal PIES (which can produce a strange dynamic when the Companion is participant in the clearing the Companion would otherwise hold). Option (a) is core default; the body should have multiple Companion-competent members so option (a) is operationally available.

**On the practice as developing over the body's lifecycle.** New bodies develop clearings competency over their first 4–8 sessions; PIES's explicit structure scaffolds the development. As the body internalizes the discipline, clearings may run shorter (less Companion framing needed; participants run the moves with internalized discipline) and may run between-session as well as in the clearings sub-component. The session-liturgy clearings remain core at the front of each session; between-session clearings supplement rather than replace the structural sub-component.

## Materials
• **None required** beyond what the session-liturgy already provides (circle seating; quiet space). Speaking and receiving participants face each other in the circle when running dyadic PIES; the Companion is positioned to attend to both.

• **A small visual aid (optional but useful for new bodies)** — a printed card or simple diagram showing the four quadrants and the five-move sequence can support new participants learning the PIES discipline. As the body internalizes the form, the visual aid is no longer needed.

## Time
• **Dyadic clearing:** 5–15 minutes typical, including the receiving response. Longer for complex clearings (15–25 minutes) where the story-externalization work is substantive.

• **Group form clearing (participant addresses the body):** 8–20 minutes typical. The body's response may be from multiple participants; the Companion holds the pacing.

• **Multiple clearings in the clearings sub-component:** The clearings sub-component of the session liturgy typically holds 10–25 minutes (per V2.Exp8B Component 3 timing). When multiple clearings are needed, the Companion's discernment governs sequencing and time-allocation; the work-component may yield time if clearings work is what the body needs in the gathering.
