# A32 Hot Seat Protocol (with Mini Hot Seat as named variant)

## Hot Seat Protocol (V2.Exp9 — Community as Amplifier, with cross-refs to V2.Exp9A, V2.Exp2A, V2.Exp7A)
## What this protocol does
Hot Seat is the core IJH community-amplification protocol for substantive periodic focused-attention work on one group member. The participant sits in the center of a U-shape circle (typically on a designated pillow or chair that physically marks the position); other group members deliver structured feedback in two rounds. The **shadow round** (first) — each group member names what they see in shadow for the participant: what is hidden, over-functioning, avoided, defended. The **gold round** (second) — each group member names what they see in gold: what is alive, what is gift, what is calling, what the Lord seems to be doing. The participant receives both rounds without responding mid-round; the participant's own discernment work on what was received happens after the practice closes.

The protocol's primary downstream effect is V2.Exp9 amplification at the level of the individual member — the body's collective Spirit-witness produces hearing that the member cannot reach alone. The secondary downstream effects are V2.Exp9A corporate-witnessed declared identity (the gold round operationalizes this directly), V2.Exp2A shame-knot clearance (the body's witness loosens shame's grip in ways the member cannot reach alone), and V2.Exp7A discernment-of-voices development (receiving feedback under V2.Exp7A testing is itself a hearing-faculty exercise).

**Two operational variants:**

• **Full Hot Seat** (the primary form): periodic deep-work session, ~45–60 minutes, two distinct rounds. Deployed for substantive periodic focus — once or twice in a man's active group life, or once a year in mature group rhythm.

• **Mini Hot Seat**: shorter form, ~15–25 minutes, often a single combined shadow-and-gold round per group member. Deployed when a present-moment issue in a regular session warrants the body's structured witness but does not need a full session.

**Personal Ownership move** (C5 step 11): a refinement where each group member, after giving their shadow or gold feedback, says *"I said you had X and I own that I have X too"* — operationalizing Matthew 7:3–5 (log-and-speck) at the feedback-giving level. **Pending founder decision** on core placement (Founder-attention flag 1 in the Phase 2 entry); the Part VI entry treats it as **Companion II variant** provisionally.

**When to use it**

• **For substantive periodic focused-attention work on one group member.** The standard deployment cadence in BoB practice: once or twice in a man's active group life for full Hot Seat; once or twice annually in mature group rhythm; whenever the body discerns that a member is in the kind of mission-decision or struggle that warrants the body's witness.

• **As a shame-clearing tool when other clearing tools have not reached the depth.** Hot Seat is the primary V2.Exp9 community-amplification approach to shame-knot work; the body's witness reaches shame that the member's individual work cannot reach alone.

• **For identity-affirmation when V2.Exp9A corporate witness is needed at substantive scale.** Gold round operationalizes V2.Exp9A directly; when a member needs the body's witness to a calling or identity-claim that has been emerging, Hot Seat is the structured form.

• **For mission-decision work when discernment requires the body's input.** Per the 2008 ASC Manual §5.1, Hot Seat is the core accountability tool when a member is in an active mission decision or struggle — *"focused group attention with the group bringing their best discernment, support, and challenge to bear."*

• **Mini Hot Seat: when a present-moment issue surfaces in a regular session.** A member shows up to session carrying something specific; the group discerns the body's witness is the right next-move; Mini Hot Seat operationalizes the shorter focused form.

• **Level I Companion competency for the basic protocol** with the *§5.1(c) FotH workshopping awareness* note. The structural moves (U-shape setup, shadow-then-gold sequencing, feedback discipline, role-clarification close) are operationally simple. **Level II Companion competency for the Personal Ownership variant** if deployed (per pending founder decision on core placement). Level II also for the FotH adaptation work in women's-group or mixed-gender-group settings (the discernment of how the practice lands differently is itself a Level II competency).

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

• **When the container's V2.Exp8 four conditions are not adequately established.** Hot Seat is high-stakes for the participant — particularly in the shadow round. Without sufficient container (the four V2.Exp8 conditions: confidentiality, willingness, focused attention, and the formally-marked structure), the practice produces participant-harm rather than formation. The Companion's discernment is whether the container is sufficient; if not, defer and route to container-strengthening work first.

• **When the participant is in acute shame or psychological crisis.** The shadow round in particular can deepen acute shame rather than clearing it. If the participant is in a present-moment crisis state (active suicidal ideation, acute psychiatric instability, fresh acute trauma), defer Hot Seat to a later time. The discernment of *when not to run* is non-optional. Pastoral support, individual work, and possibly professional referral may be appropriate intermediary steps before Hot Seat is workable.

• **When the body's collective state is in conflict or unresolved interpersonal tension.** Hot Seat operates on the assumption that the body is in functional unity; if group members carry unresolved conflict with the participant, the shadow round becomes cover for aggression rather than faithful wound. Run clearings (A1 MKP Clearing or A2 NVC OFNR) first; resolve the interpersonal tension; then Hot Seat is workable. Failure mode is real and produces both participant-harm and group damage.

• **When a group member has unaddressed shadow material toward the participant.** A man with significant projected material on the participant can use the shadow round as cover for naming what is actually his own. The specific-feedback-and-*"I see you as"* discipline is the safeguard, but it is not sufficient when the namer has not done sufficient prior work on his own material. The Companion's discernment before each round: are the group members in a state where they can give feedback as Spirit-witness, or are some carrying material that would make the feedback projective?

• **Running shadow round before gold round.** The reverse ordering produces the participant who experiences subsequent shadow as betrayal-after-encouragement. Hold the core sequencing.

• **Letting the gold round drift into flattery / niceness.** Gold is not what people like about the participant; gold is what the Spirit is showing the body about the participant's giftings and callings. The framing language matters: *"What is the Spirit showing us about [participant] in the gold register?"* not *"What do you appreciate about [participant]?"* The framing prevents the drift.

• **Letting the shadow round drift into aggression-in-Christian-dress.** Shadow is the faithful wound (Prov 27:6) that operates in love (Eph 4:15) for restoration (Gal 6:1). Shadow is not opportunity for honest-disagreement-without-filter; shadow is structured witness to what the Spirit is showing for the participant's formation. The Companion enforces the discipline.

• **Compressing the timing under social pressure.** A full Hot Seat with five or six brothers in the circle typically runs 45–60 minutes; new Companions often compress under the social pressure of the heaviness in the room after shadow, or the impulse to wrap up after gold. Resist the compression; the space between speakers is part of what the protocol does.

• **Importing the Personal Ownership move when the namers have not done sufficient prior work.** The mirror-passing operationalizes Matt 7:3–5 cleanly only when the namer has actually done the log-removing work on the shadow or gold they are naming. A man who has not done this work cannot honestly own the same shadow he is naming; the move becomes performative rather than disciplinary. Companion II competency required.

## The protocol — Full Hot Seat (primary form)
The core operational sequence. Steps 1–4 are setup; steps 5–10 are the rounds; step 11 is the optional Personal Ownership move (Companion II variant, pending founder decision); step 12 is closing.

1. **Container setup.**

- Companion: arrange U-shape circle with designated pillow or chair in the center marking the hot seat position. Confirm V2.Exp8 four conditions are met for the session. Mark the container's opening formally per V2.Exp8 refined (Claim 5).

- All participants: take seats. The participant designated for the hot seat takes the center position.

- Discernment: confirm container is sufficient; if not, defer to container-strengthening work first.

2. **Framing.**

- Companion: frame the practice. *"[Participant], we're going to do Hot Seat with you tonight. The brothers are going to give you feedback in two rounds — shadow first, then gold. You'll receive what is offered without responding during the rounds; your discernment work on what was received will happen after we close. We're listening together for what the Spirit is showing the body about you. The brothers are not the source of the feedback; they're the body's voice for what the Spirit is showing."*

- Participant: receive the framing.

- Discernment: hold the source-of-feedback framing consciously — this is the BoB / FotH locus reversal. The brothers are witnesses to the Spirit's witness, not the source of insight.

3. **Statement (one minute).**

- Participant: speak briefly — typically one minute — about what is currently present for them. Mission-decision context, struggle, season they are in. The statement frames what the body is being asked to witness about.

- Discernment: the statement orients the body's listening; the brothers' subsequent feedback is shaped by what the participant has named as the current territory.

4. **Brothers' silent attending.**

- Companion: invite brief silence — typically 30–60 seconds. *"Brothers, let's be still and listen for what the Spirit is showing us about [participant] in the shadow register, the gold register."*

- Brothers: attend in silence. Not formulating responses yet; receiving what is being shown.

- Discernment: the silence is operational, not ceremonial. It is the V2.Exp7 hearing-practice form applied corporately for the moment.

5. **Shadow round.**

- Companion: invite. *"Brothers, in turn — each of you — give [participant] feedback in the shadow register. What is in shadow for him that you see? Remember the discipline: descriptive, specific, behavior-focused; 'I see you as,' not 'you are.'"*

- Brothers in turn (typically going around the U-shape): each gives shadow feedback. ~3 minutes per brother.

- Participant: receive without responding. May make brief notes if helpful.

- Discernment: hold the discipline. If a brother slips into evaluative judgment (*"you are afraid"*) or abstract assessment (*"you have trust issues"*), the Companion may gently re-cue toward descriptive-specific (*"can you say what you see that lets you see that?"*). If a brother slips into aggression-cover or projection-cover, the Companion may need to hold structure firmly.

6. **Space between rounds.**

- Companion: brief silence after shadow round closes — typically 60–90 seconds. *"Let's hold here briefly before we move into gold."*

- All: receive what was offered. The participant is sitting with the shadow material; the brothers are present in witness.

- Discernment: the space is part of the protocol's structure. Compression here typically produces a gold round that lands less cleanly because the shadow has not had time to be received.

7. **Gold round.**

- Companion: invite. *"Brothers, in turn — each of you — give [participant] feedback in the gold register. What is the Spirit showing the body about his gifts, his calling, what the Lord is doing in him? Same discipline: descriptive, specific, 'I see you as.'"*

- Brothers in turn: each gives gold feedback. ~3 minutes per brother.

- Participant: receive without responding.

- Discernment: hold the *V2.Exp9A corporate-witnessed declared identity* framing. Gold is what the Spirit is showing the body, not what people like about the participant. The Companion may need to gently re-cue if a brother drifts toward generic-encouragement or social-comfort. *"What is the Spirit showing about his gift specifically? Be concrete."*

8. **Space after gold round.**

- Companion: brief silence. *"Let's hold here briefly."*

- All: receive what was offered. The participant has received both rounds.

9. **Optional Personal Ownership round — Companion II variant (pending founder decision on ****core**** placement).**

- **If deployed:** Companion invites. *"Brothers, as you gave shadow or gold feedback, pick one thing you said and own it for yourself. Say to whichever brother you choose: 'I said you had X and I own that I have X too.' This is the Matt 7:3–5 discipline."*

- Brothers: each in turn picks one of their feedback statements and owns the same material for themselves, addressed to another brother in the circle (or to the participant, depending on the C5 source's framing).

- Discernment: Companion II competency required. The move serves the work only when brothers have done sufficient prior work on the material they are owning; performative ownership produces a worse practice than not running the move. **Founder-attention flag pending** — this Part VI entry treats the move as variant; core placement (option a standard / option b Companion II variant / option c practitioner-discretion) pending Session 3 flag 1.

10. **Participant's first response (optional).**

- Companion: invite. *"[Participant], do you want to say anything in response now, or hold for your discernment work?"*

- Participant: respond briefly if they want; sometimes the participant names what landed strongly, sometimes the participant holds for later. No requirement.

- Discernment: the participant's discernment work on what was received primarily happens after the protocol closes, not during it; the optional first-response is a brief acknowledgment, not a full processing.

11. **Role-clarification close.**

- Companion: mark the close of the hot seat position. *"[Participant's name], the Hot Seat is complete. The witnessing role we've held is finished; we're back in fellow-pilgrim mode."* The participant rises from the pillow / chair; the structured position ends.

- Discernment: this is the V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-closing applied to the participant's structured listening role. Hold the moment as the marked transition it is.

12. **Container close.**

- Companion: close the session per the standard V2.Exp8 refined marked-closing form. May include corporate-witness affirmation per V2.Exp9A if appropriate. The brothers and the (former) hot-seat participant return to baseline fellowship for the closing.

**The protocol — Mini Hot Seat (named variant)**

A shorter form deployed when a present-moment issue warrants the body's structured witness but does not require a full session. Operationally similar to full Hot Seat with three substantive differences: shorter timing, single combined round, and lighter container-marking.

1. **Setup (brief).**

- Companion: invite the participant to take a designated position (often the same pillow or chair as full Hot Seat; sometimes simply the seat the participant is already in if the session is informal). Brief framing — typically 30 seconds — naming that the brothers are going to give Mini Hot Seat feedback on the present-moment material the participant has just named.

2. **Statement (brief).**

- Participant: name the present-moment material — 30–60 seconds. *"What I'm sitting with tonight is X."*

3. **Combined feedback round.**

- Companion: invite each brother in turn. *"In one to two minutes, give [participant] feedback — both shadow and gold, integrated — on what you see in the present-moment material he's named."*

- Brothers in turn: each gives integrated feedback. ~1–2 minutes per brother.

- Participant: receive without responding.

- Discernment: the integrated round is the operational difference from full Hot Seat. Mini Hot Seat does not separate the rounds because the present-moment material is narrower and the brothers' feedback can hold both registers simultaneously. The shadow-and-gold discipline preserves — what is in shadow, what is in gold — but both are spoken in the same brief feedback.

4. **Brief participant response (optional).**

- Companion: invite. *"[Participant], anything you want to say in response now?"*

- Participant: brief response if they want.

5. **Role-clarification close.**

- Companion: mark the close briefly. *"Mini Hot Seat complete; [participant], back to fellow-pilgrim mode."*

Mini Hot Seat may be deployed multiple times in a single session if multiple members surface present-moment material warranting witness. Full Hot Seat is typically the focal practice of a session (single deployment); Mini Hot Seat is more flexible in session-frequency.

**Closing and de-roling**

**Role-clarification close** per step 11 (full Hot Seat) or step 5 (Mini Hot Seat). The de-roling per A14 / refined V2.Exp8 applies in attenuated form — the participant's transition out of the structured listening role is marked formally; the brothers' transition out of the structured witnessing role is implicit in the practice's close. The role-clarification close is operationally smaller than A14's reciprocal formula (no figure has been held) and smaller than A29's de-roling close-line (no pilgrim/coach role-distribution); functionally analogous to both in marking the V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked-closing of structured role-distribution.

The container's overall close per V2.Exp8 refined follows the role-clarification close; the corporate-witness affirmation per V2.Exp9A may apply if appropriate to the session's work.

## Companion notes
The source-of-feedback framing is where to put your attention as you grow into leading Hot Seat. The structural moves (U-shape setup, shadow-then-gold sequencing, feedback discipline, role-clarification close) are operationally simple after a few reps. What separates a Hot Seat that does its work from one that runs through the structure without producing formation is whether the Companion's framing positions the body's feedback as Spirit-witness or as group-collective-sight. The two framings sound similar in casual hearing but operate in different registers; hold the BoB / FotH register consciously, especially in moments when the men's-work-tradition framing would feel more natural.

The discernment of *when not to run* Hot Seat is one of the most important Companion competencies for this protocol. The list of contraindications in the section above is real — container insufficiency, participant in acute crisis, unresolved interpersonal tension among brothers, group members carrying unaddressed shadow toward the participant. A Companion who runs Hot Seat under any of these conditions produces participant-harm and group damage. The discernment matures with experience; new Companions should err on the side of deferral when the discernment is unclear.

The shadow round's discipline (descriptive, specific, behavior-focused, *"I see you as"* not *"you are"*) is the safeguard against the practice drifting toward aggression-in-Christian-dress. Hold the discipline consciously; gently re-cue brothers when their feedback slips into evaluative or abstract registers. The discipline is not a stylistic preference; it is what makes the practice produce faithful wounds (Prov 27:6) rather than careless words.

The gold round's framing (the Spirit-witness-to-gift-and-calling register, not the social-comfort register) is the safeguard against drift toward flattery. The Companion's question-framing — *"what is the Spirit showing the body about his gifts and calling?"* — orients the brothers' listening. Hold the framing consciously.

The Personal Ownership move requires Companion II maturity if deployed. Read the C5 source document (the mirror-passing step 11) before facilitating; understand the Matt 7:3–5 framing; assess whether the brothers in your group have done sufficient prior work on their own shadow and gold material for the mirror-passing to operate as discipline rather than performance. Founder-attention flag 1 in the Phase 2 entry raises the core-placement question; this Part VI entry treats the move as Companion II variant pending founder decision.

The role-clarification close in step 11 is operationally small but matters. The participant has been in a structured listening role; the brothers have been in a structured witnessing role; both transitions out are marked formally per V2.Exp8 refined. Companions who treat the close as administrative housekeeping communicate that the structured roles were not real; the body absorbs that communication. Hold the close as the formally-marked transition it is.

## FotH adaptation guidance — §5.1(c) ongoing-workshopping
(*Per founder decision Session 3, May 12, 2026: §5.1(c) option (b) posture — **core** name preserved, gendered-history acknowledged in V2 prose, FotH adaptation as ongoing-workshopping in Companion notes. This sub-section is the workshopping layer.*)

The term *Hot Seat* and the U-shape circle structure carry MKP / NWTA men's-work tradition; the practice is BoB-developmental in its current Christian-frame form. The FotH work of adapting the practice for women's groups and mixed-gender groups is in active development; what follows is workshopping guidance based on current FotH practice, not core prescription.

For women's-group facilitation: practitioners report that the basic structure operates well with attention to two things. **First**, the framing language around shadow can land with different resonance — *"what is in shadow for you that we see"* is generally workable; *"what is hidden"* can produce shame-collapse more readily in women's-group settings than in men's-group settings, depending on the group's developmental phase. The Companion may want to hold the shadow-naming closer to behavior-specifics and further from internal-state-claims initially, deepening as the group's container matures. **Second**, the gold round's V2.Exp9A corporate-witness framing operates strongly in women's-group settings; the body's witness to a sister's gift and calling can be one of the most theologically dense work the group does together, particularly where the church culture has been thin on speaking-identity-into-women.

For mixed-gender-group facilitation: practitioners report two adaptations to date. **First**, the U-shape and pillow positioning can carry cross-gender complications in groups where members are not in established trust; the Companion may want to use a simpler chair-positioning and skip the pillow for early-phase mixed-gender groups, returning to the core structure as the container matures. **Second**, the Personal Ownership move (if deployed) requires more discernment about who owns what across gender lines; the namer's owning is most clean when within-gender, and cross-gender owning requires sufficient trust that the move serves the work rather than introducing complications.

The §5.1(c) workshopping posture means: these adaptations are practitioner-reports from active FotH practice, held provisionally; the core BoB form is what is documented above; subsequent revisions to this Part VI entry may update the workshopping guidance as the FotH practice matures.
