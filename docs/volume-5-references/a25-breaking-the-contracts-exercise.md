# A25 Breaking the Contracts Exercise

## Breaking the Contracts Exercise (V2.Exp9 — Community as Amplifier; cross-reference to V2.Exp6 Tool Map and V2.Exp2B The Adversary's Role)
## What this protocol does
The Breaking the Contracts Exercise is a Companion-led corporate spiritual-warfare ritual in which each participant in turn formally renounces a *contract with the enemy* — a standing interior agreement the participant entered (typically as a child, typically during specific wound moments, typically unconsciously) about who he is, what he is worth, what he is permitted to do or be, and how he is bound to the family system. The ritual operates through written-contract formulation, public reading-aloud, embodied paper-burning, optional anger-release, corporate prayer-of-agreement in Jesus's name, parent-honoring scripture reading, and prayer for healing — repeated for each participant in turn, closed with shared celebration.

The protocol operates under V2.Exp2B (The Adversary's Role) at full operational intensity. The protocol's framework names the participant's wounds as targets of the enemy's specific messaging at moments of vulnerability; the contracts as the binding interior agreements that resulted from receiving that messaging; the renunciation as the formal loosing of the binding through the corporate authority of Jesus's name (Matt 18:18–20). The corporate-prayer-of-agreement structure is operationally constitutive — a man alone cannot do this work the same way; the body's collective renunciation in Jesus's name is part of the mechanism the protocol depends on.

The protocol is the corpus's most careful entry and carries three named risks: disinhibition (the felt experience mistaken for completion), demonization (drift toward attributing every wound to demonic agency), and false-memory (the ritual's group energy producing confabulated content). The Companion II / III competency requirement, the explicit boundary-conditions, and the supplementary individual TPM work in the weeks after are the core disciplines that address the risks. A25 deployed without these disciplines can produce experiences that look powerful in the moment but leave the participant in worse pastoral position than before the protocol began.

A25 is the core home for the corpus's spiritual-warfare-and-renunciation work. Future Phase 2 work in spiritual-warfare-adjacent territory will reference A25 as the core worked instance.

**When to use it**

• **When a participant has surfaced contract-material that has not cleared through other protocols.** Standard carpet-style work (A17 family), confession work (A6 / V2.Exp4), discernment-of-voices work (V2.Exp7A via A24), Bilgere protocols, and TPM (V2.Exp3) have addressed downstream knots; what remains is an upstream binding agreement that the downstream tools have not dissolved. A25 is the appropriate protocol when contract-material is the operational layer.

• **In a body whose container is mature.** V2.Exp8 conditions must be at full strength; the witness-discipline (V2.Exp8 paragraph 3 Council-canonized) must be operational; the body's corporate-prayer life must be settled.

• **When the participant has substantial prior containment work.** A25's surfacing is operationally intense; the participant needs the operational maturity to hold what surfaces without fragmenting. Typical readiness signals: substantial carpet-style work (A17 family) completed; A24 Blessing Receiving completed (the participant has discernment-of-voices maturity sufficient to recognize the contract's source); pastoral relationships in place to hold the post-protocol consolidation work.

• **At a body's developmental milestone where multiple participants have surfaced parallel contract-material.** A25 is operationally optimal as a corporate ritual with six to eight men carrying their own contracts; smaller groups can run A25 but the corporate-witness intensity is reduced. A body whose members have done parallel formation work in adjacent territory often surfaces parallel contract-material that benefits from corporate-ritual address.

• **At an extended-format gathering** — a retreat, a multi-day intensive, a substantial seasonal moment in the body's rhythm. A25 requires three to four hours of focused work plus preparation and celebration; the gathering format supplies the time and the spiritual posture the protocol needs.

• **Companion II / III competency required** for facilitation. Stated as gating requirement. The competency includes: discernment for the three named risks; framing-language discipline for the spiritual-warfare framing and the source-reversal at the corporate-prayer-of-agreement; boundary-conditions discipline for contraindications; witness-discipline; post-protocol pastoral attention discipline.

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

This section is more elaborate than other Part VI entries because A25 carries higher risk than other Cycle 1 protocols. Each contraindication is non-negotiable.

• **With participants in severe trauma without prior containment work.** A25's surfacing exceeds the participant's containment capacity if upstream A17 family work or analogous containment has not been done. The pastoral pre-flight: has this participant done sufficient work to hold what the ritual will surface? If not, route to containment work first; revisit A25 in a subsequent cycle.

• **With participants in active psychiatric instability.** Acute depressive episode; psychotic features; manic episode; recent psychiatric hospitalization; active eating-disorder crisis; active substance-use relapse. A25's ritual intensity is contraindicated for participants in acute crisis. Route to professional care; revisit A25 only after stability has been operationally established for a sustained period.

• **With participants in active suicidality.** Non-negotiable. A25 is not for participants whose lives are in immediate risk.

• **With participants whose dissociative tendencies have not been worked.** A25 requires sustained integrated attention through the writing, the reading-aloud, the burning, and the receiving of the corporate prayer. Dissociative fragmentation under this load is documented; the participant may complete the ritual psychologically intact but operationally disintegrated. Route to integration work first.

• **With participants for whom the *****contract***** framing produces theological confusion about what was made in good faith with parents or pastors.** Some participants resist the renunciation framing because their parents were doing the best they could, or their pastor was teaching what he genuinely believed, or the family-system shaping was unintentional. The Companion's discernment: does the contract framing fit the specific material the participant has surfaced, or does it produce framing-mismatch that interferes with the work? When mismatch, the appropriate move is to address the specific material through different protocols (V2.Exp4 confession; A17 wound-channel work; V2.Exp7A discernment work) without forcing the contract framing.

• **In a body whose container is shaky.** V2.Exp8 conditions are non-negotiable. A body whose members are distracted, whose corporate-prayer life is not operational, whose witness-discipline is not mature — does not provide the corporate-amplification structure A25 depends on. Companion's pre-flight: is the body actually ready?

• **Without the witness-discipline operational in the body.** The body's role during each participant's renunciation is *witness* — present, attending, silently praying, corporately confirming at the corporate-prayer-of-agreement moment. The witness-discipline (V2.Exp8 paragraph 3 Council-canonized) is non-negotiable; a body that interprets, comments, evaluates, or pastorally counsels in the moment forecloses on the work. Confirm operationally before deploying.

• **Substituting felt-experience for interior change.** The most consequential failure mode at the conceptual level. The ritual produces a strong felt experience; the participant (and sometimes the Companion) may mistake the felt experience for the completion of interior change. The core position: A25 marks the beginning of work; subsequent formation consolidates what the ritual begins. A participant who comes out of A25 and treats the renunciation as settled — without the supplementary TPM work, the post-protocol pastoral attention, the ongoing formation consolidation — has half-done the work. The Companion's framing makes this explicit at the close: *the binding has been loosed in Jesus's name; what you carry forward is the operational possibility of living without the contract's governance, which is operationally different from the lived reality. The next weeks of formation matter.*

• **Drifting toward demonization framing without discipline.** The "contract with the enemy" language can drift toward attributing every interior wound to demonic agency. The Companion's discipline at the core framing: the contract metaphor names how internalized lies operate as binding interior agreements; not a literal transaction with a personal demon at every contract's moment. Some interior wounds are demonic in origin and require renunciation as part of their clearance; many are not. A Companion who drifts toward universal-demonic-attribution produces a different protocol than A25 — closer to deliverance-ministry framing — and imports an anthropology the corpus does not endorse.

• **Producing confabulated contract-content.** The ritual format's group energy plus the expectation of a written contract can produce content that does not match the participant's actual originating wound. Signs of confabulation: the participant's contract reads as composite-template content rather than specific to the participant's actual history; the participant cannot specify the age, the moment, or the figure with operational specificity; the cancellation reads as performance rather than as renunciation of something the participant has actually been carrying. The Companion's discernment governs; supplementary individual TPM work after the group ritual is the core means of testing what the ritual surfaced.

• **Deploying without the supplementary post-protocol work.** A25 is incomplete without the post-protocol cycle. The Companion's pastoral attention in the weeks after the ritual; the supplementary individual TPM work; the body's ongoing prayer for the participant — these are part of A25's full operational footprint, not optional extras. A facilitator who deploys A25 and considers the work done at the celebration close has half-deployed the protocol.

## The protocol
The protocol runs in roughly three to four hours of focused ritual time plus preparation, sequencing for each participant in turn, and the celebration close. A group of six to eight men is operationally optimal. The protocol assumes the body has done substantial prior formation work and that participants have surfaced contract-material through upstream protocols that the A25 cycle is now formally addressing.

**Pre-flight preparation (the weeks before)**

1. **Companion II / III confirmation.** The facilitator confirms his own competency and his discernment about which participants are ready for A25. If uncertain, consults a more senior Companion. Does not proceed under uncertainty.

2. **Participant pre-conversations.** The Companion speaks individually with each participant who will be in the cycle. Confirms readiness; surfaces any contraindications; discusses what contract-material the participant has been carrying; ensures the participant understands what the ritual involves and what supplementary work will follow. Any participant whose pre-conversation surfaces contraindications is gently routed out of the cycle to alternative work.

3. **Body container confirmation.** The Companion confirms V2.Exp8 conditions are at full strength in the body. Witness-discipline is operational.

4. **Practical preparation.** Galvanized trash can or fire-safe burning vessel; matches or lighter; paper and pens for the contract-writing; ax-handle and target (if optional anger-release component is being offered to any participant); celebration materials per body's context; scripture references for the parent-honoring and healing readings.

**Session 1 — Opening, contract-writing**

1. **Container check and opening.** Companion confirms V2.Exp8; opens the session with extended corporate prayer for forgiveness, for the Spirit's presence, for the protection of each participant through the work, for the Lord's authority over what is about to be done. The opening prayer is not perfunctory; it is operationally the establishment of the spiritual posture under which A25 operates.

2. **Framing.** Companion names what is about to happen. *"We are gathered tonight to serve the Lord Jesus Christ. Each of us has been carrying contracts — standing interior agreements that we entered, often as children, during moments of wound. Tonight we are going to formally renounce these contracts in the body, with the Lord's authority and in Jesus's name. The Lord has called us into this work; the body witnesses; the binding is loosed by his authority, not by our will. We will each take time to write our contracts in the format I will give you. Then we will return and one at a time stand before this trash can and read what we have written. The body will pray the corporate prayer of agreement after each renunciation. We will read the scriptures that honor our parents; we will pray for healing; we will close in celebration. This is sacred work; we hold it with care."*

3. **The healing-opening scriptures.** Companion reads Jer 33:6 and Matt 4:23 aloud — framing the protocol from its outset as healing-through-renunciation, not as combat as such.

4. **Contract-writing instructions.** Companion supplies the format. The contract is written in the participant's own hand, in the specific format: *"I, [name], have been in agreement with the enemy's message from age X. What happened: [a brief description — one or two sentences — of the originating wound or pattern]. The message: [the specific lie the wound carried]. What I have felt: [the list of feelings the contract has produced over years]. The cancellation: In the name of Jesus Christ, I renounce this contract; I declare it null and void; I take back the territory that has been the enemy's and return it to the Lord Jesus Christ to whom it has always belonged. Amen."*

5. **Separation for writing.** Men separate to quiet places — different rooms; outdoor space; whatever the venue supports. Plan for 45–75 minutes for the writing. The Companion remains available for individual questions but does not interfere with the writing.

6. **Companion's work during the writing.** The Companion prays. The Companion's spiritual posture during this time supports the body's work. If a participant comes to the Companion with a question or with material that exceeds what can be addressed in the moment, the Companion attends; if necessary, the participant is gently routed out of the cycle and the Companion follows up pastorally afterward.

**Session 2 — The ritual sequence (per participant)**

When all who will write have written, the men gather in the circle around the trash can. The ritual sequence repeats for each participant in turn.

7. **Participant steps forward.** The participant stands at the trash can in the center of the circle, contract in hand. The body sits or stands around the circle in witness position.

8. **Companion frames the moment.** *"[Participant's name], you have written your contract. Take a breath. Read what you have written — what happened; the message; what you have felt; the cancellation. The body witnesses."*

9. **Participant reads aloud.** The participant reads his contract in full, in his own voice, addressing the trash can or addressing the body or addressing the Lord — the participant's choice. The Companion does not script the addressing-direction; the participant speaks what surfaces.

- Discernment: hold the reading. If the participant stumbles or pauses, hold the silence; the participant continues when ready. If the participant cannot read (intense emotion; physical reaction), the Companion attends; sometimes a brief pause is sufficient; sometimes the participant needs to step back and gather himself; sometimes the Companion reads aloud what the participant has written, with the participant present, if the participant cannot vocalize but can hold the work.

10. **The burning.** When the cancellation has been read, the participant places the paper in the trash can and lights it. The body watches the paper burn.

- Discernment: hold the silence. The visible destruction is the embodied form of the renunciation (Acts 19:19); allow it to land. Do not rush past the silence.

11. **The corporate prayer of agreement.** When the paper has burned, the Companion leads the body in the corporate prayer of agreement. *"In the name of Jesus Christ, we as the body of Christ agree with [participant's name] in his renunciation. We declare this contract null and void by the authority of the Lord Jesus Christ. We loose the binding that has been holding [participant's name] and we receive the territory back to the Lord to whom it has always belonged. We thank you, Lord, for what you have just done. Amen."*

- Body: speaks Amen in agreement.

- Discernment: the body's Amen should be substantive, not perfunctory. The Companion may pause before the corporate Amen to allow the body to attend.

12. **Optional ax-handle anger-release (Companion-discernment-gated).** If the Companion has discerned in pre-conversation that this participant carries strong anger-component requiring embodied release, and if the Companion's discernment in the moment confirms readiness, the participant may step to the ax-handle station for anger-pounding. The cautions transfer from A8 / A9: not in isolation from prior containment; not for participants whose energy in the moment is dysregulated; with the body witnessing; for duration the Companion governs. When complete, the participant returns to the circle.

13. **The parent-honoring scripture reading.** Companion reads the parent-honoring scripture cluster — Exod 20:12, Deut 5:16, Matt 15:4, Matt 19:19, Mark 10:19, Eph 6:2 — aloud, slowly. The body witnesses.

- Companion frames the move. *"We renounce the contract that the enemy delivered through wound; we do not renounce the parent through whom the message came. [Participant's name], your parents [or the specific figures] are honored. The contract is broken; the parents are restored to their place. The Batch 2 Council-canonized V2.Exp6A applies — image-bearing dignity preserved through the renunciation."*

- Note: in pastoral application, the Companion does not name the V2.Exp6A claim aloud to participants; the citation is for facilitator-level grounding. The participant-facing framing is the simpler theological language.

14. **Prayer for healing.** Companion leads the body in prayer for the participant's healing. *"Lord, what you have just loosed, please now heal. Bring health and peace where the contract was; show [participant's name] your love in the place that has been bound. We thank you for what you have done. Amen."*

- Body: agrees in Amen.

15. **Repeat for each participant.** Steps 7–14 repeat for each man in turn. The body witnesses each; each man stands for each man. The order may be Companion-arranged (often the man with the lightest material goes first, working toward the heavier material as the body's prayer-posture deepens) or may emerge organically from the body's discernment.

**Session 3 — Closing**

16. **Contract-closure marking.** When all who will have renounced have renounced, the Companion marks the cycle's close. *"Brothers, we have done the work the Lord called us to tonight. The contracts have been written, read, burned. The body has agreed in Jesus's name. The bindings have been loosed; the parents have been honored; the Lord has been thanked for the healing. The contracts have no more authority. You are not under what you renounced tonight. Walk in what has been given you."*

- This is the structural-analog-to-de-roling move: it formally marks the contract's end in the participant's interior life. Skipping it leaves the contract as a residual psychological holding-place. Do not skip.

17. **Celebration.** The body moves to the celebration form appropriate to the body's context. Cigars outside in the historical BoB form; coffee-and-pastries or a shared meal in other contexts; a hymn in another. The celebration is theologically core (Luke 15:6–7 — the rejoicing after the return); the specific form is contextual.

- Discernment: hold the celebration. Allow space for the men to be glad. The Lord has done something tonight that calls for rejoicing.

18. **Companion's pastoral follow-up planning.** Before the body fully disperses, the Companion confirms with each participant when their post-protocol pastoral conversation will occur (typically within 7–14 days). Confirms supplementary individual TPM work where appropriate. Names the body's continued prayer for each participant in the weeks ahead.

19. **Formally-marked session close per V2.Exp8 refined.**

## Companion notes
This section carries more weight than the corresponding section in other Part VI entries because A25 is the most careful protocol in the corpus.

• **The Companion II / III competency requirement is gating, not aspirational.** If you are uncertain whether you are at this competency level for A25, you are not. Consult a more senior Companion. Apprentice with a Companion III deployment as observer before facilitating yourself. Do not run A25 from theoretical training alone.

• **The witness-discipline is non-negotiable.** The body's role during each participant's renunciation is witness — present, attending, silently praying, corporately confirming at the prayer-of-agreement moment. A body member who interprets, comments, evaluates, or pastorally counsels during a renunciation forecloses on the work. If the body has not internalized the witness-discipline (V2.Exp8 paragraph 3 Council-canonized May 2026), do not deploy A25 until they have.

• **The spiritual-warfare framing is the corpus's measured framing.** The contract metaphor names how internalized lies operate as binding interior agreements. Do not drift toward universal-demonic-attribution; do not soften the framing into pure psychological-pattern language. The corpus's V2.Exp2B sits between these extremes and A25 operates in V2.Exp2B's measured space.

• **The corporate-prayer-of-agreement is the operative spiritual move.** The renunciation's authority is the Lord's, invoked through the participant and corporately confirmed by the body in Jesus's name (Matt 18:18–20). The Companion's framing at the prayer-of-agreement moment is non-negotiable. A weakened framing (the body affirms; the participant feels supported; we hope the Lord is in this) imports a different protocol than A25.

• **The post-protocol work is part of the protocol.** Pastoral conversation within 7–14 days; supplementary individual TPM where appropriate; ongoing body prayer in the weeks ahead. A participant who comes out of A25 and is not followed up has been half-served. The Companion's discipline includes the post-protocol attention.

• **For mixed cases (contract-material with substantial sin-channel component):** A25 can hold the renunciation move and route the sin-channel material to subsequent V2.Exp4 confession work (typically in a different session). The Companion's discernment: is the participant's contract primarily wound-channel (the enemy's targeting at the wound; the participant received the message) or primarily sin-channel (the participant's own choices to agree with the enemy's message over time)? Pure wound-channel material runs A25 cleanly; pure sin-channel material may be better-served by V2.Exp4 / A6 directly; mixed cases route both, with the Companion's discernment about which move comes first.

• **The ax-handle anger-release is optional and Companion-discernment-gated.** Some participants need the embodied release; some do not; some should not receive it. The carpet-manual A8 (Transforming a Shaming Voice) and A9 (Resisting Last Man) cautions apply directly. When uncertain, default to without; the participant can receive the embodied release in a different session if it surfaces as needed.

• **The cigars-outside-after celebration is the historical BoB form.** Practitioner-discretion at the specific form. The celebration itself (Luke 15) is core. Substitute fitting forms in mixed-gender, FotH-women's, or culturally-different contexts. The §5.1(c) gender-framing pastoral-pattern applies.

• **Watch for the disinhibition signal in the days after the protocol.** A participant who is unusually elated, who is making large life-decisions in the immediate aftermath, who is treating the renunciation as having settled what has not yet been consolidated — needs Companion attention. The felt experience can produce confidence-without-foundation; the pastoral move is to slow the participant down, hold what has been received, let the consolidation work do its work over time.

• **For women's groups (FotH), the §5.1(c) gender-framing pastoral-pattern applies.** The protocol's structural moves transfer cleanly. The framing language at the spiritual-warfare moments and the parent-honoring scripture cluster transfer without adaptation. Specific framing language at the contract-writing format (e.g., the wounds that women's lives typically carry differently than men's) and the celebration form may benefit from adaptation. Companion judgment governs.

• **For the Companion's own preparation:** A25 deployment activates the Companion's own contract-material in operational-witness mode. The discipline of having renounced one's own contracts (in a prior A25 cycle or analogous work) before facilitating others' A25 cycles is operationally important. This is part of why the Companion II / III competency is the gating requirement.

## Materials
• A room appropriate for sustained corporate ritual with sufficient space for the circle around the burning vessel.

• A galvanized trash can or fire-safe burning vessel positioned in the center of the circle.

• Matches or a lighter.

• Paper (sufficient for each participant to write a one-to-two-page contract) and pens.

• An ax-handle and a target (a wooden pillar; a heavy bag; a floor target) — only if the optional anger-release component is being offered.

• Scripture texts printed or accessible for the parent-honoring cluster (Exod 20:12, Deut 5:16, Matt 15:4, Matt 19:19, Mark 10:19, Eph 6:2) and the healing-opening (Jer 33:6, Matt 4:23).

• Celebration materials per the body's context (cigars in BoB historical form; coffee-and-pastries; shared meal; hymn book; etc.).

• A trained Level II / III Companion as facilitator.

• A body of six to eight men or women per gathering (smaller groups can run A25 but the corporate-witness intensity is reduced; larger groups stretch the time substantially).

## Time
• Pre-flight preparation: weeks before. Individual pre-conversations with each participant; body container confirmation; practical preparation.

• The session itself: 3–4 hours of focused ritual time, plus preparation and celebration.

- Opening and framing: 20–30 minutes.

- Contract-writing (separated): 45–75 minutes.

- The ritual sequence (per participant: 15–25 minutes; for 6–8 men: 90–180 minutes).

- Contract-closure marking and celebration: 30–60 minutes.

• Post-protocol pastoral conversations: 7–14 days after, scheduled per participant.

• Supplementary individual TPM work and ongoing body prayer: continues in the weeks following.
