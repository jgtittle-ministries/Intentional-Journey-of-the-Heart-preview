# A24 Blessing Receiving Protocol (with the God-Split for Shame Removal)

## What this protocol does
The Blessing Receiving Protocol is a two-part Companion-led protocol that addresses shame at the depth where the participant's experience of God has become entangled with an internalized shaming voice. The first part surfaces and works the shame-loyalty dynamic, performs a God-split (places the shaming voice in the scene as a discrete entity, asks what is left when that voice is set aside, identifies the shaming voice as an idol that has been carried in God's place, deposes the idol), and restores the participant's capacity to hear what God actually says. The second part runs a blessing round in which another man takes the participant's role and the participant pronounces over him the blessing the Lord has been speaking that the shame had been blocking; the roles then reverse and the participant receives the blessing spoken back. The corporate-witness amplification (the body present and attending) lands the blessing in a multi-modal way that the participant carries forward.

The protocol's primary downstream effect is that the participant's image of God is healed at the point where the shame-loyalty dynamic had distorted it. The secondary downstream effects: the participant's capacity to receive divine love operationally increases; the participant's relationships with the actual parental figures involved are clarified (the parent is restored to image-bearing personhood once the parent's shaming voice has been distinguished from God's voice — V2.Exp6A + V2.Exp6B territory); the participant's downstream forgiveness work has different traction.

A24 is the corpus's first core protocol-level deployment of V2.Exp7A (Discernment of Voices) at the Lord's-voice-vs-internalized-shaming-voice level. The protocol is also the corpus's first core protocol for the positive-reception side of shame work — what fills the place the shame had occupied once the deposing has been done.

**When to use it**

• **When a participant's shame work has surfaced confusion between an internalized shaming voice and God's voice.** This is the core indicator. Signs: the participant cannot pray about the shame because every interior listening for God produces voice-content that matches the shaming figure's voice; the participant reports that *God* condemns him in language that closely tracks what a parent or other formative figure used to say; the participant's image of God has acquired the texture of a specific historical relationship rather than scriptural revelation.

• **When prior carpet-style work (A17 family) has reached the deepest shame layer but the shame is not clearing.** Sometimes A17 work reaches shame at depth and the standard close (forgiveness-of-other; gold-and-shadow) does not produce sufficient release because the shame has become structurally tied to the participant's image of God. A24 is the appropriate downstream protocol when this is the surfacing.

• **When the corpus's standard shame tools (gold-and-shadow Hot Seat; TPM truth-replacement) have not reached the deepest layer.** A24 addresses shame at a depth those tools do not typically reach. The Companion's discernment about whether to deploy A24 or to deploy the standard tools should test whether the participant's reported God-experience has acquired the shame-voice's texture (A24 territory) or whether the work is at a different shame layer (standard-tools territory).

• **When the participant is in a developmental moment where image-of-God reconfiguration is appropriate.** A24 is not for every shame moment; it is for the specific case where the participant is ready to receive a different image of God than the one the shaming voice has been supplying. Some participants are not yet at this moment; the Companion's pastoral discernment governs.

• **Within a body whose container is mature and whose corporate prayer life is operational.** A24 requires V2.Exp8 container conditions at full strength; the body's corporate posture during the work supplies the prayerful witness that the protocol operates within.

• **Level II Companion competency required** for facilitation. The Companion's discernment at the God-split moment, the framing-language for the blessing-round source-reversal, and the boundary-conditions for when not to deploy are all Level II competencies.

**When NOT to use it (failure modes and contraindications)**

• **With participants in severe trauma without prior containment work.** A24 surfaces material at depth; participants whose trauma has not been adequately contained may be destabilized by the surfacing. The pastoral pre-flight discernment: has this participant done sufficient containment work (A17 family or analogous) to hold what A24 will surface? If not, route to containment work first.

• **With participants in active psychiatric instability** (acute depressive episode; psychotic features; active eating-disorder episode; recent psychiatric hospitalization). A24's depth and the God-split's reconfiguration of the participant's image of God are not appropriate for participants in acute psychiatric crisis. Route to professional care; revisit A24 when stability has been restored.

• **With participants whose dissociative tendencies have not been worked.** A24 requires the participant to hold attention through the God-split and the blessing-round in an integrated way; dissociative participants may fragment under the protocol's demands. Companion's pre-flight discernment.

• **When the parent relationship is currently in active rupture or crisis.** A24's distinguishing of the parent's voice from God's voice produces clarity that may destabilize a current parent relationship before that relationship's own work is ready. Pastoral discernment: is the parent relationship at a moment that can hold the post-A24 clarity, or is A24 better deferred until the relationship's own work has progressed?

• **Without the Companion II competency for the God-split moment.** A Level I Companion who deploys A24 without the discernment to recognize the God-split moment, the framing-language discipline to perform the deposing-as-theological-not-psychological move, or the source-reversal discipline at the blessing-round, can produce a protocol experience that looks powerful but leaves the participant's image of God further confused. The competency requirement is gating, not optional.

• **Softening the deposing language into integration language.** The single most consequential failure mode for A24. The Companion frames the deposing of the idol-voice as integration of a wounded part-self (drawing on IFS framing or similar) rather than as deposing of an idol that has been carried in God's place. The Christian-frame anthropology is foundational to A24; softening the framing imports an alien anthropology and produces a different protocol than the one A24 names. The Companion's framing language is non-negotiable; if you cannot hold the deposing language with theological clarity, do not deploy A24.

• **Treating the blessing as self-generated rather than as the Lord's spoken word.** The second consequential failure mode. The Companion's framing at the blessing-round operationalizes the source-reversal: the blessing is the Lord's; the participant pronouncing it gives voice to it; the participant receiving it receives what the Lord has been speaking. A Companion who frames the blessing-round as self-affirmation (the participant constructing the blessing he needs and giving voice to it through a structured exercise) produces psychological affirmation rather than reception of the Lord's actual blessing. The protocol still works psychologically in this softened form; it does not do the theological work A24 is for.

• **Insufficient body container maturity.** The body's corporate posture during the protocol is operationally constitutive. A body whose container is shaky, whose members are distracted, whose corporate prayer life is not operational will not supply the witness-context the blessing-round requires for its full effect. Companion's pre-flight check: is the body actually present and ready?

• **Without explicit reciprocal de-roling per A14.** The role-played figures (parent, shaming voice) and the role-reversal in the blessing-round both require reciprocal de-roling. Skipping or perfunctorily performing the de-roling — common when the work has been intense and Companions are tired — leaves role-players holding figures or holding the participant's role. The de-roling discipline is non-negotiable.

## The protocol
The protocol is described as a single integrated session running 60–90 minutes including container forming and de-roling. A24 typically follows other carpet-style work in the same session (the carpet-style work surfaces the shame at depth; A24 addresses what surfaces); the description below assumes A24 is being run as the downstream protocol of an opening carpet-style sequence, with the participant already in the shame-dynamic territory.

1. **Container check and continuation.**

- Companion: confirm V2.Exp8 conditions throughout. If A24 is being run as continuation of prior carpet-style work, the container is already open and operational; if A24 is being run as a stand-alone session, confirm the container is at full strength.

- Discernment: A24 is not appropriate for a freshly-opened container; the participant needs to be in shame-territory through prior work that has surfaced it.

2. **The diagnostic question.**

- Companion: ask the operative question. *"You are at the shame. Where is God in this scene? What is God saying? Listen."*

- Participant: attend; respond. The response will typically be that God is present in the scene and is saying something that overlaps substantially with what the shaming figure (the parent / pastor / teacher) is saying or has said.

- Discernment: this is the diagnostic moment. If the participant's reported God-experience matches the shaming-voice content, you are in A24 territory. If the participant's reported God-experience is distinct from the shaming-voice (the participant can hear God as different from the shaming figure but feels distant from God; the participant has anger toward the shaming figure but no confusion about God) — you are not in A24 territory; the work routes elsewhere.

3. **The God-split.**

- Companion: name what has surfaced. *"The voice you are hearing as God's voice is the voice of [the parent / the pastor / the figure]. It has been speaking in God's place. We are going to set that voice aside in this scene as a discrete figure. Then I will ask what God actually says when that voice is not in the room."*

- Operational mechanics: deploy a role-player to carry the shaming voice as a discrete figure. Position the role-player at a clear physical distance from the participant. The role-player holds the voice; the participant turns away from it.

- Companion: ask. *"Now, with the shaming voice set aside, where is God in this scene? What does God actually say?"*

- Participant: attend; respond. The response now typically shifts substantially. What the participant encounters, with the shaming voice removed, is something they have not been able to reach — the Lord's love, welcome, presence without the condemnation that had been overlaying it. This is the moment.

- Discernment: hold the silence as the participant encounters what surfaces. Do not press for verbal articulation; the experience is itself the work. If the participant cannot reach anything when the shaming voice is set aside (no positive content, only emptiness), the work routes to extended pastoral conversation or to V2.Exp4 confession work — sometimes the participant has been so structurally tied to the shaming voice that the Lord's actual presence requires more upstream work to access. Do not press the God-split through this absence.

4. **The deposing of the idol.**

- Companion: name what has been surfaced theologically. *"What you have been hearing as God's voice is an idol — a voice that has been speaking in God's place that is not God. Scripture says, You shall have no other gods before me. The shaming voice has been carrying that idol-status in your interior life. We are going to depose it. The voice is named for what it is. It does not get the place it has occupied. The place belongs to the Lord, who has just shown himself to you in his actual voice."*

- Participant: receive the naming. May verbally agree; may simply hold the recognition.

- Companion: invite explicit deposing. *"Speak it. Address the idol-voice — name it for what it is, renounce it, send it from the place it has occupied. The Lord receives back what has belonged to him."*

- Participant: speak aloud the deposing. Specific words vary; the structure (name → renounce → send → receive) is core. Example wording the participant might produce: *"You are the voice of [my father / the message I took on / the shame I carried as God's word]. You are not God. You have been occupying God's place in my interior life. I renounce you in Jesus's name. I send you from the place that belongs to the Lord. Lord, receive back what is yours."*

- Discernment: the deposing is theological, not psychological. The Companion's framing throughout is in the language of idolatry / restoration, not in the language of integration / healing-the-wounded-part. If the framing slips toward integration language, gently re-cue.

5. **The blessing-round setup.**

- Companion: name the next move. *"What we are going to do next: the Lord has just shown himself to you in his actual voice. There is a blessing the Lord has been speaking over you that the shaming voice has been blocking. We are going to put a brother in your place. You are going to speak to your brother the blessing the Lord has been speaking to you — giving voice to it. Then we will reverse roles, and your brother will speak the blessing back to you, and you will receive it. The blessing is not what you are constructing; it is what the Lord is speaking. You are giving voice to it. He is receiving what is his."*

- Operational mechanics: select a brother from the body to take the participant's role. The chosen brother stands in the participant's place; the participant stands facing him.

- Discernment: choosing the right brother for the role matters operationally. The participant's own discernment is often the right gate; the Companion may ask *"who in this body should hold your place for this?"* The selection itself can be a moment of recognition.

6. **The blessing pronouncement.**

- Companion: invite. *"Speak what the Lord has been speaking to you. Address [brother's name] by your own name; speak the blessing that fills the place the shaming voice had been occupying."*

- Participant: speak. The blessing's specific content varies — sometimes a single sentence, sometimes a longer pronouncement. The Companion does not script the blessing's content; the participant is giving voice to what has surfaced from the Lord's actual presence in the work that has just been done.

- Discernment: if the participant constructs an obvious-inverse-of-the-shame (*"You are enough; you have not disappointed; you are loved"*), the Companion's discernment is whether this is what the Lord is actually speaking or whether it is the participant's psychological response to the shame. Sometimes the obvious-inverse is the actual word; sometimes the deeper word is more specific and unexpected. The Companion may ask, before the participant speaks, *"What is the Lord actually saying? Take a breath; let it surface."* The pause often produces the deeper word.

7. **The role-reversal.**

- Companion: mark the role-reversal explicitly. *"[Brother's name], step out of [participant's] place. [Participant's name], the roles reverse. You stand to receive. [Brother's name], you will speak back to [participant] the blessing he just spoke — giving voice to what the Lord has been speaking to him."*

- Operational mechanics: the brother who held the participant's place now stands facing the participant; the participant stands to receive.

- Brother: speak back the blessing — addressing the participant by name, with the same content the participant just spoke, with the brother's own voice and presence.

- Participant: receive. Eyes open or closed as appropriate. Hands open in posture of receiving.

- Discernment: the Companion holds the room. The body witnesses. The participant's reception may be silent or may produce visible response (tears; deep breath; visible release). Hold the silence after the brother's speaking; let the participant be with what has been received.

8. **Body witness amplification (optional).**

- Companion: if appropriate, invite the body to amplify. *"Brothers, if your hearts are amplifying what we have just witnessed, you may speak it aloud — addressing [participant] by name — or you may hold it silently in prayer."*

- Members: respond per their own discernment. Some may speak their own version of the blessing; some may hold silently.

- Discernment: this is optional and depends on the body's maturity and the moment. A more elaborate body-amplification can produce richer reception; a simpler form (one brother speaking; the body silently witnessing) is often sufficient.

9. **Test for residual shame/fear.**

- Companion: check. *"Where is the shame now? Where is the fear? Is anything left?"*

- Participant: report.

- Discernment: if substantial shame or fear remains, the protocol may need to loop back — sometimes a second God-split is needed because a different shaming voice has surfaced; sometimes the blessing-round needs to address a different facet of the blessing. The Companion's discernment about whether to re-run or to close.

10. **De-roling.**

- Companion: invite the de-roling. *"Before we close, we will de-role. [Role-player who held the shaming voice], [brother who held participant's place in the role-reversal], we release you from your roles."*

- Each role-player speaks per the A14 reciprocal formula. *"I am [own first name], not [the figure I held or the role I held]. [Participant's name], I release you from holding me as [figure / role]."*

- Participant responds: *"[Role-player's name], I release you from holding [figure / role]. You are [role-player's first name]."*

- Discernment: the blessing-round role-reversal de-roling is structurally important — the brother who held the participant's role must release the participant from having held *his role* during the receiving, and the participant must release the brother from having held *his role* during the pronouncement. Do not skip the double de-roling.

11. **Closing prayer and formally-marked session close.**

- Companion: lead a brief closing prayer naming what has happened. *"Lord, we thank you. You have shown yourself to [participant] in your actual voice. The idol has been deposed; the blessing has been received. May what has just been received remain in [participant] as he walks out into his life. In the name of Jesus, amen."*

- Close the session per V2.Exp8 refined formally-marked closing.

## Companion notes
• **The God-split framing is theological, not psychological.** This is the single most important point about facilitating A24. Practice the deposing language until it lands cleanly: *idol; carried in God's place; deposed; the Lord receives back what is his.* The language is not metaphor; it is theological description of what has been happening. Soft framing (*integrating the wounded part*; *healing the inner critic*) imports an alien anthropology and produces a different protocol.

• **The Companion's discernment at step 2 (the diagnostic question) gates the rest.** If the participant's reported God-experience does not match the shaming-voice content, A24 is not the protocol; route elsewhere. The diagnostic is binary — either the God-voice-shaming-voice confusion is operational, or it is not.

• **The participant's deposing language at step 4 may surface theological frames you did not expect.** Some participants will speak deliverance-tradition language; some will speak liturgical-tradition language; some will speak simple Lord-receive-back language. The Companion does not script the participant's deposing; the participant speaks what surfaces from the work. The structure (name → renounce → send → receive) is core; the specific words are the participant's.

• **The blessing at step 6 is sometimes simple, sometimes specific, sometimes unexpected.** A participant who constructs the obvious-inverse-of-the-shame as the blessing may be giving voice to what the Lord is actually saying; sometimes the deeper word is more specific and unpredictable. The Companion's pause-and-listen invitation often produces the deeper word.

• **The brother who holds the participant's place in the role-reversal carries operationally important work.** The selection matters; the brother should be capable of receiving the blessing-pronouncement on the participant's behalf and speaking it back with presence. Brothers who are themselves carrying unprocessed shame in the same territory may struggle with the role-holding; the Companion's selection-discernment considers this.

• **Watch for over-extension of the protocol.** A24 sometimes opens material that exceeds the session's scope — a second God-split layer; sin-channel material requiring confession; wound-track material requiring downstream A17 family work. The Companion's discernment: address what A24 is for in this session; route additional surfacing to subsequent protocols and subsequent sessions. Do not press A24 through material it is not designed to hold.

• **The downstream forgiveness work (V2.Exp6A + V2.Exp6B coupled pair) is its own session.** A24 produces operational possibility for forgiveness work that was not present before; do not collapse the forgiveness work into the A24 session. The parent restored to image-bearing personhood remains to be forgiven in a separate cycle that A24 makes possible.

• **For women's groups (FotH), the §5.1(c) gender-framing pastoral-pattern applies.** The protocol's structural moves transfer cleanly. The shame-loyalty dynamic with the parent is a substantively gender-neutral mechanism; the specific texture (the maternal-shaming voice as distinct from the paternal-shaming voice; the gendered shape of the blessing the participant has been unable to receive) may vary by group context. Companion judgment about specific framing language at the blessing-round step.

• **For the Companion's own preparation:** the protocol exposes the Companion to material that may activate the Companion's own shame-loyalty work. The discipline of having done one's own A24 work (or analogous work) before facilitating others' A24 work is operationally important. This is part of why Companion II competency is the gating requirement.

## Materials
• A room appropriate for sustained carpet-style work with sufficient space for role-players to stand at clear physical distances.

• A trained Level II Companion as facilitator.

• A body of at least four to six members for the corporate-witness amplification; smaller bodies can run A24 but the body-witness step is operationally diminished.

• No paper or writing materials required; the protocol is entirely verbal and embodied.

## Time
• 60–90 minutes for the A24 session including container, the protocol itself, de-roling, and closing.

• The protocol typically follows other carpet-style work in the same session (A17 family or analogous work that surfaced the shame at depth); if A24 is the standalone session, plan for the longer end of the range.
