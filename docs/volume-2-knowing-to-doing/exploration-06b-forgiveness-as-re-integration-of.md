# Sixth Exploration (B): Forgiveness as Re-integration of the Wronger

## The Form Forgiveness Takes
If Exploration 6A names the discipline of preserving the wronger's personhood through the anger work, this Exploration names the form forgiveness itself takes once the anger work has done its work. The two claims travel together as a coupled pair, and they prevent a particular failure mode the corpus has watched produce damage repeatedly across two decades of pastoral work: men who do beautiful, complete anger work in a session and walk out with their forgiveness work undone — still resolving their anger at *bad dad* while the actual father is somewhere else entirely, unforgiven and unaddressed.

The corrective form is the one scripture itself models. Matthew 18:21–35's unforgiving-servant parable addresses the whole person. Ephesians 4:32's *as God in Christ forgave you* invokes a divine pattern that forgives the integrated person — the one the gospel justifies is not split into wounding-self and loving-self for the purposes of the justification; the gospel addresses the whole. Luke 15:20's prodigal's father embraces the integrated returning son rather than the just-the-good-parts version. Luke 23:34's *Father, forgive them* — *them* is the actual persons, not an abstraction.

## What the Companion Has to Do
The Companion's discipline is therefore not just to use the therapeutic split for the anger work but to *put the person back together for **the forgiveness*. This is operationally specific: the closing of any work that has used the split includes a re-integration move where the wronger is named again as the whole person, the anger work's split-off figures are released, and the forgiveness work (whether in the same session or in a later cycle) operates on the integrated person.

The failure mode is not a moral defect; it is a sequencing failure that the corpus's anger-work protocols can produce when the closing move is omitted or rushed. Stating the coupled pair explicitly — 6A governs the anger-with-split, 6B governs the forgiveness-after-re-integration — gives the Companion a clean canonical reference for the close of any anger-work session. Forgiveness work that operates only on the split-off bad figure leaves the relationship unresolved and produces release-without-reconciliation, which is half the work.

**Proposed Law (Pastoral-Anthropological): Forgiveness in Christian formation is forgiveness of the integrated person. Therapeutic operations that split the ****wronger**** into wounding-behavior and loving-behavior figures are tools for accessing the anger honestly; they are not the form forgiveness itself takes. After the ****anger work**** has done its work, the forgiveness work re-integrates: the actual person, image-bearing and whole, is the one to be forgiven. Forgiveness work that operates only on the split-off bad figure leaves the relationship unresolved and produces release-without-reconciliation, which is half the work. The Companion's discipline is to use the therapeutic split as a tool for ****the anger**** and to put the person back together for ****the forgiveness****.**

**Certainty: Clearly ****Taught  ***The** basic claim is convergent across the four streams of the tradition. The specific formulation was tested in Council deliberation to confirm it does not foreclose on the Reformed-Evangelical unilateral-interior-act framing nor under-state the sacramental-tradition reconciliation **move**. **The pairing with Exploration 6A gives the coupled-pair structure governing all **wronger**-figure therapeutic work.
