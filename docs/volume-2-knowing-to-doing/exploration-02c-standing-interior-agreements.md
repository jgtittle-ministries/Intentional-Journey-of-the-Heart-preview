# Second Exploration (C): Standing Interior Agreements

## A Third Sibling to Wound-and-False-Self and the Adversary's Role
Explorations 2A and 2B named two layers beneath the knots of Exploration 2: the *wound-and-false-self* pattern, where the knot has hardened into an identity (2A); and the *adversary's role* in installing lies at moments of vulnerability (2B). This Exploration names a third sibling layer that the corpus has been operating on for decades, especially in the BoB and Fellowship of the Heart contexts, but that I have never given a formal home in this volume until now: **standing interior agreements** — binding contracts the participant entered (often as a child, often during specific trauma moments, often unconsciously) about who they are, what they are worth, what they are permitted to do or to be, and how they are loyal to the family system.

![A letter sealed in wax — the interior agreement is real, signed, and operative whether or not the signer remembers it.](images/06-wax-seal.jpg)

The agreement is *operationally distinct* from a knot. A knot is what the participant is *carrying* — a sustained energetic load on the heart, in the language of Exploration 2. An agreement is what the participant has *committed to* carrying — a binding interior contract that produces and re-produces knots across multiple blockage-types over years and decades. A single agreement may produce grief knots (committed to mourning incompletely because completion would feel like disloyalty), shame knots (committed to a verdict about the self as the price of belonging), fear knots (committed to a survival strategy out of which the fear cannot be released), anger knots (committed to a contract that forbids the anger from being named), and sin-channel behaviors (committed to a pattern that the agreement requires) — *all from the same underlying agreement*.

This has a sharp practical implication. **Addressing the downstream knots without naming the upstream agreement clears each knot individually without dissolving the binding that produces them.** The participant can do beautiful knot-clearing work on grief and shame and fear and anger and feel real release in each — and find six months later that the same patterns have re-formed because the standing agreement is still in force, still producing the same knots from upstream. The agreement-territory is upstream of the knot-territory; both must be addressed for full healing.

## The Operational Form: Renunciation Work
The corpus's canonical protocol for renouncing standing interior agreements is **A25 Breaking the Contracts**, which operates in Exploration 9's territory (Community as Amplifier) with **corporate prayer in Jesus's name as the operational form of the renunciation**. The protocol is treated at depth in Vol 5 Part VI. What matters at the canonical-claim altitude is that renunciation is the right form for this territory — naming the agreement, formally renouncing it in the name of Jesus, with the community as witness and amplifier — because an agreement, by its nature, is exited by a counter-act that has the same authority-structure as the original entering act. A contract entered in a child's vulnerable moment is exited by an adult renunciation in the authority of the Lord, in community, before witnesses. The form matches the territory.

Two boundary notes. **First**, the renunciation is of the *contract-message*, not of the parent or person who was its human conduit; the parent retains image-bearing dignity throughout (Exploration 6A). The work is the gospel's freedom to address the contract directly without dishonoring the conduit. **Second**, agreement-renunciation work is Companion II / III competency; it is not first-cohort beginner work. Vol 5 Part VI's A25 entry treats the gating in detail.
