# Sixth Exploration (A): The Image-Bearing Dignity of the Wronger

## The Operational Split and Its Limit
Several of the corpus's anger-work protocols deploy a *therapeutic split*: the wrongdoer the participant is working to forgive is provisionally divided into the wounding-behavior figure and the loving-behavior figure, so the participant can feel the anger toward the wounding figure honestly, without violating their loyalty to the actual person or denying the actual person's personhood. A12 Splitting a Role is the canonical instance; many of the Tell-Show-Release family protocols (A18–A20), and the BoB Healing and Forgiveness work (A17), use a version of the same move.

The split works because it solves a specific problem: most participants cannot direct full anger at someone they also love and to whom they are obligated. The unsplit anger turns inward and becomes shame, or sideways and becomes corrosive resentment, or never surfaces at all. The split gives the anger a target it can be directed at without the participant having to choose between feeling the anger and honoring the bond.

But the split has a sharp limit, which Exploration 6A names. **The split is operational, not ontological.** It is a tool for accessing the anger; it is not a description of who the wrongdoer actually is. The wrongdoer is, ontologically, one person — an image-bearer whose dignity the formation work must preserve throughout. The participant is not granted, by virtue of having been wounded, permission to undo the wrongdoer's personhood. The clearing work is *not* permission to dehumanize.

## What This Discipline Asks of the Companion
The Companion's discipline at the close of any work that has used the split is to bring the wrongdoer back together as the actual person. The downstream forgiveness work (Exploration 6B) re-integrates the wronger as the actual person to be forgiven, not as the split-off bad figure the anger work was directed at. Without this re-integration, the participant walks out of the session having done beautiful anger work and an entirely separate, half-completed forgiveness work — released from the anger toward the split-off figure but still entangled with the actual person on terms the work was supposed to address.

**Proposed Law (Pastoral-Anthropological): Every person, including the wrongdoer the participant is working to forgive, is an image-bearer whose dignity the formation work must preserve. The therapeutic move of distinguishing wounding behavior from loving behavior is operational, not ontological — it serves the participant's capacity to feel ****the anger**** honestly without violating loyalty or denying the wrongdoer's personhood. Downstream forgiveness work re-integrates the ****wronger**** as the actual person to be forgiven, not as the split-off bad figure the anger work was directed at.**

The image-of-God doctrine (Gen 1:26–27; Jas 3:9; Matt 5:44–45; Luke 15:20 — the prodigal's father embraces the integrated returning son) is among the most ecumenically settled, convergent across all four streams of the tradition we draw from. Possibly the cleanest single claim added in the Council Batch 1 acceptance (May 11, 2026): strongest epistemic class — directly stated in scripture plus operational pattern — and minimal Council deliberation. Pairs with Exploration 6B as the coupled pair governing all **wronger**-figure therapeutic work.*
