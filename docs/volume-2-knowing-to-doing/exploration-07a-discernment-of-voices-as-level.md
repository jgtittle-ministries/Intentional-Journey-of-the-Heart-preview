# Seventh Exploration (A): Discernment of Voices as Level II Companion Competency

## Why This Has To Be Named Before the Voice-Work Protocols
Exploration 7 named the hearing faculty as a skill that develops through specific practices, and opened with a doctrinal frame naming the canonical boundary inside which all the hearing work in this volume operates: the Spirit speaks today, but what the Spirit says is the application of canonical truth to the specific moment, never new canonical revelation. The discernment-of-voices work below operates inside that same boundary — the Spirit-conviction this discipline helps identify is the Spirit’s application of revealed truth to the participant in the moment, never new revelation that adds to or alters what scripture has already established. This Exploration names a discipline that has to be in place *before* any of the voice-work protocols (the shame-voice work in A8, the empty-chair work in A3, the figure-speech work in B11, the somatic-voice work in B14, the localized-trauma speech work in B15, the spiritual-quadrant discernment in B17, the steps 2 and 4 discernment in B18, the contract-naming work in A25) can be safely deployed. The discipline is the discernment of *what kind of voice* is operating in the moment of the work.

There are at minimum three distinct kinds of voice the Companion encounters in formation work, and each requires a different response. **First**, *Spirit-conviction* (John 16:8). The Spirit convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment. The response when this voice is operating is *obedience*, not protocol-based transformation. A Companion who treats a Spirit-conviction as a wound to be cleared has transformed what should be obeyed; the participant is left further from the obedience the Spirit was inviting them into. **Second**, *accusation* (Revelation 12:10). The accuser of the brethren accuses them day and night. The response when this voice is operating is *resistance*, on the basis of the standing identity in Christ (Romans 8:1). A Companion who treats an accusation as a Spirit-conviction calls the participant to confess what scripture has already declared in the negative; the participant is left negotiating on the accuser's terms. **Third**, an *internalized lie* or *remembered parental voice*. This is the territory the voice-work protocols actually address — the lie at the root (Exploration 3), the false-self verdict (Exploration 2A), the contract-message (Exploration 2C). The response when this voice is operating is therapeutic engagement through the relevant tool family.

The Companion's discernment of which kind of voice is operating is a Level II competency. Protocols deployed without this discernment produce real damage — they transform what should be obeyed, engage what should be resisted, or fail to address what was actually a lie.

## How This Sits With the Refined Container Discipline
This Exploration is paired with the witness-discipline (refined Exploration 8 paragraph 3 below): the Companion *discerns* the voice's kind and *witnesses* the participant's engagement with it. Both disciplines operate in the same Companion moment. Discernment without witness becomes a Companion-led interpretation that forecloses on the formation work; witness without discernment becomes presence without protection. The two together — discerning *which* voice is operating and witnessing *the participant's* engagement with it — are the canonical Companion-II competency for voice-engaging protocols.

**Proposed Law (Companion Competency): Formation work involving internal voices presupposes a prior discernment of the voice's kind. Spirit-conviction (John 16:8) is heeded; the response is obedience, not protocol-based transformation. Accusation (Revelation 12:10) is resisted; the response is the standing identity in Christ (Romans 8:1), not negotiation on the accuser's terms. Internalized ****lie**** or remembered parental ****voice is**** addressed therapeutically through the relevant tool family. The Companion's discernment of which kind of voice is operating is ****a Level**** II competency, and protocols deployed without this discernment produce real damage — they transform what should be obeyed, engage what should be resisted, or fail to address what was ****actually a**** lie.**

**Certainty: Clearly ****Taught  ***Directly** stated in scripture across four texts (1 John 4:1 for the testing discipline; John 16:8 for Spirit-conviction; Rev 12:10 for accusation; Rom 8:1 for the standing-in-Christ identity). Operational pattern across decades of practice. Convergent across all four streams of **the tradition**. The discernment step is non-optional at the front of any voice-engaging protocol — among the most operationally consequential moves in the discipline.*
